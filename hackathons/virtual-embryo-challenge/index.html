NeurIPS 2026 Virtual Embryo Challenge · Aug – Dec 2026 · solo (team Second Heart Field, agent track)

Virtual Embryo Challenge data notes

Two public data notes. E10.5 cells were sequenced about 40% shallower than E9.5 cells, and a model that continues the change between them carries that forward as if it were development. A scoring protocol reproduces 26 of 29 of my validation-board records to four decimal places.

Code ↗

The Virtual Embryo Challenge at NeurIPS 2026 is run by the Qiu Lab at Stanford, on mouse embryo data from Dr Qingquan Zhang and Dr Neil Chi at UCSD. Teams submit predictions for several tasks, and public boards score each submission. I am taking part alone, in the agent track, as team Second Heart Field. The challenge is still in progress. This page covers two public data notes; I did the analysis for both.

Note 1: sequencing depth is a stage covariate

The question. Whether part of the measured change between two stages comes from a difference in sequencing depth.

What the data show.

  • Cells at E10.5 are about 40% shallower than at E9.5. The median count of RNA molecules per cell (UMI) is 6,723 at E10.5 against 11,080 at E9.5 and 10,857 at E8.5. Total UMI per stage is roughly constant, and E10.5 has about 1.5 times the cells.
  • The drop is within cell type. Of the 12 cell-type labels with at least 50 cells at both stages, 10 sit at 0.49 to 0.70 of their E9.5 depth. So it is a property of the library, not of a changing mix of cells.
  • The measured change from E9.5 to E10.5 carries a depth signature. Between two stages of equal depth (E8.5 to E9.5), 55 genes change in pseudobulk expression by 0.25 or more. Between E9.5 and E10.5, 462 do, and 390 of them go down. Thinning E9.5 to E10.5 depth before measuring removes 148 of the 462.

Why it matters. A model that fits the change between two released stages and continues it carries the depth difference forward as if it were development. On the public Task 1 test board, a submission of mine that continued the E9.5 to E10.5 change scored 68.56. E10.5 cells submitted unchanged score about 69.4.

What did not work. Correcting the output afterwards. On the Task 1 validation board my best file scored 57.65. I submitted five corrections of it, and all five scored lower. Four gene-wise rescalings scored 55.13 to 56.36. A frozen-gene replacement with renormalisation scored 56.20, although it had met every local target. Undoing the thinning for the 6,000 most abundant genes lost 6.8 points.

What worked. Removing depth before the fit: thin the earlier stage to the median depth of the donor stage, then fit the step. On a local replica with the same depth structure (5 seeds), the plain continuation scored 63.3 ± 0.6, untouched donor cells 64.2 ± 0.8, and the depth-neutral fit 66.8 ± 0.7. This is a local replica, not a board score.

The note, a notebook that reproduces it from the released files in about a minute, and a short reference implementation are in the repository.

Note 2: how close is a local score to the board

The question. Many community tools run the organisers’ scorer locally. None of them could say how close the number they print is to the number the board returns.

What I built. A calibration set of 29 prediction files, each pinned by its hash. For each file it gives the board score and the score from a local replica of the scorer (5 seeds), across all five validation boards. The protocol that goes with it reproduces 26 of the 29 board records to four decimal places.

What it shows.

  • On the Task 1 validation board the local replica is unbiased and keeps the order of submissions: local minus board is −0.13 ± 0.34 over 13 files.
  • On Task 3 it reads about 1.1 points high (+1.07 ± 0.27 over 8 files).
  • The spread across seeds has a median standard deviation of 0.74, and up to 1.8 on Task 3. A difference of half a point between two submissions is therefore not evidence.

What is still open. Three Task 2 records do not reproduce under any fixed weighting. The note asks other teams to add their own records.

The set holds only hashes, scores and counts, not challenge data. The files and the protocol are in the repository.
