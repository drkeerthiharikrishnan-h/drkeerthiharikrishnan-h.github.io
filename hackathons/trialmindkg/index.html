NVIDIA-AWS BioAgentKG Challenge · 6 Oct – 17 Nov 2026 · solo

TrialMindKG

An agent that enriches, prunes and explains the Pharos drug-target graph, with 34 failed drug programmes as worked cases. Every number comes from a committed result file, including the test that failed.

Code ↗White paper ↗Live demo ↗Video ↗

The challenge asked whether an agent can run one loop over a large biomedical knowledge graph: ingest, filter, clean, limit and maintain. The graph was Pharos, the drug-target graph from the NCATS Illuminating the Druggable Genome programme. I built TrialMindKG, which adds evidence to Pharos, gives every edge a keep-or-prune verdict with its reasons, and answers plain-language questions with the evidence behind each answer.

Submitted 17 November 2026. Judging runs to 8 December; results are due on 15 December.

The question

Graphs for drug discovery are merged from many sources. Each has its own identifiers, its own idea of evidence and its own errors. Many links are weak, some are wrong, and a user cannot easily see which is which. I set three tests: whether enrichment makes the graph more useful on held-out data; whether pruning removes edges that are less likely to be true; and whether a non-specialist can get a sourced answer in a few minutes.

What I built

  • The graph. The core is Pharos 3.19.2. Around it I added Open Targets 26.09 gene-disease associations, ChEMBL 37 compound-target activities, and the MONDO, Gene Ontology and Human Phenotype Ontology hierarchies. All classes and predicates are BioLink terms. Every edge carries its source, release and evidence type.
  • The trial layer. 34 failed drug programmes, chosen by hand as case studies, with 3,220 nodes and 5,220 edges from ClinicalTrials.gov, openFDA, GTEx and the literature. It is not a registry of all trials.
  • The pruner. It scored all 26,462,802 Pharos edges and marked 256,639 (0.97%) for pruning. Pruned edges stay in the graph, marked, so nothing is lost.
  • The question box. A language model drafts a graph query; a validator checks it against the graph before it runs. Each answer shows the query, a short summary and the evidence rows.

It was a solo entry. Coding agents wrote the programs, ran the experiments and logged each hypothesis and rule in a dated ledger before the run. I decided the open questions: which classes to keep, which mappings to exclude and which controls to run.

Results

Link prediction. A path-aware graph model recovered held-out gene-disease links better on the enriched graph than on Pharos alone. Mean AUROC rose from 0.6614 to 0.7849, a gain of +0.1235 (95% interval +0.1167 to +0.1304), higher in 5 of 5 folds. On the enriched and pruned graph the gain was +0.1219. AUROC is a ranking score on a benchmark, not biological accuracy.

Pruning. In a graph-embedding model (RotatE), pruning beat removing the same number of edges at random by +0.0073 (+0.0022 to +0.0124; 5 of 5 folds). The effect is small. Open Targets, which the pruner never sees, listed 17.9% of kept gene-disease edges that are not text mining, but only 0.7% of pruned ones (280 pruned edges, so this covers few of them).

Reports on the 34 programmes. A model judge checked each claim against the graph evidence for the question. Supported: TrialMindKG 0.971 (273 claims), the same model without the graph 0.588, and with the programme documents but no graph 0.617. The judge’s reference is the graph’s own evidence, which favours the graph. So this measures agreement with that evidence, not biological truth.

What broke

  • The first benchmark found nothing. A plain graph network, run under the frozen protocol on an earlier build of the graphs, gained −0.0045 (0 of 5 folds higher). It cannot count the paths between a gene and a disease. The path-aware model was chosen after this null result.
  • Most of the gain is the disease hierarchy. About 75% of the headline model’s gain comes from MONDO: a gene linked to a parent or child of a disease gets predicted for that disease. Without MONDO the gain is +0.0329.
  • The blinded pruning check failed. The rule, fixed before rating, needed at least 60 of 100 pruned edges and at most 15 of 100 kept edges judged unsupported. Pruned: 71. Kept: 56. Most kept-but-unsupported rows were STRING protein interactions or text co-occurrence links.
  • The machine raters were not confirmed. On 39 of the rows I rated blind, I agreed with the raters’ majority label on 7.

So enrichment gives the large gain, and pruning keeps it and beats random removal slightly. Kept does not mean confirmed.

The evidence behind an answer

Every edge opens to a record like this: the statement, the source record, the evidence type, when it was retrieved and the knowledge source. Stored example answers return in 3.50 to 3.92 seconds; a typed question took 50.56 seconds (median), because a language model drafts and checks the query. No usability test with outside users was run.
