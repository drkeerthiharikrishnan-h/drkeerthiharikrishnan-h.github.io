    • CAR-T: each lot made from one patient's cells

    Imatinib, semaglutide, pembrolizumab, trastuzumab deruxtecan, tisagenlecleucel, onasemnogene abeparvovec, exagamglogene autotemcel, nusinersen and Comirnaty cover the nine modalities.     A good record passes five tests a stranger can check

    • Identity, unit, version
    • Provenance: who, when, which instrument and protocol
    • Context: conditions such as ATP concentration

    Source: Scientific Data (Wilkinson et al.), 2016; EMBL-EBI ChEMBL; US National Institute of Standards and Technology

    Speaker notes

    FAIR asks that machines as well as people can find and reuse data. Nano is 10 to the minus 9 and micro 10 to the minus 6, a factor of 1,000. Ask: which of the five does your current schema not store?

    A pIC50 check catches a unit error that looks plausible

    • pIC50 = 9 minus log10 of IC50 in nM
    • 0.48 typed as nM gives 9.32, stored 6.32
    • The gap of 3.00 is the factor of 1,000

    Source: EMBL-EBI ChEMBL

    Speaker notes

    The value 0.48 µM is 480 nM, and 9 - log10(480) = 6.32 [D:sm-potency]. A pIC50 typed into the IC50 column passes a range check, so cross-column checks matter. Ask: which other column pairs in your data must agree?

    A subject id that differs between datasets breaks joins silently

    • USUBJID must be identical in every dataset
    • Dates in SDTM use ISO 8601
    • AESER takes Y or N, nothing else

    Source: US FDA (CDER, CBER), 2026; PHUSE, 2022

    Speaker notes

    An improper USUBJID is a common error that often requires resubmission. In the messy extract, one subject's events drop from 2 to 1, and the table's serious events from 1 to 0 [D:clin-sdtm-ae]. Ask: would your pipeline raise an error here, or return a smaller count?

    Part 11 turns provenance into code requirements

    • Computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails
    • Changes must not obscure earlier entries
    • Annex 11: validated application, qualified infrastructure

    Source: US Office of the Federal Register (eCFR); European Commission, 2011

    Speaker notes

    21 CFR 11.10(e) also requires the audit trail to be kept as long as the record. Annex 11 is softer: firms should consider an audit trail based on a risk assessment. MHRA guidance says shared logins should not be used for GxP data, and sums up good records as ALCOA+, nine attributes. EMA's ALCOA++ adds a tenth, traceable. Ask: where in your stack could a value be overwritten in place?

    An out-of-specification result is kept, never cleaned away

    • Compute the flag from result and limit
    • 0.62% water against 0.5% is out of specification
    • A retest is a new row, linked to the first

    Source: US FDA, 2022; US Office of the Federal Register (eCFR)

    Speaker notes

    0.62 - 0.5 = 0.12 points, 24% above the limit [D:cmc-batch]. The good table keeps assay run 1 (93.8, flagged Y) and the retest (101.0) as two rows under INV-2024-0012 [D:cmc-batch]. Any result outside its acceptance criterion triggers a documented investigation. Ask: which of your fields are typed by a person when they could be computed?

    What you will be able to do

    • Trace a medicine from discovery to real-world use and name the system of record, and the identifier, that each stage writes.
    • Test a record for the five properties of good data (identity, unit, version, provenance, context) and turn each test into a check in code.
    • Say which facts you can fetch from a public API by a stable identifier and which exist only inside the company, and show a locator for every value your tool returns.

    Learn

    How a medicine reaches a patient, and what each stage writes

    A medicine passes through nine stages, and each one writes records into a different system. Discovery screens thousands of candidate compounds; few warrant further study[1]. A large project can screen 200,000 to over 1,000,000 compounds and narrow hundreds of leads to one or two candidates[2]. Preclinical work tests toxicity before any human dose, under good laboratory practice (GLP) rules in 21 CFR Part 58[3].

    The sponsor (the company running the trials) then files an IND, the US filing that lets it start human trials[4]. Phase I tests safety in a small group, Phase II tests whether the drug works and at what dose, and Phase III confirms benefit in a large group[4]. The marketing application is sent as eCTD (electronic Common Technical Document), the format FDA requires for NDAs, BLAs and commercial INDs[5]. Once an application is filed, FDA’s review team has 6 to 10 months to reach a decision[6]. After approval, the plant makes the medicine under current good manufacturing practice (CGMP) rules in 21 CFR Parts 210, 211, 212 and 600[7]. Pharmacovigilance (the science of detecting and preventing adverse effects) keeps watching for safety problems for as long as the medicine is sold[8, 9]. Real-world data from health records, claims and registries then shows how it performs in routine care[10].

    Most programmes stop on the way. In 9,704 programmes tracked from 2011 to 2020, 52.0% passed Phase I, 28.9% passed Phase II and 57.8% passed Phase III; overall, 7.9% went from Phase I to approval[11]. For a developer this has two consequences. Datasets of past programmes have few positive examples. And most of the records a company holds describe compounds and trials that never became medicines, so they must stay findable without a product name[11].

    Nine modalities, and the identifiers and units each one adds

    A modality is the kind of medicine: a chemical, a protein, a cell or a gene. Labels give the class first, then the mechanism. Each modality is made and measured differently, so each adds its own identifiers and units to the data model.

    Five properties of good data, and the rule that enforces each

    A record is good when a stranger can reuse it without phoning the person who made it. FAIR (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable) states this as a goal for machines as well as people[40]. Five tests cover most failures.

    Identity: the record carries a stable id that never changes meaning, such as a UniProt accession or USUBJID, the subject id that must be identical in every dataset of a submission[17, 41]. Unit: every number has one. Nano is 10 to the minus 9 and micro is 10 to the minus 6, so a µM value typed into an nM column is wrong by a factor of 1,000[42]. Version: the assay protocol, analytical method or dictionary version is stored with the result[43, 44]. Provenance: who made the record, when, on which instrument and under which protocol. Context: the conditions that change the number, such as ATP concentration in a kinase assay or the species of plasma[45].

    Regulators write provenance into law. In the US, 21 CFR 11.10(e) requires secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails, and changes must not obscure what was recorded before[46]. The EU’s Annex 11 asks for validated applications and qualified IT infrastructure for GMP systems[47]. It is softer on audit trails: firms should consider building one in, based on a risk assessment[47]. Both read as code requirements: append-only change history, time stamps from a controlled system clock[46] and individual logins, never shared ones[48]. The MHRA’s summary is ALCOA+: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available[48]. EMA’s clinical trial guideline uses ALCOA++, which adds a tenth attribute, traceable[29].

    Public safety data has limits. FDA does not receive a report for every event, so FAERS cannot give incidence rates[64]. openFDA’s API responses tell you to assume its results are unvalidated[62].

    Only the company holds the compound registry, the ELN, the LIMS, executed batch records and the EDC[27, 32, 29]. The useful work joins the two: an internal compound to ChEMBL by InChIKey, an internal target to UniProt by accession[13, 17].

    A worked pattern shows the order. The registry row for CPD-0001 stores its full InChIKey. Your code builds a PubChem URL from the prolog, the compound domain, the inchikey namespace and a property operation, and gets back a CID[49]. The same InChIKey finds the ChEMBL molecule, whose id then addresses its published activities[50]. Keep the URL you called and the date beside the result. The internal value and the public value stay in separate columns with separate provenance; they are never averaged into one number[45].

    What you do with it on day one

    Your first build is often retrieval over ELN, LIMS or registry records. Language models struggle to give provenance for what they say; retrieval from an index was proposed partly to fix that[65]. So every value your interface shows carries its locator: record id, version and system[30]. Your tests are evidence too. A regulated system needs documented validation, and each requirement needs a test kept current as requirements change[46, 29].

    A careful team never does four things. It never joins on drug or gene names[56]. It never overwrites a result in place[46]. It never strips a unit to make a column numeric[42]. And it never treats a vendor’s validation pack as proof for its own configuration[48].

    Example data

    Start with the potency table from a discovery assay database. Look at the ic50_nm and pic50 columns together: pIC50 is minus log10 of the molar IC50, so the two must agree[45]. In the messy version, one row holds a pIC50 in the IC50 column and one holds a µM value; only a cross-column check catches both. Look also at replicate_id: the good table stores CPD-0005 twice, as replicates r1 and r2[data].

    Next, an SDTM adverse event extract, the format in which trial data reaches the FDA[66]. Look at USUBJID, which must match across all datasets, and at AESTDTC, which must be ISO 8601[41, 67]. Each defect breaks a join or a count without raising an error.

    Last, batch release results from the LIMS. Look at whether each result has a method version, a unit and a limit, and whether the out-of-specification flag agrees with the numbers[43, 68]. The messy last row shows what an overwrite looks like when no audit trail stops it. The good table keeps the failing first run and the retest as two rows under one investigation id[data].

    Try it

    1. You are writing a load check for the potency table. The rule: pIC50 must equal 9 minus log10 of the IC50 in nM, within 0.05. Apply it to messy row 2 (CPD-0002)[data].
      AnswerpIC50 is minus log10 of the molar IC50[45]. With IC50 in nM, that is 9 minus log10(IC50 in nM). For 0.48: 9 - log10(0.48) = 9 + 0.32 = 9.32. The stored pIC50 is 6.32[data]. The gap is 9.32 - 6.32 = 3.00, which is log10 of 1,000: the micro-to-nano factor[42]. The row fails. The fix is 0.48 µM = 480 nM, and 9 - log10(480) = 6.32, which agrees. The check cannot test messy row 1, because its pIC50 is empty; make an empty pIC50 on an IC50 row a failure too.
    2. Messy rows 5 and 6 hold CPD-0005 twice (55 and 61 nM); row 6 has no replicate id[data]. You join the table to a compound registry with one row per compound. How many rows come out for five compounds, and what single value should you report?
      AnswerSix rows for five compounds, because CPD-0005 matches twice[data]. Without a replicate id on row 6, nobody can tell a second measurement from a duplicate load. Any count or average downstream now weights CPD-0005 double. Report one summary value: mean pIC50 = (7.2596 + 7.2147) / 2 = 7.237, which is 10 to the power (9 - 7.237) = 57.9 nM, the geometric mean of 55 and 61 nM[data][45]. Use the unrounded pIC50 values; the two-decimal values in the table give 58.2 nM. Store both replicates, as the good table does with r1 and r2, and report n = 2; do not drop one.
    3. In the messy AE extract, count adverse events for subject STUDY-001-101-0001, and count serious events (AESER = Y) in the whole table[data]. Compare with the good table.
      AnswerThe good table has 2 events for STUDY-001-101-0001 and 1 serious event in the whole table[data]. The messy table gives 1 event, because row 1 has USUBJID 101-0001 and does not match; USUBJID must be identical across every dataset[41]. It gives 0 serious events, because row 4 says “yes” and AESER uses Y or N[67]. So the subject’s events are undercounted by 1 in 2 (50%) and serious events by 1 in 1 (100%). Neither query raises an error.
    4. Messy row 3 has AESTDTC 02/04/2024[data]. A parser could read it as 2 April or as 4 February. How far apart are the two readings, and what should the loader do?
      AnswerFrom 4 February 2024 to 2 April 2024 is 25 + 31 + 2 = 58 days (2024 is a leap year, so February has 29 days, and 29 - 4 = 25). The good table says 2024-04-02[data]. SDTM dates use ISO 8601, YYYY-MM-DD, with partial dates such as 2024-04 allowed[67]. The loader should reject the value and send it back to the source, not guess a format.
    5. In the messy batch table, LOT-2024-008 shows water 0.62 % w/w against “not more than 0.5”, flagged N, and an assay cell reading “101.0 (retest, first run 93.8)”[data]. Write the two rules your code should enforce.
      AnswerRule 1: compute the flag from result and limit; never trust a typed flag. 0.62 - 0.5 = 0.12 points over, or 0.12 / 0.5 = 24% above the limit, so the flag is Y[data]. A result outside its acceptance criterion is out of specification and starts a documented investigation[68]. Rule 2: one result per row, append only. The first run, 93.8, is 95.0 - 93.8 = 1.2 points below the lower limit[data]. A change must not obscure what was recorded before, and the audit trail must record it[46]. So the 93.8 is kept as its own row; the retest is a second row linked to it. The good table does exactly this: run 1 at 93.8 flagged Y, and the retest at 101.0, both under investigation INV-2024-0012[data].
    6. A scientist asks your retrieval tool: “What is the IC50 of CPD-0002 in the kinase assay?” Using the good potency table, write the answer as the interface should show it, and one test that proves it[data].
      AnswerAnswer: “480 nM (pIC50 6.32), IC50, compound CPD-0002, batch B01, replicate r1, assay ASY-KIN-07 version v3, at 10 µM ATP”[data]. The batch, assay version and ATP concentration are the context that make the number comparable[45]. The locator lets a reviewer open the record[30]. Test: given a frozen index version, the query returns this exact row as the top source and the displayed value equals the stored value with its unit. Keep the test result, the index version and the software version together as evidence[29].

    Check yourself

    3. Which requirement comes from 21 CFR 11.10(e)? a) Records must be printed weekly b) Audit trails must be computer-generated and time-stamped, and changes must not obscure earlier entries c) Every user shares one admin login d) Results are rounded before storage
