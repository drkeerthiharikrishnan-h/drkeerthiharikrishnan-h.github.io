Which level fits you?

Three quick questions. Answer honestly; nothing is recorded.

  1. Can you name the main stages a medicine goes through before approval?
  2. Do you know which systems and data formats each department works with?
  3. Have you built, delivered or assessed an AI system on regulated life-science data?

Biopharma for developers and engineers

Map · Machine · Frontier

Most drugs fail in trials, so trial data is what the industry guards

  • Of 100 drugs that start Phase I, about 8 are approved
  • Phase II is the biggest drop: 28.9% advance
  • About 10.5 years from Phase I to approval

Source: BIO, Informa Pharma Intelligence and QLS Advisors, 2021

Speaker notes

These numbers come from 9,704 programmes tracked from 2011 to 2020. Lack of efficacy explains 40-50% of clinical failures and unmanageable toxicity about 30%. So the data that shows a drug works and is safe is the asset. Ask the room: what would a lost week of trial data cost?

Your system's output lands in one CTD module, which tells you who reads it

  • Module 1 is regional; Modules 2 to 5 are common
  • Module 3 holds quality data (how the drug is made and controlled); Module 5, clinical study reports
  • eCTD is required for NDAs and BLAs

Source: US FDA, 2017; US FDA, 2026

Speaker notes

The FDA splits the file by discipline: medical officers and statisticians read clinical data, pharmacologists read animal data. The review team has 6 to 10 months once the file is complete. Ask the client which module the system feeds, then design for that reader.

Each stage runs its own systems, so first find where yours sits

  • Lab: LIMS and electronic lab notebooks
  • Trials: EDC, eCOA, IRT, CTMS, eTMF
  • Manufacturing: MES at ISA-95 Level 3, ERP at Level 4

Source: Raviteja and Gupta, JSS College of Pharmacy, 2013; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; ISA

Speaker notes

LIMS is a laboratory information management system; EDC is electronic data collection. IRT handles randomisation and supplies; eTMF is the electronic trial master file. Ask the client: which system is the record of truth, and which only copies it?

Trial data follows a fixed path, so map the formats before you code

  • CDASH: how data is collected
  • SDTM: how it is organised; required by FDA and PMDA
  • ADaM: analysis datasets, traceable back to SDTM

Source: CDISC, 2023; CDISC, 2024; CDISC, 2021

Speaker notes

CDISC is the standards body for clinical data; PMDA is Japan's medicines regulator. Results end up in a clinical study report in Module 5. Each arrow is a transformation that someone must be able to trace. Ask: who owns the mapping between each pair?

Safety data has a fixed format and fixed dictionaries, so build to them

  • Each suspected side effect is an individual case safety report
  • EU requires the ICH E2B(R3) format since 30 June 2022
  • Events coded in MedDRA, medicines in WHODrug

Source: European Medicines Agency, 2020; MedDRA MSSO (ICH), 2026; Uppsala Monitoring Centre; European Medicines Agency

Speaker notes

MedDRA has five levels, with 27 System Organ Classes at the top. Health records use other codes, such as SNOMED CT, LOINC and RxNorm, exchanged as HL7 FHIR resources. Expect to map between vocabularies. Ask: how will you keep the coding consistent?

Regulated records keep every change, so never overwrite in place

  • Part 11 covers US electronic records and signatures
  • Audit trail: each GMP-relevant change, with its reason
  • ALCOA++: attributable, contemporaneous, original, traceable data

Source: US FDA, 2003; European Commission, 2011; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023

Speaker notes

Annex 11 says audit trails must be readable and reviewed regularly. ALCOA++ comes from EMA's guideline for clinical trial systems. Each word is a design choice: attributable means every record carries who or what made it. Ask: if an inspector picks one value, can you show who changed it, when and why?

Validated systems change only after testing, so plan releases around it

  • GAMP 5: testing effort matches the risk to patients and data
  • EU: GMP computer systems validated, IT infrastructure qualified
  • A validated system cannot simply take an untested change

Source: ISPE, 2022; European Commission, 2011

Speaker notes

GAMP 5 (second edition, 2022) is published by ISPE, the pharmaceutical engineering society. The FDA's computer software assurance guidance, written for medical devices, applies the same idea: more testing where risk is higher. GxP is shorthand for the good-practice rules: GLP, GCP, GMP. Ask: how long did the client's last validated release take?

In production, Merck drafts study reports under medical-writer oversight

  • Merck & Co.: drafts clinical study reports
  • Signal only: a Bayer study, one team of 3
  • FDA: Elsa helps staff read, write and summarise

Source: Merck & Co., 2025; Benaiche et al. (Bayer Healthcare SAS France), 2025; US FDA, 2025

Speaker notes

Merck's platform is designed to operate with oversight by qualified medical writers; first drafts take 3 to 4 days, not 2 to 3 weeks. The Bayer study (a signal, not yet production) ran about four months. More than 70% of FDA staff used Elsa voluntarily, per FDA internal data. Novo Nordisk drafts reports from expert-approved text; its figures are vendor-published.

Biopharma on one page, for developers and engineers

The chain, ten terms, five questions, cases.

The chain in one line

Discovery → preclinical research → clinical research → FDA review → post-market safety monitoring[1].

Ten terms

  • IND: the filing that lets a sponsor start trials in people[2].
  • GCP (ICH E6): the standard for running trials so participants are protected and data can be trusted[3].
  • CTD / eCTD: the five-module layout of a marketing application, sent electronically[4, 5].
  • SDTM: the CDISC standard shape of clinical trial data, required by the FDA and Japan’s regulator, PMDA[6].
  • Part 11: US rule for electronic records and signatures under FDA rules[7].
  • Annex 11: EU rule that computer systems used in manufacturing (GMP, good manufacturing practice) are validated[8].
  • Audit trail: a system record of every relevant change, with the reason[8].
  • ALCOA++: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available, traceable[9].
  • ICSR: one report per patient case of suspected side effects, in ICH E2B(R3) format in the EU[10].
  • MedDRA: the international terminology used to code adverse events[11].

Five questions to ask

  1. Is the system or its data covered by good-practice (GxP) rules, and which applies: Part 11 or Annex 11?
  2. Who validates it, and how are changes tested and approved?
  3. Where does the audit trail live, and who reviews it?
  4. Which data standard goes in and out: CDISC for trials, E2B for safety reports, FHIR for health records?
  5. If AI is involved, who reviews its output and where is that recorded?

Cases and one signal

  • Merck & Co. drafts clinical study reports with generative AI, with oversight by qualified medical writers[12].
  • Signal, not yet shown in production: Bayer. A peer-reviewed study measured a 23.3% time saving when one French drug-safety (pharmacovigilance) team of 3 drafted case documentation letters with its internal AI chat tool[13].
  • US FDA staff use Elsa, an internal generative AI tool. More than 70% of staff had used it, per FDA internal data in a December 2025 release[14].
