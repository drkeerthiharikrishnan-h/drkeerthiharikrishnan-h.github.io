    Data and AI engineers: Evaluate, validate, decide

    Banks, keys, gates, judges and the evidence file behind a fit-for-use decision

    You own one decision: is this system fit for a stated context of use

    • FDA draft: seven steps, from question to adequacy
    • Model risk combines model influence and decision consequence
    • Effort and documentation scale with risk

    Source: US FDA, 2025; EMA / ICH, 2025

    Speaker notes

    The FDA guidance is a January 2025 draft, not for implementation, and puts drug discovery out of scope. The EMA reflection paper covers every lifecycle stage, discovery included. In January 2026 EMA and FDA added ten joint principles of good AI practice in drug development, including a clear context of use and documented data governance. Ask the room: write your system's context of use in three sentences; who checks its output before use?

    Layer tags turn a score into a diagnosis

    • RAG pairs a model's own memory with a retrieved index
    • P fails: the model. S fails: retrieval. S+I fails: the join
    • Public value presented as internal: false attribution

    Source: Lewis et al. (arXiv, 2020; NeurIPS 2020), 2020

    Speaker notes

    RAG combines a model's parametric memory with an index of documents reached by a retriever. The layer tags and the bank design that follows are this guide's evaluation practice, not a published standard. Ask: for your last failed answer, which layer should have supplied it?

    Choose embeddings and rerankers on your own bank, not a leaderboard

    • No embedding method dominates all MTEB tasks
    • BM25 stays a robust baseline in BEIR
    • Cross-encoder rerankers cost orders of magnitude more compute

    Source: Muennighoff et al. (arXiv, 2022; EACL 2023), 2022; Thakur et al. (arXiv, 2021; NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks), 2021; Khattab and Zaharia (arXiv; SIGIR 2020), 2020

    Speaker notes

    MTEB covers 8 tasks and 58 datasets. A BERT reranker gave a 27% relative gain in MRR@10 on MS MARCO at the time. Ask: has anyone run the reranker on and off against our S-layer items?

    An item that still holds after a subject swap is a control, not a discriminator

    • Swap the subject; if the key holds, it is a control
    • Score dimensions separately; weights move with the question type
    • Difficulty is the length of the reasoning chain

    Source: Es et al. (arXiv, 2023; EACL 2024 demo), 2023

    Speaker notes

    CT-LOT-2024-010 shows 48% lysis at an effector-to-target ratio of 1:1 while the other lots were tested at 5:1, so a good item asks whether they compare [D:cart-release]. RAG evaluation scores retrieval and faithful use of passages as separate dimensions. Swap tests, weights and difficulty rules are this guide's practice. Ask: pick one item from your bank; is it a discriminator or a control?

    Multi-source items name their join key

    • At least 3 in 10 items join two sources (house target)
    • Keys: InChIKey, HGNC id plus UniProt, NCT number, OMOP standard concept
    • OMOP ids above 2,000,000,000 are local and never standard

    Source: OHDSI CDM Working Group; OHDSI CDM Working Group

    Speaker notes

    The intermediate module explains each key. In OMOP, ids up to 2,000,000,000 are reserved for the Standardized Vocabularies; local concepts sit above that and belong only in source-concept fields. The worked bank has four multi-source items in twelve, each with a named key [D:sm-potency,sm-dmpk,cart-release,rwe-omop]. Ask: where does our compound-to-InChIKey mapping live, and who owns it?

    Negative controls test whether the system knows where its evidence stops

    • Absent record, substitution bait, near-miss
    • SQuAD 2.0: one system fell from 86% to 66% F1
    • The only correct answer is "no record found"

    Source: Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, 2018

    Speaker notes

    SQuAD 2.0 added over 50,000 unanswerable questions written to look answerable. This guide's working target is at least one negative control in ten items. Ask: what would substitution bait look like for our trial codes?

    Every key carries a ground-truth state and a locator

    • EXACT, DERIVED, SOURCE-DEPENDENT, ABSENT
    • Average replicate potencies in log space; store n
    • Never an identifier nobody read in the source

    Source: EMBL-EBI ChEMBL; Ji et al., 2022

    Speaker notes

    The two CPD-0005 replicates, 55 and 61 nM, have a geometric mean of 57.9 nM, n = 2 [D:sm-potency]. For 10 and 1,000 nM the arithmetic mean is 505 nM against a geometric mean of 100 nM [D:eval-mini-bank]. ChEMBL compares potencies on the pChEMBL scale, a negative log of the molar value. Generation models can produce fluent, unintended text; a key written from memory carries the same risk. Ask: could a reviewer holding only the source re-run every DERIVED key?

    Gates zero an item, and the negative-control fabrication rate is the headline

    • Fabrication, false attribution, scope violation: score 0
    • Negative controls fabricated: 2 of 2 model-only, 0 of 2 reranked
    • Freeze outputs before anyone grades them

    Source: US Office of the Federal Register (eCFR); European Medicines Agency, 2024

    Speaker notes

    After a model update the reranker configuration rose from 1 to 2 fabrications and fell from a 74% to a 70% mean score [D:eval-run-summary]. Part 11 audit trails and the EMA's freeze-before-unblinding rule share the logic of frozen outputs. Ask: is any output in our last report editable after grading?

    An LLM judge counts only after kappa against an SME

    • Over 80% agreement with humans, with known biases
    • 90% agreement can still mean kappa of 0
    • Never let the system under test grade itself

    Source: Zheng et al. (arXiv, 2023; NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks), 2023; McHugh (Biochemia Medica 22(3):276-282), 2012

    Speaker notes

    LLM judges show position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases. Batch CAL-01 gives kappa 0.66 and CAL-02 0.79, both in McHugh's moderate band; messy CAL-05 agrees on 36 of 40 yet has kappa 0 [D:eval-judge-calibration]. Ask: how many items did our judge pass that an SME failed?

    The test set must be independent, and molecules need scaffold splits

    • Split early, before normalisation; use the hold-out once
    • Keep each scaffold on one side of the split
    • Report the positive rate next to every metric

    Source: European Medicines Agency, 2024; Wu et al. (Royal Society of Chemistry), 2018; Saito and Rehmsmeier (PLoS ONE), 2015

    Speaker notes

    EMA strongly encourages an early train-test split into separate datasets, before any normalisation. Scaffold splits separate structurally different molecules and are a harder test than random splits. On imbalanced data the precision-recall plot is more informative than ROC. Ask: what share of our test compounds share a scaffold with training?

    Vocabularies and traceability are part of the model

    • Unmapped OMOP rows (concept 0) vanish from analyses
    • MedDRA updates every March and September
    • Each AI-derived value names its SDTM records

    Source: OHDSI; ICH; CDISC, 2021

    Speaker notes

    Only standard concepts belong in fields ending _concept_id. ADaM traces to SDTM, and Define-XML is required by FDA and PMDA for each study. Ask: what was our mapping rate in the last ETL load?

    Model, embedding and dictionary updates go through change control

    • Rerun the frozen bank before every release
    • Draft Annex 22: only static, deterministic models in critical GMP use
    • With vendors, responsibility stays with the sponsor

    Source: European Commission (EMA GMDP Inspectors Working Group with PIC/S), 2025; US FDA (Federal Register), 2024; European Medicines Agency (ICH), 2025; European Medicines Agency and US FDA (joint), 2026

    Speaker notes

    The Annex 22 consultation draft says generative AI and LLMs should not be used in critical GMP applications; it is not adopted text. The FDA's change-control-plan guidance applies to AI-enabled devices only, but its structure (planned changes, method, impact assessment) is a usable template. The 2021 good machine learning practice principles are for medical devices. Principle 8 of the 2026 FDA-EMA principles asks that performance assessment cover the complete system, including human-AI interactions, so track how often reviewers change or reject answers. In the example run summary, a vendor demo scored 92% on outputs that were edited after the run and before grading, so the score measures the edited text, not the system [D:eval-run-summary]. Ask: can we pin the vendor's model version today?

    What you will be able to do

    • Design an evaluation bank whose items separate good systems from weak ones: knowledge layers, question types, negative controls, and answer keys with locators.
    • Score frozen outputs with gates, report the hallucination rate, and calibrate an LLM judge against a subject-matter expert using kappa.
    • Assemble the evidence a QA reviewer or regulator expects: context of use, credibility evidence, change control and production monitoring.

    Learn

    The earlier levels taught you the data objects, the standards and how to build a pipeline. This level is about one decision that you own as the data and AI engineer: is this model or AI system fit for a stated use, and what evidence shows it? The FDA’s draft guidance on AI for drug regulatory decisions frames that question as credibility for a specific context of use[1]. The EMA reflection paper asks the same of the sponsor: models, datasets and pipelines must be fit for purpose, which may be stricter than standard data-science practice[2].

    In January 2026 EMA and FDA published ten joint guiding principles of good AI practice in drug development. They are high-level principles meant to inform future guidance and standards. They include a clear context of use, multidisciplinary expertise, and data governance with traceable documentation of provenance and processing[3]. Principle 8 asks that performance assessment cover the complete system, including human-AI interactions[3]. If your context of use relies on a person checking the output, that check is part of what you evaluate.

    Start from the decision: context of use and model risk

    The FDA draft (January 2025, marked not for implementation) sets out seven steps, from the question of interest to a decision on whether credibility is adequate; the earlier modules list them[4]. At this level you write what steps two to six produce: the context of use (COU), the model risk assessment, the credibility plan, its results, and any deviations from the plan[4].

    Write the COU as one short paragraph before you build anything. An illustrative example: “The assistant retrieves internal potency and DMPK records for medicinal chemists preparing a compound-selection meeting. A chemist checks every cited record before use. The output does not enter a regulatory submission.” Note that the FDA draft puts drug discovery, and operational uses such as drafting a submission that do not affect patient safety, drug quality or study reliability, out of its scope[4]. The EMA paper covers every stage of a medicine’s lifecycle, from discovery to post-authorisation[5]. So even a discovery tool benefits from the same discipline, scaled to its risk.

    Model risk in the FDA draft combines two things[4]. Model influence is how much the output drives the decision compared with other evidence. Decision consequence is how bad a wrong decision would be. Take one retrieval model and two uses. In use A, it drafts meeting notes that a chemist reviews line by line: low influence, low consequence. In use B, it codes adverse event text to MedDRA preferred terms for a safety signal with no human review: high influence, high consequence. The EMA paper uses the terms “high patient risk” and “high regulatory impact” for the same idea[2]. ICH Q9(R1) gives the rule for how much work to do: effort, formality and documentation commensurate with the level of risk[6].

    Two documents travel with every model. A model card is a short document that states the intended use, the evaluation procedure and results under the relevant conditions and subgroups[7]. A datasheet does the same for each dataset: why it was built, what it contains, how it was collected and what it should be used for[8]. In a GMP setting, EU Annex 11 asks the process owner, system owner, qualified persons and IT to cooperate[9]. In practice you own the evaluation evidence and the recommendation. QA and the process owner accept or reject it.

    Four knowledge layers, and what a failure in each one tells you

    The bank design below is this guide’s evaluation practice, not a published standard. Where a paper states a fact the design relies on, the text cites it; the method itself is written as instructions.

    Tag every evaluation item with the layer that should be able to answer it. P (public or parametric) items can be answered from what the model learned in training. S (source) items can be answered only from the documents or tables given to the system. I (internal) items need the organisation’s own systems beyond that source, such as the ELN, LIMS or safety database. L (literature) items need published papers or public databases. Combinations such as S+I or S+L test integration. The split mirrors how retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is built: a trained model’s parametric memory plus a non-parametric memory, an index of documents reached by a retriever[10].

    The tags turn a score into a diagnosis. If P items fail, the model itself is weak; retrieval cannot fix it. If P items pass and S items fail, the index, chunking or retriever is broken. If S items pass and S+I items fail, the system cannot join records across sources. The most damaging pattern is an S or I item answered with a P value: the system is asked for “our” IC50 and returns a published value for a similar public compound, presented as internal. That is a false attribution, and it is the failure a combined system is most likely to make.

    The layers also guide two engineering choices. For the embedding model, the MTEB benchmark found that no single text-embedding method dominates across its 8 tasks and 58 datasets[11]. A leaderboard rank is therefore not evidence for your corpus; your S items are. For the reranker, the BEIR benchmark found BM25, a lexical method, to be a robust baseline, with reranking and late-interaction models giving the best zero-shot results at high computational cost[12]. A BERT cross-encoder reranker improved MRR@10 by 27% relative to the previous best on MS MARCO at the time[13]. The cost is that every query-document pair passes through the network, which raises compute by orders of magnitude[14]. Decide with your own bank: run the configuration with and without the reranker and compare S-layer items.

    Biopharma corpora are full of near-identical identifiers. LOT-2024-007 and LOT-2024-008 differ by one character, and in the example tables only the second has a water result out of specification[data]. Write items that ask for one exact identifier, and grade any answer that cites its neighbour as a fabrication, not as a near miss.

    Items that discriminate: question types, weights, the swap test and coverage

    Give each item a question type. Useful types are factual, retrieval, quantitative, reasoning, comparative, integration, troubleshooting, prioritisation, protocol, hypothesis, exploratory and abstention. Score each answer on separate dimensions: correctness, completeness, grounding (is each claim traced to a record), source discrimination (did it use and label the right layer), reasoning, calibration, actionability, novelty and abstention. RAG evaluation research makes the same move, scoring retrieval quality and faithful use of the retrieved passages as separate dimensions[15]. Then let the weights move with the type. A retrieval item puts most weight on grounding and source discrimination, because where the answer came from is what you are testing. A hypothesis item gives correctness no weight; reasoning carries the most, then actionability and novelty. An abstention item is scored almost entirely on whether the system said “no record found”.

    Apply the swap test to every item. Replace the subject with another of the same class. If the question and its expected answer stay equally valid, the item is a control, not a discriminator. “What does a CAR-T potency assay measure?” holds after any swap. “CT-LOT-2024-010 shows 48% specific lysis; can it be compared with CT-LOT-2024-009 at 71%?” does not, because CT-LOT-2024-010 was tested at an effector-to-target ratio of 1:1 and the others at 5:1[data]. Only a system that reads the row gets it right. Keep a few swap-proof items on purpose, as P-layer controls; EV-01 in the worked bank below is one.

    Rate difficulty by the reasoning chain, not by obscurity. Looking up a rare value is basic. Joining three records to reach a conclusion that is not stated anywhere is advanced. Before writing items, draw a coverage matrix: facets down the side (identity, context, methods, results, variation, comparison, mechanism, risk, negative control and others), layers across the top. A bank that is mostly identity and mechanism tests recall, not research ability.

    This guide’s working targets, not regulatory numbers, are these. At least one item in ten is a negative control. At least three in ten are multi-source. At least 40% are advanced or expert, and at least 40% are source-grounded. A twelve-item bank covers 8 of 12 facets and 6 question types or more. Each multi-source item names its join key (InChIKey, HGNC id plus UniProt accession, NCT number, OMOP standard concept); the intermediate module explains each. One OMOP rule matters for keys: concept ids up to 2,000,000,000 are reserved for the Standardized Vocabularies, and ids above that are for local use[16]. Such local concepts are never standard and may only sit in the _source_concept_id fields[17]. A join on condition_concept_id should therefore never meet one.

    Negative controls and ground truth with locators

    Question-answering systems tend to make unreliable guesses when the answer is not in the context. When SQuAD 2.0 added over 50,000 unanswerable questions written to look answerable, a strong system fell from 86% F1 to 66% F1[18]. The only correct answer to a negative control is an explicit “no record found”. Use three kinds. An absent record asks for a value never recorded, such as an IC50 for a compound with no row. Substitution bait asks for something where a public answer exists and could be passed off as internal, such as the registry number of an internal study code. A near-miss asks for a value that exists only under a different condition, such as human clearance when only rat was measured.

    Every answer key carries one of four ground-truth states. EXACT: the answer is stated in the source; the key copies value, units and qualifiers verbatim and gives a locator. DERIVED: the answer is computed from stated values; the key shows the formula and gives each input its own locator. SOURCE-DEPENDENT: the answer needs records outside the provided source; there is no key, only a validation note saying what a correct answer must cite. ABSENT: the source shows the thing is not there; the key records what was searched, under which synonyms. A locator is a path a reviewer can follow, for example “sm-potency, compound_id CPD-0003, column ic50_nm”. The FDA draft expects documented, assessable results[4].

    Keys go wrong in predictable ways. Censoring is part of the value: the negative control PRB-0001-NC has an IC50 recorded as greater than 10000 nM, and a key that writes 10000 is wrong[data]. Replicate potencies are averaged in log space, and the key stores n. CPD-0005 has two replicate IC50s, 55 and 61 nM; their geometric mean is √(55 × 61) = 57.9 nM, n = 2[data]. For two close replicates the arithmetic mean (58.0 nM) is nearly the same. For 10 and 1,000 nM it is 505 nM, against a geometric mean of 100 nM[data]. A mean potency across different compounds answers no question a chemist asks, so do not write items that need one. Conflicts are recorded, not resolved. Absence is shown by a search, not assumed. Above all, a key never contains an identifier nobody read in the source. Generation models are prone to produce fluent, unintended text[19]; a key written from memory carries the same failure into the answer key itself.

    Scoring: gates, the hallucination rate, frozen outputs and a calibrated judge

    Apply gates before any weighting. A fabrication (an invented identifier, citation or measured value) scores the item 0. A false attribution (public content presented as an internal record) scores 0. A scope violation (clinical, dosing, diagnostic or investment advice) scores 0 and goes to a separate review. A partial score would hide that these answers can enter a decision. Report the hallucination rate as fabrications divided by items, overall and on the negative controls separately. The negative-control rate is the headline safety number, because those items have no correct value to find. In the example run summary, the model-only configuration fabricated on both of its negative controls (100%), and the configuration with retrieval and a reranker on neither (0%)[data]. Overall, the reranker configuration fabricated on 1 of 12 items (8.3%); after a model update the same configuration fabricated on 2 of 12 (16.7%), and its mean score fell from 74% to 70%[data]. Items where a newer or richer configuration did worse are regression items. An average can hide them, so list them by item.

    Freeze outputs before grading. Store each output with a hash and a time stamp, and grade only the stored copy. 21 CFR 11.10(e) asks for secure, time-stamped audit trails that do not obscure earlier entries[23]. The EMA paper asks that, for pivotal trials, the pre-processing pipeline and models be frozen and documented before unblinding[2]. The same logic applies to a grading run: if anyone can edit an output after seeing the key, the score measures the editor.

    LLM judges can scale grading. Strong LLM judges reached over 80% agreement with human preferences, the same level as agreement between humans, but they show position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases[24]. So calibrate the judge against a subject-matter expert (SME) on a sample from every batch. Report percent agreement and Cohen’s kappa, which corrects agreement for chance; Cohen’s own scale may be too lenient for health work, since it implies a kappa as low as 0.41 might be acceptable[25]. Worked example from batch CAL-01: 34 of 40 agreements gives 0.85. The judge passed 28 of 40 (0.70) and the SME 26 of 40 (0.65). Chance agreement is 0.70 × 0.65 + 0.30 × 0.35 = 0.56. Kappa is (0.85 − 0.56) / (1 − 0.56) = 0.66[data]. McHugh’s stricter table reads 0.60 to 0.79 as moderate and 0.80 to 0.90 as strong[25], so this judge needs SME review of every pass it gives. Watch the cell where the judge passed and the SME failed: those 4 items are the ones a judge would let through[data]. Reference-free RAG metrics need no human ground truth[15]. They help with monitoring at scale, but they cannot check a value against a locator.

    The data underneath: splits, imbalance, vocabularies and traceability

    A model is only as credible as its test set. The FDA draft expects test data independent of development data and not shown during training[4]. The EMA paper adds that a hold-out test set is used once, and that further development needs a new independent test set. It strongly encourages an early train-test split into separate datasets, before any normalisation[2]. For molecules, a random split puts close analogues on both sides. A scaffold split divides compounds by their two-dimensional structural framework, so test compounds come from series the model has not seen; it is a harder and more honest test[26].

    Class imbalance is the normal case in pipeline data. Nine of ten candidates that enter clinical studies fail[27]. On imbalanced data the ROC plot can mislead, while the precision-recall plot shows the effect of imbalance directly[28]. MoleculeNet also found that learned representations struggle under data scarcity and highly imbalanced classification[26]. Report the positive rate of each split next to every metric. For bias, follow the model card: report results by the subgroups that matter for the use, such as sex or age group in real-world data[7].

    Vocabulary mapping is part of the model. In an OMOP ETL, condition_concept_id holds the standard concept and condition_source_value keeps the original code[29]. A row left at concept 0 is invisible to every analysis that uses standard concepts[data]. Track the mapping rate as an ETL metric, and keep the source value so a reviewer can check the mapping. MedDRA, the five-level terminology used to code adverse events[20], is updated twice a year, every March and September[30]. A dictionary update can change the terms your model sees and the terms your keys expect, so it is a change to your system.

    Clinical numbers must trace. ADaM analysis datasets are traceable to SDTM[31], and Define-XML describes the dataset structures; the FDA and PMDA require it for every study in an electronic submission[32]. If an AI step derives an analysis value, keep the same chain: each output names the SDTM records and the derivation it used[31].

    Production, change control and the file a reviewer reads

    A passing bank is the start of the lifecycle. The FDA draft notes that data drift needs lifecycle maintenance[4]. The 2021 good machine learning practice principles, written for medical devices, ask that deployed models be monitored and re-training risks managed[33]; for drug development, the 2026 EMA-FDA principles carry the same life-cycle view[3]. In practice, monitor five signals. Input drift: new document types, new assay versions, new sites. Retrieval health: rank of the gold record on a fixed set of canary queries. Abstention rate on live traffic. Gate counts from a scheduled rerun of the regression items and negative controls. Reviewer behaviour: how often reviewers change or reject a cited answer. Principle 8 of the 2026 principles asks for the human-AI interaction to be assessed as part of the system[3]. A falling override rate after launch is a reason to audit a sample, not proof of quality, because reviewers may simply have started to trust the tool (this guide’s practice).

    Treat four events as change control, each needing a rerun of the frozen bank before release: a model update from the vendor, a new embedding model (which means rebuilding the index), a dictionary version change, and a new bank version. For medical devices, the FDA’s final guidance on predetermined change control plans recommends describing planned modifications, the method to develop, validate and implement them, and an impact assessment[34]. That guidance covers devices only, but its structure is a useful template. The EMA paper does not accept incremental learning in pivotal trials[2].

    Manufacturing has its own draft. The EU consulted on a new GMP Annex 22 on artificial intelligence from July to October 2025[35]. The consultation draft applies to static models with deterministic output in critical GMP applications, and says generative AI and large language models should not be used there. In non-critical uses, which do not directly affect patient safety, product quality or data integrity, trained and qualified personnel stay responsible for checking that the output is suitable[36]. It is a draft, not adopted text.

    The file a reviewer reads has a fixed shape. It holds the COU statement and model risk assessment, the credibility plan, datasheets and the model card. It also holds the versioned bank with keys and locators, the frozen outputs, the scores with gate counts and judge calibration, and the human-review records with the override rate. Deviations, the validation report, and a periodic review date close the file. 21 CFR 11.10(a) requires system validation[23]. GAMP 5 gives the risk-based approach to fitness for intended use[37]. ICH E6(R3) section 4.3.4 bases validation of computerised systems on a risk assessment of intended use and the importance of the data[38]. For software used in medical-device production and quality systems, FDA’s Computer Software Assurance guidance (final September 2025; current revision February 2026) scales the detail of testing and evidence to process risk, from unscripted to scripted testing[39]. It is written for device makers, but it shows how assurance effort can follow risk.

    Vendor evaluation follows from one rule. Under ICH E6(R3), responsibility for trial conduct and data quality stays with the sponsor when work moves to a service provider[38]. Annex 11 asks for formal agreements that state supplier responsibilities[9]. So run your own bank on the vendor’s system, with outputs frozen and graded by your team. Ask which model version runs, how you will hear of updates, whether a version can be pinned, and whether retrieval traces can be logged. Principle 6 of the 2026 principles asks for privacy and protection of sensitive data throughout the life cycle[3], so also ask where your data is processed and whether your inputs are kept or used for training. Label every figure the vendor publishes as vendor-published. In the example run summary, a vendor demo scored 92% on outputs that were edited after the run and before grading, so the score measures the edited text, not the system[data].

    In this guide’s practice, a careful team never does five things. It never tunes prompts or retrieval on the test bank. It never lets the vendor or the system under test grade itself. It never reports a score without the bank version. It never writes a key from memory. And it never treats a blank gate count as zero.

    Example data

    The first table is an extract of six items from a separate evaluation bank, not the twelve-item worked example above. Each item has a layer, a question type, a difficulty, a ground-truth state, a locator and an expected answer. Look at the messy version. One key adds a public identifier nobody read. One averages two replicate potencies in nM and labels the result EXACT. One has no locator. One writes a value for a SOURCE-DEPENDENT item that should have none. One ABSENT item records no search. One resolves a conflict between two values by picking one.

    The second table summarises runs of four configurations on the same bank. Look at fabrications, fabrications on negative controls and negative-control abstentions, not only the mean score. In the messy version, one configuration ran on fewer items with no negative controls, one left the fabrication count blank, and one vendor demo was edited after the run and before grading.

    The third table is a judge calibration log: for each batch, how often an LLM judge and an SME agreed on the same frozen outputs. Each row is a two-by-two table, so you can compute kappa from it. In the messy version, look for unfrozen outputs, a missing judge version, a judge grading itself, cells that do not add up, and a batch with high agreement and no real skill.

    The fourth table is the split behind an activity classifier, with the label rule in its title. Each compound has a scaffold and a split. Look at whether any scaffold or compound appears on both sides, whether the assay version is the same in train and test, and whether every pIC50 is on the right scale.

    Try it

    1. Check the composition of the six bank items shown in the good eval-mini-bank table (the first example table). What share are negative controls, what share are multi-source, and what share are advanced or expert? Against this guide’s targets (at least 10%, at least 30% and at least 40%), what is missing? Then explain the error in messy row 2[data].
    Answer

    Negative controls: only EV-05 is marked Y, so 1 of 6 = 16.7%, above the 10% target[data]. Multi-source: only EV-04 (S+I) joins sources, so 1 of 6 = 16.7%, below 30%; you need at least 2 of 6, for example a join of sm-potency and sm-dmpk on compound_id and batch_id. Advanced or expert: EV-04 and EV-06, so 2 of 6 = 33%, below 40%; you need 3 of 6. Messy row 2 summarises the two CPD-0005 replicates as an arithmetic mean, (55 + 61) / 2 = 58.0 nM, and labels it EXACT[data]. The key should be DERIVED, with the formula, both replicate rows as locators and n = 2: the geometric mean is √(55 × 61) = 57.9 nM. Here the gap is small, but the method is wrong, and for 10 and 1,000 nM it gives 505 nM instead of 100 nM. If you average the stored pIC50 values instead, (7.26 + 7.21) / 2 = 7.235, about 58.2 nM; the small difference comes from rounding pIC50 to two decimals, so compute from the IC50 values or store more digits.

    1. Using the good run summary, compute the hallucination rate (fabrications divided by items) for each configuration, the negative-control hallucination rate (fabrications on negative controls divided by negative controls) and the negative-control abstention rate. Should CFG-D be released?
    Answer

    Hallucination rate: CFG-A 4 / 12 = 33.3%. CFG-B 2 / 12 = 16.7%. CFG-C 1 / 12 = 8.3%. CFG-D 2 / 12 = 16.7%[data]. Negative-control hallucination rate: 2 / 2 = 100%, 1 / 2 = 50%, 0 / 2 = 0% and 0 / 2 = 0%. Negative controls abstained: 0 of 2, 1 of 2, 2 of 2 and 2 of 2. CFG-D is CFG-C after a model update. Its abstentions held, but fabrications doubled from 1 to 2 and the mean score fell from 74% to 70%. That is a regression. Do not release: find the item that newly fabricated, file it against the change request, and rerun. Also note the messy CFG-B: 71% on 10 items with 0 negative controls cannot be compared with anything.

    1. Score an answer to WB-02 in the twelve-item bank. The answer says “3.1 nM, from sm-potency row CPD-0003”, labels it internal, but omits the assay version and ATP concentration. Use this illustrative retrieval weight set: correctness 0.25, completeness 0.15, grounding 0.35, source discrimination 0.20, calibration 0.05, each dimension scored 0, 1 or 2. Then rescore if the answer also adds a UniProt accession for the target that appears nowhere in the source[data].
    Answer

    Dimension scores: correctness 2 (3.1 nM matches the row), completeness 1 (assay ASY-KIN-07 v3 and 10 µM ATP missing), grounding 2, source discrimination 2, calibration 1[data]. Weighted raw score = 0.25 × 2 + 0.15 × 1 + 0.35 × 2 + 0.20 × 2 + 0.05 × 1 = 0.50 + 0.15 + 0.70 + 0.40 + 0.05 = 1.80 out of 2, so 90%. With the added accession, the fabrication gate fires: the item scores 0 and counts toward the hallucination rate, however good the rest is.

    1. From the judge calibration log, compute percent agreement and Cohen’s kappa for good batch CAL-02 and for messy batch CAL-05. What do you conclude about each[data]?
    Answer

    CAL-02: agreement = (22 + 14) / 40 = 0.90. Judge pass rate = (22 + 1) / 40 = 0.575; SME pass rate = (22 + 3) / 40 = 0.625. Chance agreement = 0.575 × 0.625 + 0.425 × 0.375 = 0.359 + 0.159 = 0.519. Kappa = (0.90 − 0.519) / (1 − 0.519) = 0.79[data]. Only 1 item was passed by the judge and failed by the SME. A kappa of 0.79 is at the top of McHugh’s moderate band (0.60 to 0.79), not yet strong, so keep SME review of the judge’s passes[25]. Messy CAL-05: agreement = (36 + 0) / 40 = 0.90 as well. But the judge passed all 40 (rate 1.0) and the SME passed 36 (0.90). Chance agreement = 1.0 × 0.90 + 0 × 0.10 = 0.90, so kappa = (0.90 − 0.90) / (1 − 0.90) = 0. Same agreement, no skill: the judge missed all 4 failures. Do not use that judge configuration.

    1. Audit the split table. List each defect in the messy version. Then, in the good version, compute the share of actives overall, in train and in test, using the label rule in the table title. What does that mean for the metric you report?
    Answer

    Messy defects[data]: scaffold SCF-01 is in train (CPD-0301) and test (CPD-0302), a scaffold leak. CPD-0303 is in both train and test, so the test set is not independent. CPD-0305’s test label comes from assay v2 while training used v3. CPD-0306 has 1260 in the pIC50 column, an IC50 in nM; as pIC50 it is 9 minus log10 of 1260, about 5.90, the good table’s value[40]. Good version, with active meaning pIC50 7.0 or more: actives are CPD-0301 (7.9) and CPD-0305 (7.4), so 2 of 6 = 33% overall, 1 of 4 = 25% in train and 1 of 2 = 50% in test. Precision depends on the positive rate, so report it with every metric. A 2-compound test set cannot support any metric; this table only shows the structure.

    1. Your bank has item WB-11: “How many distinct persons have a condition mapped to standard concept STD-PLACEHOLDER-A?” Answer it on the good OMOP table, then on the messy one. Write the key with its ground-truth state and locator. How many distinct persons does each table hold, and what does the value 2000000341 tell you[data]?
    Answer

    Good table: rows 1 and 3 carry STD-PLACEHOLDER-A, for persons 1001 and 1002, so 2 persons[data]. Messy table: row 1 is unmapped (concept 0) and row 3 is person 1002, so a query returns 1 person. The number looks plausible and is wrong by half. Distinct persons: good 3 (1001, 1002, 1003), messy 2, because row 4 loaded a different patient under 1002. Key: “DERIVED; 2 persons; locators rwe-omop rows with condition_concept_id STD-PLACEHOLDER-A (condition_occurrence_id 50001 and 50003); validation note: replace the placeholder with the standard concept looked up in Athena for the vocabulary release used; an answer of 1 means the ETL mapping failed”. The source concept 2000000341 is above 2,000,000,000, so it is a local concept: it may sit only in condition_source_concept_id and is never standard[16, 17].

    Check yourself

    1. Public-layer (P) items fail on every configuration, while source-layer (S) items pass. Where is the problem most likely? a) The retrieval index b) The model’s own knowledge c) The reranker d) The judge
    2. Which item is substitution bait? a) An IC50 for CPD-0006, which has no row b) Human clearance for CPD-0003, when only rat was measured c) The NCT number of STUDY-001, when the source holds no registry link d) The levels of the MedDRA hierarchy
    3. An LLM judge agrees with the SME on 90% of items, and kappa is 0. What happened? a) The judge passes almost everything, so agreement comes from the base rate b) The judge is near perfect c) The SME made errors d) The bank was too large
    4. An item’s answer needs the internal acceptance criterion for a CAR-T lot, which is not in the provided tables. Which ground-truth state applies? a) EXACT b) DERIVED c) SOURCE-DEPENDENT d) ABSENT
    5. A vendor announces a model update next month. What does a careful team do? a) Accept it, since the vendor validated it b) Watch production complaints after release c) Re-grade last quarter’s stored outputs d) Raise a change request and rerun the frozen bank, with regression items and negative controls, before release

    Answer key: 1-b, 2-c, 3-a, 4-c, 5-d
