    • No text embedding model won every MTEB task
    • Molecules: ECFP fingerprints; proteins: language-model embeddings

    • Store version, status and locator with every chunk
    • Filter on MedDRA or OMOP standard concept ids

    Hybrid search rewards agreement between lexical and dense retrievers

    • BM25 is strong on exact ids and stays a robust baseline
    • Rank fusion adds 1/(60 + rank) from each list
    • Ranks 2 and 2 beat ranks 1 and 4

    Source: Robertson and Zaragoza (now Publishers), 2009; Thakur et al. (arXiv, 2021; NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks), 2021; Bruch, Gai and Ingber (arXiv), 2023

    Speaker notes

    Worked example: 1/61 + 1/64 = 0.03202, while 2/62 = 0.03226. Dense retrievers beat BM25 in their training domain but often fell behind it zero-shot. Ask: which of your queries are really identifier lookups?

    A cross-encoder reranker fixes the top of the list, at a cost

    • Reads query and passage together, scores each pair
    • BERT reranker: 27% relative gain in MRR@10
    • Run it on a short list from a recall-tuned first stage

    Source: Nogueira and Cho (arXiv), 2019; Khattab and Zaharia (arXiv; SIGIR 2020), 2020; Thakur et al. (arXiv, 2021; NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks), 2021

    Speaker notes

    Passing every pair through the network raises cost by orders of magnitude. On BEIR rerankers gave the best average zero-shot results at high compute cost. Ask: what latency can your users accept for a better top 3?

    Retrieval enforces permissions before ranking and reads documents as data

    • Filter on the user's access groups inside the retriever
    • Arm-level results leaking in a blinded trial is an unblinding
    • Indexed text can carry instructions: indirect prompt injection

    Source: European Medicines Agency (ICH), 2025; US National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2024

    Speaker notes

    ICH E6(R3) asks that user access be assigned by duties, functions and blinding arrangements. NIST describes indirect prompt injection as attackers placing prompts in data likely to be retrieved. In the messy chunk index an unblinded listing from STUDY-002 is open to all users [D:rag-chunk-index]. Ask: where in your pipeline is the permission check, before or after ranking?

    Grounded answers carry a locator, or the system abstains

    • Every claim: document id, version, section, page
    • No answering record means a stated abstention
    • Score retrieval, faithfulness and answer quality separately

    Source: Lewis et al. (arXiv, 2020; NeurIPS 2020), 2020; Gao et al. (arXiv preprint), 2023; Es et al. (arXiv, 2023; EACL 2024 demo), 2023

    Speaker notes

    RAG pairs a model with a document index to help provenance; refusing when nothing answers is called negative rejection. In the good table recall@5 is 4/5 = 0.80 and MRR is 0.567 [D:retrieval-eval]. Ask: does your test bank include questions with no answer?

    Docking, FEP and AlphaFold outputs are predictions, not measurements

    • Docking scores rank poses; they are not affinities
    • FEP predicts relative potency between named analogue pairs
    • Check pLDDT per residue and PAE between domains

    Source: Pantsar and Poso (MDPI), 2018; Warren et al. (American Chemical Society), 2006; Mondal, Florian and Warshel (J Phys Chem B), 2019; Ross et al., Schrödinger Inc (Communications Chemistry), 2023; Varadi et al. (Nucleic Acids Research), 2022

    Speaker notes

    Ten docking programs and 37 scoring functions gave no useful affinity prediction. A 1 kcal/mol FEP error can mean up to a 5-fold difference in affinity. Ask: where in your warehouse could a predicted value end up in a measured column?

    Key graph edges on ontology ids and record the ontology version

    • Open Targets keys evidence to Ensembl gene and EFO disease ids

    Source: Ochoa et al. (Oxford University Press), 2021; Himmelstein et al. (eLife), 2017

    Speaker notes

    Hetionet v1.0 has 47,031 nodes and 2,250,197 relationships from 29 public resources, with source and licence stored on edges. One term can carry labels in several languages, so a label is not a key. In the messy table a query on DOID:2377 misses the edge keyed by its label [D:kg-edges]. Ask: which ontology version did your graph load last, and where is it recorded?

    Random splits flatter models; scaffold and time splits are closer to reality

    • Ignoring atom types, 32 frameworks describe half of 5,120 drugs
    • Scaffold split is harder than random
    • Time split comes closer to prospective results than random selection

    Source: Bemis and Murcko (Journal of Medicinal Chemistry, American Chemical Society), 1996; Wu et al. (Royal Society of Chemistry), 2018; Sheridan (American Chemical Society), 2013

    What you will be able to do

    • Specify a life-science AI task before choosing a model: the question, the label and its unit, the split and the acceptance metric.
    • Build and test retrieval over protocols, CSRs and SOPs: structure-aware chunks, hybrid search, code filters, reranking, cited answers and abstention.
    • Judge a property or structure prediction by its split, applicability domain, calibration and confidence scores, and document it for a GxP context of use.

    Learn

    Start with the question, the label and the funnel

    Write the question down before you choose a model. Without it, nobody can say what a right answer is. The FDA draft’s first two steps, the question of interest and the context of use, are covered in the earlier modules; use them as the template here[1].

    Next, say exactly what the label is. An activity label is a measurement with an assay, a unit and a threshold. ChEMBL’s pChEMBL value is the negative log10 of a molar IC50, EC50, Ki or Kd, so 1 nM is 9[2]. “Active means IC50 under 1 µM” is therefore the same rule as “pIC50 above 6”. A value reported as “greater than 10 µM” is censored: the true value is unknown, so it is not an exact 5.0. The OECD principles for QSAR models (quantitative structure-activity relationships) list a defined endpoint first, ahead of the algorithm[3]. Mixing two assay versions or two species under one label breaks that first principle.

    Then count the positives. A project might screen 200,000 to more than 1,000,000 compounds, and hundreds of leads are narrowed to one or two candidates[4]. In BIO’s 2011-2020 data, 7.9% of programmes entering Phase I reached approval[5]. Positive examples are scarce at every stage, and that one fact shapes the split, the metric and the size of any claim you can make. MoleculeNet’s authors found that learned representations still struggle under data scarcity and highly imbalanced classes[6].

    Data readiness is the last check before modelling. A datasheet for a dataset records its motivation, composition, collection process and recommended uses[7]. For assay data that means the assay id and version, the unit, the censoring rule, the date range and who curated it. If you cannot fill in the datasheet, the data is not ready.

    Embeddings for text, molecules and proteins

    An embedding is a fixed-length list of numbers that stands for an item, built so that similar items sit close together. Closeness is usually scored with cosine similarity. Sentence-BERT showed why this matters for cost: finding the most similar pair among 10,000 sentences takes about 65 hours with a BERT model that reads every pair together, and about 5 seconds with sentence embeddings[8].

    Choose the text embedding model by testing it on your own documents. The MTEB benchmark covered 8 task types, 58 datasets and 112 languages, and found that no single text embedding method dominated across all tasks[9]. A model that ranks first on web questions may not be first on protocol sections full of drug codes and dose tables. Build a small gold set from your corpus first, then compare two or three models on it.

    Molecules have their own embeddings. Extended-connectivity fingerprints (ECFPs) are circular fingerprints built for structure-activity modelling; each bit records whether a particular substructure is present[10]. Two fingerprints are usually compared with the Tanimoto index: the bits set in both, divided by the bits set in either[11]. A Tanimoto value only means something with its fingerprint type and radius stated, because a different fingerprint gives a different number for the same pair.

    Proteins have learned embeddings too. Rives and colleagues trained a protein language model on 86 billion amino acids across 250 million sequences, and its representations carried biological properties learned from sequence alone[12]. ESMFold predicts structure from a later model of this family[13]. Key protein records on the UniProt accession, the identifier of the protein entry, not on a gene name typed by hand[14]. One rule covers all three kinds: never compare vectors made by two different models or two versions of one model.

    Chunks, metadata and vocabulary filters

    Retrieval works on chunks, not whole documents. The most common method splits text into a fixed number of tokens such as 100, 256 or 512; larger chunks carry more context but more noise and cost[15]. Regulated documents have structure you should use instead. A clinical study report (CSR) follows the single core structure of ICH E3[16], so chunk by section, keep each table whole with its header, and record the section and table number.

    Each chunk carries metadata: file name, page, author, category and timestamp, and retrieval can be filtered on these fields[15]. In a trial master file the system must support document identification, version history, search and retrieval[17]. So the index stores document id, version and status (effective or superseded) with every chunk. Without a status filter a superseded protocol can outrank the current one.

    Filters work best on codes, not strings. MedDRA, the medical dictionary for adverse events, has five levels from system organ class down to lowest level term, with 27 system organ classes[18]. FDA study data use MedDRA for adverse events and WHODrug Global for concomitant medications[19]. In OMOP (the common data model for real-world data), only standard concepts go in fields ending in _concept_id, and several source codes can map to one standard concept[20]. The original code stays in condition_source_value[21]. An OMOP ETL (extract, transform, load) that maps local codes well gives you a clean filter key; one that leaves condition_concept_id at 0 hides those rows from every concept filter.

    Retrieval, reranking and grounded answers

    Lexical retrieval matches words. BM25 sums a weight for each query term; the weight rises with term frequency but saturates, and is normalised for document length against the collection average[22]. Because it matches exact tokens, it suits identifier lookups such as CPD-0001, LOT-2024-007 or an NCT number (“NCT” plus 8 digits)[23]. Dense retrieval matches embeddings. A trained dense retriever beat a strong BM25 system by 9 to 19 points in top-20 accuracy on open-domain question sets[24]. Across 18 different datasets, BM25 stayed a robust baseline and dense models often did worse outside the domain they were trained on[25].

    Hybrid retrieval runs both and merges the lists. Reciprocal rank fusion gives each document 1/(60 + rank) from each list and adds them[26]. Worked example: chunk A is rank 1 in BM25 and rank 4 in dense, so it scores 1/61 + 1/64 = 0.01639 + 0.01563 = 0.03202. Chunk B is rank 2 in both, so it scores 2/62 = 0.03226 and comes first. Agreement between two retrievers beats one strong vote. A vector database stores the embeddings and finds near neighbours with approximate search, for example HNSW, a layered graph searched from the top layer down[27]. Approximate means it can miss a neighbour, so apply hard filters (study, status, product) before ranking and test recall after any index rebuild.

    A reranker fixes the top of the list. A cross-encoder reads the query and one passage together and scores the pair; a BERT reranker improved MRR@10 on the MS MARCO passage benchmark by 27% relative to the previous best[28]. The price is that every query-passage pair passes through the network, which raises cost by orders of magnitude[29]. On BEIR, reranking gave the best zero-shot results on average but at high computational cost[25]. The usual design is a cheap first stage tuned for recall, then a reranker on a short list. Choose the reranker the way you chose the embedding model: on your gold set, with latency measured.

    Retrieval must respect who may read what. Enforce each user’s document permissions inside the retriever, before ranking, not only in the user interface; otherwise an answer can quote a record the user may not open. In a blinded trial that leak is an unblinding. ICH E6(R3) asks that user access be assigned by duties, functions and blinding arrangements, and that roles for access to unblinded information be defined and documented[30]. So store an access group with every chunk, filter on it before any score is computed, and log which chunks each answer used[data].

    Treat indexed text as data, not instructions. Text inside a document can try to redirect the model. NIST calls this prompt injection, and describes an indirect form in which attackers place prompts in data likely to be retrieved[31]. A protocol, a supplier PDF or a literature abstract can all carry such text. Keep retrieved passages apart from the system’s instructions, never let a passage trigger an action on its own, and include a few planted-instruction documents in the retrieval test set.

    Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) joins a language model to a document index read by a retriever, which helps with provenance and updating knowledge[32]. In a regulated setting provenance is a requirement: every claim carries the document id, version, section and page. When no record answers the question, the system should say so. Gao and colleagues call this ability negative rejection[15]. Ragas scores retrieval relevance, faithfulness to the retrieved text and answer quality as separate dimensions[33]. A careful team scores them separately too, because a fluent answer can be unfaithful to a perfectly retrieved passage.

    Predictions are not measurements

    ADMET models (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity) and other QSAR models predict a property from structure. Store their output in a separate column or table from assay results, with the model version, and never let a predicted value fill a gap in a measured column. Docking estimates how a compound sits in a binding site. Whether it can estimate binding affinity has been answered “no” in several comprehensive analyses[34]. In one study of 10 docking programs and 37 scoring functions on eight proteins, none made a useful prediction of binding affinity[35]. A docking score is a ranking aid, not a Kd.

    Free energy perturbation (FEP) computes relative binding potency between close analogues. An error of 1 kcal/mol can mean a wrong prediction of up to a 5-fold difference in binding affinity[36]. Free-energy calculations can still be appealing for guiding lead optimisation at 1 to 2 kcal/mol when many compounds will be made, and may add little if very few will be[37]. A 2023 benchmark from Schrödinger, developer of the FEP+ software, reports a median root-mean-square error of 1.08 kcal/mol across 1,237 ligands, estimated retrospectively, and says reported errors are typically higher in prospective use, so treat it as developer-published evidence[38]. Record FEP results as predicted differences between named pairs, not as measured potencies.

    AlphaFold predicts protein structure. In the CASP14 assessment its median backbone accuracy was 0.96 Å against 2.8 Å for the next best method[39]. Each residue carries pLDDT, a confidence from 0 to 100: 90 and above is very high, 70 to under 90 confident, 50 to under 70 low, and under 50 very low, often a sign of disorder[40]. Predicted aligned error (PAE) gives the expected error in Å between pairs of residues; high PAE between two domains means their relative position should not be trusted[40]. A good mean pLDDT can hide a poorly placed domain, so check both.

    Join keys for public sources (InChIKey, ChEMBL id, UniProt accession, NCT number) are covered in the intermediate module. Keep the internal id as the key and store the public id, its source and the retrieval date beside it.

    Ontologies and knowledge graphs as join keys

    A knowledge graph stores entities as nodes and relations as edges. Hetionet v1.0, built from 29 public resources, has 47,031 nodes of 11 types and 2,250,197 relationships of 24 types[41]. A graph like that is only as good as its keys. Key every node on an ontology id, not on a label. Open Targets maps every piece of evidence to an Ensembl gene id and an EFO disease id (EFO is the Experimental Factor Ontology)[42]. The Hetionet authors converted every identifier to their node vocabularies before adding edges, and they called mapping by gene symbol disfavoured because symbols can be ambiguous[41]. Labels are weak keys for a second reason: one term can carry labels in several languages[43].

    Treat the ontology version as data. An ontology is a hierarchy. Open Targets propagates evidence from a child term up to its parents, so a disease has indirect associations next to its direct ones[42]. When Open Targets adopted EFO version 3, the number of associations rose by 90% in its 19.11 release; the larger ontology let new direct associations in and spread evidence further through indirect ones[42]. An ontology update changes query answers even when no new experiment was run. So record the ontology and its version on every edge, record whether the edge is direct or propagated, and re-run the graph’s test queries after each ontology update. Terms also get deprecated or moved, and the edges that use them go stale; the OLS4 authors name assessing that impact as one use of searching annotations[43]. Mappings between ontologies are often subjective and project-dependent[43], so store which mapping set you used.

    The Ontology Lookup Service (OLS) at EMBL-EBI is a search engine for ontologies, used to annotate data with ontology terms[44]. Use it at load time to check that each id exists and that its label matches the stored one. Edges need provenance too. Hetionet’s JSON and Neo4j releases carry the source, the licence and a confidence score on each edge[41]. Of the 31 resources its authors considered, nine had no licence at all[41]. An edge without a source cannot be checked or weighed, and an edge from an unlicensed source may not be reusable.

    Splits, leakage and the applicability domain

    Chemistry comes in families, so a random split flatters a model. Bemis and Murcko reduced drugs to frameworks (ring systems and linkers). Ignoring atom and bond types, 5,120 drugs had 1,179 frameworks, and the 32 most common described half of them; with atom types there were 2,506 frameworks[45]. State which scaffold definition your split uses. A random split puts siblings from one series on both sides, and the test measures recall of the family. A scaffold split groups compounds by framework and is a harder test than a random split[6]. A time split trains on older compounds and tests on newer ones; it gave R² values closer to true prospective prediction than random selection, which was too optimistic[46].

    Leakage is any path by which test information reaches training. Common paths are a compound registered twice, a re-test batch, or a salt form of the same parent. A salt has its own InChIKey, which differs from the parent’s in the first block[47, 48], so raw keys do not catch it. Map each salt form to its parent first, as ChEMBL’s molecule hierarchy maps imatinib mesylate (CHEMBL1642) to its parent CHEMBL941[49]. Then de-duplicate on the parent’s full standard InChIKey before splitting; the first block alone gives a stricter, stereo-blind check[50]. The EMA reflection paper strongly encourages an early train-test split, before any normalisation, and evaluates the final model once on the hold-out test set; further development needs a new independent test set[51]. The FDA draft wants test data independent of development data and not shown during training[1].

    The applicability domain is the chemical space where a model was built to predict. OECD principle 3 asks for a defined domain because QSAR models only predict reliably for certain structures, properties and mechanisms[3]. A simple domain rule is the Tanimoto similarity of a new compound to its nearest training compound, with fingerprint, radius and threshold written down[10, 11]. Report out-of-domain predictions separately. A model card records intended use, evaluation conditions and performance across relevant groups[52]. For a property model the relevant groups are chemical series, assay versions and time windows, so one dominant series cannot hide weak results elsewhere.

    Evaluation: retrieval, imbalanced labels, calibration and judges

    Retrieval metrics come from ranks. Recall@k is the share of queries whose correct record appears in the top k. MRR averages 1/rank of the first correct record, so rank 3 scores 0.333. nDCG adds up relevance down the list, discounted by rank, and divides by the ideal ranking[53]. With the usual log2 discount, one relevant record at rank 3 scores 1/log2(4) = 0.5. Gao and colleagues list hit rate, MRR and NDCG as standard retrieval scores[15]. Always record k, the index version and the gold set version with the result.

    Prediction metrics must respect scarcity. On imbalanced data the precision-recall plot changes with class balance while the ROC plot does not, and Saito and Rehmsmeier found the precision-recall plot more informative there[54]. A random classifier scores 0.5 AUROC whatever the balance, but its PR-AUC is about the positive rate. With 20 actives in 400 test compounds, that baseline is 0.05[data]. An AUROC of 0.91 and a PR-AUC of 0.52 can describe the same run; the second tells a chemist how many flagged compounds will be real.

    Regulators ask for the same care. The EMA reflection paper prefers metrics that are insensitive to class imbalance; for classification these could include the Matthews correlation coefficient, which uses the full confusion matrix. It also strongly encourages sensitivity analyses that stratify the training data by calendar time[51].

    Calibration asks whether probabilities mean what they say: of compounds given 0.7, about 70% should be positive. Modern neural networks are often poorly calibrated, and temperature scaling, a one-parameter fix, works on most datasets[55]. Check calibration in bins on the untouched test set, and only inside the applicability domain.

    LLM judges grade free-text answers at scale. Strong judges reached over 80% agreement with human preferences, the level humans reach with each other, but showed position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases[56]. Calibrate a judge before trusting it: have a subject-matter expert grade a sample of frozen outputs, compare verdicts cell by cell, and use a judge model different from the system under test. Human review stays on the items the judge and expert disagree on.

    GxP: context of use, freezing, change control and monitoring

    Regulators frame AI by risk. The EMA reflection paper, adopted in September 2024, uses the terms high patient risk and high regulatory impact. The sponsor is responsible for making sure models, datasets and pipelines are fit for purpose under GxP (the good-practice rules for regulated work)[51]. For pivotal trials, the pre-processing pipeline and models are frozen and documented before unblinding, and incremental learning is not accepted[51]. The FDA draft ties the evidence needed to model risk for the stated context of use[1].

    Every component that changes an output is under change control: the model, the embedding model, the reranker, the prompt, the index build and the dictionary versions. MedDRA itself is versioned; version 29.0 is dated March 2026[18]. Model outputs used in GxP work are electronic records, so ALCOA++ applies: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available and traceable, with audit trails[57]. In clinical data, ADaM datasets trace back to SDTM[58], and reviewers expect traceability from analysis results to source data[19]. A model-derived variable needs the same lineage: input dataset version, model version and run id.

    Monitoring continues after release. The 2021 good machine learning practice principles were written for medical devices; they ask for deployed models to be monitored and re-training risks managed[59]. For drug development, FDA and EMA published ten guiding principles of good AI practice in January 2026. They are high-level principles meant to inform future guidance and standards, and they include a clear context of use and documented data governance[60]. The FDA draft names data drift as a reason for life-cycle maintenance[1]. In practice, re-run the frozen gold set after every change, and track the share of predictions outside the domain over time. A careful team never tunes on the test set, never reports AUROC alone on a 5% positive rate, never lets a docking score stand in for a Kd, and never ships an answer without a locator.

    Example data

    The chunk index is what retrieval actually searches. Look at the status column, the locator, the embedding model and the access group. In the messy table, one superseded protocol is marked effective, one chunk was embedded with an older model, a fixed-size split left a table header on its own, and an unblinded listing from an ongoing trial is open to all users.

    The retrieval test log records where the expected record ranked for each query. Look at the negative control (a query with no answering record), the missing k and index version, and the query whose top hit was an old protocol version.

    The property-model run log scores one activity label on random, scaffold, time and prospective test sets. Watch AUROC and PR-AUC fall as the split gets more honest, and look for the leaked compounds and the training cut-off in the messy table.

    The prediction log holds one row per compound: the probability, the similarity to the nearest training compound, the domain flag and the measured class. Look for the compound that was in its own training set and the confident prediction outside the domain.

    The knowledge-graph edge table links internal targets to disease terms. The disease ids are real ontology ids taken from the Hetionet and OLS4 papers; the targets and evidence ids are illustrative. Look at the term id column, the direct-or-propagated column and the ontology load. In the messy table one label stands in for an id, one id does not match its label, one edge has no evidence source and one has no ontology version.

    Try it

    1. Use the good retrieval test table. Ignore the negative control Q-005. Compute recall@5, MRR and nDCG@5 (one relevant record per query, log2 discount). What does Q-004 tell you about the index?[data]
    Answer

    The five answerable queries have gold ranks 1, 3, not retrieved, 2 and 1[data]. Recall@5 = 4/5 = 0.80. MRR = (1 + 0.333 + 0 + 0.5 + 1)/5 = 2.833/5 = 0.567. nDCG terms are 1/log2(rank + 1): 1, 0.5, 0, 0.631, 1, so nDCG@5 = 3.131/5 = 0.626.

    Q-004 ranked the gold PROT-001 v3 second, behind PROT-001 v2[data]. The superseded version is competing with the current one. A status filter at indexing time fixes this, and the chunk index shows how it happens: CH-0002 (v2) is marked effective in the messy table[data].

    1. In the messy chunk index, a user asks for safety assessments in STUDY-001 and the filter is study_id = STUDY-001 and status = effective. Which chunks pass the filter, and which of those can be cited?[data]
    Answer

    CH-0001 and CH-0002 pass (both protocols marked effective for STUDY-001). CH-0003 and CH-0004 are status final, not effective, so this filter drops the CSR; the filter should allow “final” for reports. CH-0005 has free-text “Study 1”, so it fails the filter by accident[data].

    Of the two that pass, CH-0001 has no locator, so it cannot be cited. CH-0002 has a locator, but it is protocol v2, which was superseded. The only correct, citable answer is v3 with its locator, as in the good table: “Sec 9.2 Safety assessments, p. 41”[data].

    1. Use the good property-model run log. For RUN-01 (random split) and RUN-02 (scaffold split), compute the positive rate, the precision of the flagged set and the recall. Then compare RUN-03 with the baseline RUN-04.[data]
    Answer

    Both runs have 20 actives in 400, a positive rate of 20/400 = 0.05[data]. RUN-01 flags 30 with 14 true: precision 14/30 = 0.467, recall 14/20 = 0.70. RUN-02 flags 35 with 9 true: precision 9/35 = 0.257, recall 9/20 = 0.45. The scaffold split nearly halves precision; PR-AUC drops from 0.52 to 0.24 while AUROC drops less, from 0.91 to 0.78.

    On the time split, RUN-03 flags 33 with 6 true (precision 0.182) and the nearest-neighbour baseline RUN-04 flags 30 with 5 true (precision 0.167)[data]. PR-AUC is 0.19 against 0.14. The model beats the baseline, but by a small margin on 15 actives; the prospective RUN-05 found 1 active in 8 flagged.

    1. Find the leakage in the messy run log. How much of RUN-01’s test set had already been seen, and why is RUN-03 not a time split?[data]
    Answer

    RUN-01 was split by measurement row, and 37 of 400 test compounds share an InChIKey with training compounds: 37/400 = 9.25%[data]. Its AUROC rises from 0.91 to 0.95 and PR-AUC from 0.52 to 0.68 against the good table.

    RUN-03’s training snapshot is 2025-06-30, but the test compounds were registered from 2025-01-01 to 2025-06-30[data]. The test compounds and their labels were available for training. AUROC climbs from 0.74 to 0.86. The fix is a cut-off before the test window, as in the good table (2024-12-31).

    1. Use the good prediction log. Count in-domain and out-of-domain compounds. For in-domain rows, compare the mean predicted probability with the observed share of “high”. Then list the compounds called high at a 0.5 threshold and the precision.
    Answer

    Five compounds are in domain (similarity at least 0.40) and one, CPD-0205 at 0.24, is out[data]. In-domain mean probability = (0.85 + 0.12 + 0.62 + 0.35 + 0.58)/5 = 2.52/5 = 0.504. Observed high = 2 of 5 = 0.40. That is close, but five rows cannot establish calibration; you would need many compounds per probability bin.

    At 0.5, in-domain calls are CPD-0201 (high), CPD-0203 (high) and CPD-0206 (low): precision 2/3 = 0.67[data]. The out-of-domain CPD-0205 had the highest probability, 0.91, and was low. Reporting it with the in-domain set would drop precision to 2/4 = 0.50.

    1. The AlphaFold DB entry for UniProt P00533 has mean pLDDT 75.94 and residue fractions 0.228 very low, 0.065 low, 0.233 confident and 0.474 very high. What share of residues is below 70, and what would you check before using a domain for structure-based design?[61]
    Answer

    Below 70 = very low + low = 0.228 + 0.065 = 0.293, so about 29% of residues[61]. Confident or better = 0.233 + 0.474 = 0.707. The mean of 75.94 sits in the confident band and hides that nearly a third of the chain is low confidence.

    Before design, check pLDDT over the binding-site residues, not the mean, and check PAE between the domain you use and any partner domain; high PAE means their relative placement is unreliable[40]. Record the model id AF-P00533-F1 and the database version with the analysis. The AlphaFold DB API returns several models for P00533, including isoform models such as AF-P00533-2-F1 next to AF-P00533-F1; select the entry whose id matches the canonical accession[61].

    1. A scientist queries the knowledge graph by term id. In the good and the messy edge tables, which edges does a query for DOID:2377 (multiple sclerosis) return, and how many are direct? Then do the same for MONDO:0006052 (pulmonary tuberculosis).[data]
    Answer

    Good table, DOID:2377: KG-0001 (TGT-A, direct), KG-0002 (TGT-A, propagated from a child term) and KG-0005 (TGT-C, direct). That is 3 edges, 2 direct[data]. Messy table: KG-0002 and KG-0005, both marked direct. The count of direct edges is still 2, but it is right by accident. KG-0001 drops out because its id is a label, and KG-0002 is a propagated edge mislabelled as direct[data].

    Good table, MONDO:0006052: one edge, KG-0004, with an evidence source. Messy table: two edges. KG-0003 is about extrapulmonary tuberculosis stored under the pulmonary id, and KG-0004 has no evidence source, so neither can be used as it stands[data]. An id-to-label check at load time catches KG-0003. OLS, a search engine for ontologies, is one place to look the terms up[44].

    1. A blinded study physician on STUDY-002 belongs to the access groups “STUDY-002 team” and “all staff”. She asks which adverse events have been reported in STUDY-002. In the good and in the messy chunk index, which chunks may the retriever rank, and what should the answer be?[data]
    Answer

    Good table: the permission filter runs first and keeps only chunks whose access group is one of hers. That leaves CH-0005, the SOP (“all staff”), which does not answer the question[data]. CH-0006, the listing by treatment arm, is “STUDY-002 unblinded team only” and is dropped before any score is computed. The right answer is that no record she may open answers the question, which is negative rejection[15].

    Messy table: CH-0006 is open to “all”, so it passes the filter. It is the only STUDY-002 chunk, so it ranks first and the answer quotes adverse events by arm[data]. That is an unblinding through retrieval. ICH E6(R3) asks that access be assigned by blinding arrangements, and that any unplanned unblinding be documented and assessed for its impact on the trial results[30].
