    Stages, modalities, departments and data: who owns which problem, and who signs

    About 8 in 100 programmes entering Phase I are approved

    • Phase I 52.0%, Phase II 28.9%, Phase III 57.8%
    • 90.6% of submissions approved; 7.9% overall from Phase I
    • Lack of efficacy explains 40-50% of clinical failures

    Source: BIO, Informa Pharma Intelligence and QLS Advisors, 2021; Sun, Gao, Hu and Zhou, 2022

    Speaker notes

    The rates come from 9,704 programmes tracked from 2011 to 2020. Multiply the four rates to get 7.9%. Ask the room: which stage is your prospect in, and what failure do they fear most?

    Each stage ends in a filing that a regulator reads

    • IND (US) or one EU application through CTIS before first dose
    • FDA review 6-10 months once filed
    • EMA evaluation up to 210 active days

    Source: US FDA, 2018; European Commission; US FDA, 2018; European Medicines Agency

    Speaker notes

    CTIS became the single EU entry point for trial applications on 31 January 2023. The European Commission decides within 67 days of the EMA opinion. Ask: which filing does your tool's output end up in?

    Nine modalities are made, dosed and measured differently

    • Small molecule and peptide: tablets or weekly injections, dosed in mg
    • CAR-T and gene-edited stem cells: one lot made for one patient
    • AAV gene therapy: one infusion, dosed in vg/kg

    Source: European Medicines Agency; European Medicines Agency; European Medicines Agency; European Medicines Agency, 2024; CADTH (NCBI Bookshelf)

    Speaker notes

    Approved examples: imatinib, semaglutide, pembrolizumab, trastuzumab deruxtecan, tisagenlecleucel, exagamglogene autotemcel, onasemnogene abeparvovec, nusinersen and Comirnaty. Exagamglogene autotemcel is made from the patient's own blood stem cells, edited with CRISPR/Cas9. An ADC lot carries three lot ids: antibody, drug-linker and conjugate [D:adc-dar]. Ask: does the tool you sell assume one modality's records?

    Every department has one system of record, and it owns the problem

    • Lab: ELN, LIMS; trials: EDC, CTMS, eTMF
    • Plant: MES at ISA-95 Level 3, ERP at Level 4
    • Safety database, QMS, RIM, CRM, data platform

    Source: Raviteja and Gupta, JSS College of Pharmacy, 2013; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; European Medicines Agency, 2018; ISA

    Speaker notes

    The system of record is the one copy that counts. Safety cases leave the safety database as E2B(R3) messages; real-world data is often mapped to OMOP. Ask: which system holds the record your prospect wants to improve, and who owns it?

    Good data passes five tests: identity, unit, version, provenance, context

    • Identity: InChIKey, UniProt accession, NCT number
    • Unit: nM and µM differ by a factor of 1,000
    • Provenance: time-stamped audit trails that keep earlier entries

    Source: InChI Trust (IUPAC); UniProt Consortium, 2026; US National Library of Medicine, ClinicalTrials.gov; US National Institute of Standards and Technology; US Office of the Federal Register (eCFR)

    Speaker notes

    In row 1 of the messy table a pIC50 of 7.9 sits in the IC50 column; the true IC50 is 12.5 nM [D:sm-potency]. pIC50 is the negative log of the molar IC50. Ask: which of the five tests would have caught it?

    A release table is only good if failures stay visible

    • Every result needs method version, unit and limit
    • A result outside its limit is out of specification
    • A retest cannot erase the first failing result

    Source: European Medicines Agency (ICH), 2000; US FDA, 2022

    Speaker notes

    In the messy table, water at 0.62% w/w against a limit of 0.5 is not flagged, and a failing first assay run of 93.8 hides behind a retest of 101.0 [D:cmc-batch]. The good table flags the water result and keeps run 1 and the retest as two rows under one investigation [D:cmc-batch]. Each out-of-specification result needs a documented investigation. Ask: would your tool compare the result with the limit, or trust the flag?

    Public sources describe the world; only the company holds its own records

    • Public: ChEMBL, PubChem, UniProt, PDB, AlphaFold DB, Open Targets
    • Also: ClinicalTrials.gov, EPARs, DailyMed, FAERS, EudraVigilance, OMOP
    • Company only: registry, ELN, batch records, EDC

    Source: EMBL-EBI ChEMBL; US National Library of Medicine, NCBI PubChem; UniProt Consortium (EMBL-EBI, SIB, PIR), 2024; Ochoa et al. (Oxford University Press), 2021; US National Library of Medicine; US FDA

    Speaker notes

    FAERS (now AEMS) cannot be used to calculate how often a side effect occurs. AlphaFold DB confidence under 50 pLDDT is very low. Ask: what does your tool add that a free public website does not?

    Four gates stand between a signed deal and a live tool

    • Procurement: terms; QA: supplier assessment and written agreement
    • IT: validation for intended use, scaled to risk
    • Legal and privacy: health data and processor contracts

    Source: European Commission, 2011; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; ISPE, 2022; European Union (EUR-Lex), 2016; European Union (EUR-Lex), 2016

    Speaker notes

    Annex 11 asks for close cooperation between relevant staff such as the process owner, system owner, qualified persons and IT. In trials the sponsor stays responsible for work given to vendors. Ask: which gate has stalled a deal you worked on?

    A headline number is a claim until you find its baseline

    • Preprint: 6x at 384 residues on one V100
    • Same preprint: about 60x on short sequences
    • vLLM: 2-4x throughput at the same latency

    Source: Lin et al. (bioRxiv), 2022; Recursion (company blog); Kwon et al., 2023

    Speaker notes

    At 384 residues ESMFold takes 14.2 s, 6 times faster than one AlphaFold2 model; the 60x holds only for shorter sequences. A company blog gives only the 60, for short sequences, with no length or hardware. The published abstract says "an order-of-magnitude acceleration". vLLM compares against FasterTransformer and Orca, not the buyer's stack. Ask: what are the metric, baseline, setting and publisher of the last claim you repeated?

    Production evidence is proof; a pilot or adoption count is a signal

    • One team of 3, four months: 23.3% time saving
    • More than 70% voluntary use measures adoption only
    • Label company-reported figures as company-reported

    Source: Benaiche et al. (Bayer Healthcare SAS France), 2025; US FDA, 2025; Merck & Co., 2025

    Speaker notes

    The 23.3% saving has a 95% CI of 13.8-32.8%. First CSR drafts in 3-4 days instead of 2-3 weeks is company-reported from live submissions. Ask: which of your case studies is proof and which is a signal?

    A credible evaluation plan is fixed before the run

    • Name the question and the context of use
    • Keep test data independent; use the hold-out set once
    • Agree the baseline and acceptance level with QA

    Source: US FDA, 2025; European Medicines Agency, 2024

    Speaker notes

    The FDA framework is a draft from January 2025 with seven steps. EMA expects a new independent test set after further development. Ask: what would the buyer's QA lead need to see before signing?

    Learn

    How a medicine reaches a patient

    A medicine passes through nine stages. Discovery finds a target (the molecule in the body a drug acts on) and a candidate[1]. Preclinical work tests toxicity in the lab and in animals, under good laboratory practice (GLP) rules in 21 CFR Part 58[2]. Before the first human dose, the sponsor (the company running the trials) files an IND in the US, the filing that lets it start trials[3]. In the EU the equivalent is a clinical trial application, sent once through one portal, CTIS, since 31 January 2023[4].

    Phase I tests safety in a small group, Phase II tests whether the drug works and at what dose, and Phase III confirms the benefit in a large group[3]. The sponsor then submits a marketing application. FDA review takes 6 to 10 months once filed[5]. The EMA scientific evaluation runs up to 210 active days, and the European Commission then decides within 67 days of the opinion[6]. After approval, the product is made under current good manufacturing practice (CGMP, 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211)[7]. Pharmacovigilance (monitoring side effects after launch) continues for the product’s life[8]. Real-world data from health records, claims and registries then shows how the medicine performs in ordinary care[9].

    Most programmes stop on the way. In 9,704 programmes tracked from 2011 to 2020, 52.0% passed Phase I, 28.9% passed Phase II, 57.8% passed Phase III and 90.6% of submissions were approved[10]. Multiplied together, that is 7.9% from Phase I to approval, about 8 in 100[10]. In a review of 2010-2017 data, lack of clinical efficacy explained 40 to 50% of clinical failures[11]. For a seller, the stage tells you who has the problem. A Phase II team fears a failed efficacy readout; a plant fears a failed batch.

Units differ too: mg for a tablet, vg/kg for a gene therapy[12, 19]. A tool built for one modality’s records will not read another’s without new mapping.

    The owner of the system owns the data problem. EU GMP Annex 11 asks for close cooperation between relevant staff such as the process owner, system owner, qualified persons and IT[38]. So expect several roles on the buying side of a manufacturing sale.

    What good data means

    Five tests catch most problems. Identity: each record carries a stable id that never changes. Examples are an InChIKey for a compound, a fixed 27-character code[39]; a UniProt accession for a protein, 6 or 10 characters[40]; and an NCT number for a trial, “NCT” plus 8 digits[41]. Unit: a number means nothing without one; nano is 10 to the minus 9 and micro 10 to the minus 6, a factor of 1,000[42]. Version: each result names the version of the method that produced it, because a specification links each test to its procedure[31]. Provenance: who made the record, when, on which instrument and under which protocol; US rules require time-stamped audit trails that do not obscure earlier entries[43]. Context: the conditions of the measurement, such as the ATP concentration recorded with each kinase result in the example potency table[data].

    The first good-versus-messy table, in Example data below, shows how a right-looking number goes wrong. In row 1 of the messy potency table, a pIC50 of 7.9 sits in the IC50 column[data]. pIC50 is the negative log of the molar IC50, so 1 nM is pIC50 9[44]. The true IC50 is 12.5 nM. Nothing looks broken, yet the compound now appears more potent than it is. EMA sums up the target as ALCOA++, ten attributes: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available and traceable[25].

    Public data and company-only data

    Public sources answer questions about the outside world. ChEMBL holds bioactivity results from the literature and offers web services[45]. PubChem returns compound properties, including the InChIKey[46]. UniProt holds annotated protein sequences[47]. The Protein Data Bank gives each structure a 4-character id[48]. AlphaFold DB holds predicted structures with a 0 to 100 confidence score, pLDDT; under 50 is very low[49]. Open Targets scores target-disease links from 20 evidence sources (in its 2021 description)[50]. ClinicalTrials.gov registers trials by NCT number[41]. EPARs are EMA’s public reports on authorised medicines[12]. DailyMed shows the latest US labels submitted and in use, but not every label[51]. FAERS and EudraVigilance collect suspected side-effect reports[52, 8]. OMOP, from the OHDSI community, is a shared model for real-world data[37].

    Each has limits a careful team states. FAERS cannot be used to calculate how often a side effect occurs, and a report does not show the drug caused it[52]. A low-pLDDT region may be disordered, not a failed prediction[49].

    Only the company holds its compound registry, ELN entries, batch records, trial data in EDC and its safety cases[23, 29, 25]. The value usually sits in the join: an internal compound id linked to a public record through a shared key such as the InChIKey[39, 46]. A tool that only reads public data competes with free websites.

    Who signs: the buying process

    A regulated buyer has four gates. Procurement runs the commercial terms. Quality assurance (QA) assesses the supplier; Annex 11 expects formal agreements that state the supplier’s responsibilities[38]. In trials, written agreements with vendors come before work starts, and the sponsor stays responsible[25, 53]. The sponsor may rely on a vendor’s validation documents after assessing them, often through an audit[25].

    IT validation checks that the system is fit for its intended use, with effort scaled to risk[54, 53]. Legal and privacy review the data. Under the GDPR, health and genetic data are special categories that may be processed only under listed exceptions[55]. A vendor that processes such data needs a written processor contract[56].

    Proof, signal and a vendor claim

    Proof is evidence from production: a named setting, a baseline and a running system. A signal is a pilot, a single team or an adoption count. One study found a 23.3% time saving drafting case letters (95% CI 13.8-32.8%), in one team of 3 over about four months: a signal[57]. “More than 70% of staff used it voluntarily” measures adoption, not accuracy[58].

    Read every number for its metric, baseline, setting and publisher. ESMFold is a protein structure predictor. Its preprint reports 14.2 seconds for a 384-residue protein on one V100 GPU, 6 times faster than one AlphaFold2 model, and about 60 times on shorter sequences[59]. A company blog gives only the 60, for short sequences, with no length or hardware[60]. The published paper’s abstract says “an order-of-magnitude acceleration”[61]. The same tool gives 6 or 60 depending on sequence length. vLLM, a model-serving system, raises throughput 2 to 4 times at the same latency, against two named systems[62]. It does not promise 2 to 4 times on the buyer’s current stack.

    An AI tool on company data runs a chain: clean and join records, embed them as vectors, retrieve candidates, rerank them, generate or predict, then evaluate and validate. Retrieval that can show its source (provenance) was one motivation for retrieval-augmented generation[63]. Validation asks whether the output is credible for one context of use[64]. The advanced and expert modules take each step in turn.

    A credible evaluation plan in a proposal names the question and the context of use, then sets the test before the run[64]. Test data stays independent of development data[64]. A hold-out test set is used once[65]. On day one, ask five scoping questions: which decision changes, which system holds the record, who owns it, what the current baseline is, and who signs. A careful seller never quotes a figure without its baseline, never calls a pilot production, and never predicts a buyer’s results.

    Example data

    The potency table is the first good-versus-messy pair. Compare row 1 of each side: the same compound shows 12.5 nM in the good table and 7.9 in the messy one, because a pIC50 landed in the IC50 column[data]. Then look at row 4, where a single-concentration percent inhibition was stored as an IC50, and row 6, a second CPD-0005 result with no replicate id. The good table labels the two CPD-0005 runs r1 and r2, so a reader can tell a replicate from a duplicate[data].

    The batch release table shows what QA cares about. Each result needs a method version, a unit and a specification limit. Look at row 4 of the messy table, a water result above its limit with no out-of-specification flag, and row 5, where a failing first result is hidden behind a passing retest. The good table keeps both: it flags the water result with an investigation id, and it shows the failing first assay run and the retest as two rows under one investigation[data].

    The vendor claim register is the seller’s own data. In the good table every claim has a metric, a baseline, a setting and a publisher. In the messy table the same claims lose their baselines, a speed figure loses its sequence length and hardware, and an adoption figure turns into an accuracy figure[data].

    Try it

    1. A prospect says its Phase III programme has “about a 50-50 chance” of approval. Using the BIO 2011-2020 rates, check the figure.[10]
      AnswerPhase III success is 57.8% and submission-to-approval is 90.6%[10]. 0.578 x 0.906 = 0.524, so about 52%. The prospect’s figure is close to the published average. Say “about 52%, using the BIO 2011-2020 averages”; the rate varies by disease area, so do not apply it to their programme as a prediction.
    2. In row 1 of the messy potency table, CPD-0001 shows 7.9 in the IC50 column. Convert the pIC50 back to an IC50 and say how wrong the stored value is.[data]
      AnswerpIC50 = minus log10 of the molar IC50[44]. So IC50 = 10 to the minus 7.9 M = 1.26 x 10 to the minus 8 M = 12.6 nM, which matches the 12.5 nM in the good table after rounding[data]. The stored 7.9 reads as 7.9 nM. 12.5 / 7.9 = 1.58, so the compound looks about 1.6 times more potent than it is. The error survives because 7.9 is a plausible IC50; only the pIC50 column, left empty, gives it away.
    3. In row 4 of the messy batch table, LOT-2024-008 has water at 0.62% w/w against a limit of not more than 0.5, flagged N. By how much is it over, and what must happen?[data]
      Answer0.62 - 0.5 = 0.12 percentage points over the limit, or 0.12 / 0.5 = 24% above it[data]. Any result outside its acceptance criterion is out of specification and triggers a documented investigation[66]. The flag must be Y, as in the good table, which also records investigation INV-2024-0011[data]. A tool that reads oos_flag instead of comparing result with spec_limit would miss this lot.
    4. In row 5 of the messy batch table, the assay cell reads “101.0 (retest, first run 93.8)” against 95.0 to 105.0. What is wrong, and what should a data tool do?[data]
      AnswerThe first run, 93.8, is 95.0 - 93.8 = 1.2 points below the lower limit[data]. A retest cannot replace the original result without a documented laboratory error[66]. The cell also mixes two numbers and a note. A data tool should keep both results as separate rows with their run order and flag the first as out of specification; it must never “clean” the cell down to 101.0. The good table shows the target: run 1 at 93.8 flagged Y, and the retest at 101.0 flagged N, both under investigation INV-2024-0012[data].
    5. Row VC-002 of the messy claim register says “60x faster than AlphaFold2”, published by “the paper”. Correct it, and compute the AlphaFold2 time the preprint implies.[data]
      AnswerThe 60x is real, but it holds only for shorter sequences and for the inference step[59]. At 384 residues the preprint reports 14.2 s, 6 times faster than one AlphaFold2 model, so AlphaFold2 takes about 14.2 x 6 = 85.2 s[59]. Quoting 60x for a 384-residue protein overstates the gain 60 / 6 = 10 times. The messy row’s real defect is the blank setting[data]. Record “6x at 384 residues, one V100, one AlphaFold2 model, preprint; about 60x on shorter sequences”. If the figure reached you through a blog, name the blog as a secondary source[60].
    6. Classify rows VC-003, VC-004 and VC-005 of the good claim register as proof, signal or neither, and give the width of the VC-004 confidence interval.[data]
      AnswerVC-003 (first CSR drafts in 3-4 days instead of 2-3 weeks) is company-reported from live submissions: production evidence, labelled company-reported[data][67]. VC-004 is a signal: one team of 3, about four months; the interval runs 32.8 - 13.8 = 19.0 percentage points wide[57]. VC-005 is neither: it counts users, not outcomes[58].

    Check yourself

    1. Which public source finds a trial by an id such as “NCT” plus 8 digits? a) ChEMBL b) ClinicalTrials.gov c) UniProt d) FAERS
    2. A count of FAERS reports for a drug tells you: a) how often the side effect occurs b) that the drug caused it c) how many reports were received; it cannot give an incidence rate d) whether the drug is approved
    3. Which system holds the executed batch record? a) EDC b) CTMS c) MES d) CRM
