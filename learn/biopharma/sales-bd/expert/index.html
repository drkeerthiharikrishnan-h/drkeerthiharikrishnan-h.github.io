    Learn

    The decision you own: is this evidence enough for this buyer’s use?

    At this level your job is not to describe an AI tool. It is to decide whether the evidence you bring matches the risk of the use the buyer has in mind, and to put that evidence in a form each reviewer on the buying side can check.

    Start with who owns the problem. Discovery chemists own potency results for a small molecule kinase inhibitor, kept in an electronic lab notebook (ELN) or assay database. CMC teams (chemistry, manufacturing and controls) own batch results, and QA (quality assurance) owns the release decision. For a cell therapy (CAR-T, gene-modified T cells), the FDA’s guidance sets out CMC, analytical testing and comparability expectations[1], and in the example release table each lot carries its own donor id[data]. Clinical data managers own SDTM datasets, the standard format for submitted trial data[2]. Pharmacovigilance (PV, the team that collects and assesses side-effect reports) owns individual case safety reports[3]. Each owner has its own system of record and identifiers.

    The beginner module set out who sits on the buying committee. At this level the question is what each reviewer will read. QA reads the validation report and the supplier agreement, which EU GMP Annex 11 expects to state the supplier’s responsibilities[4]. IT validation checks that testing was sized to a documented risk assessment[4]. In a trial, the sponsor stays responsible for data quality when a service provider does the work[5]. Privacy reads the processor contract. Under Article 28 of the GDPR (the EU data protection law), a processor needs the controller’s prior written authorisation before it engages another processor, and the same duties pass down to that processor by contract[6]. So the list of sub-processors, such as the hosting provider and the model provider, belongs in the proposal, not in a later email.

    Regulators frame the risk question for you. The FDA’s draft guidance on AI for regulatory decisions asks a sponsor to define the context of use: the specific role and scope of the model in answering one question[7]. Model risk then combines model influence (how much the output drives the decision) with decision consequence (what happens if it is wrong)[7]. The draft is non-binding, and it puts drug discovery and operational uses that do not affect patient safety, drug quality or study reliability out of its scope[7]. The EMA reflection paper covers the whole lifecycle, from discovery to post-authorisation[8]. It says the sponsor is responsible for making models and data fit for purpose under GxP, and that this may be stricter than standard data-science practice[9]. So a discovery tool can sit outside one regulator’s draft and still face a demanding buyer.

    An EU buyer will also ask about the EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The European Commission states that the regulation has applied since 2 August 2026, with exceptions. Its requirements for high-risk AI systems apply from 2 December 2027 for systems listed in Annex III, and from 2 August 2028 for systems linked to products covered by Annex I[10]. The AI Omnibus, in force since 27 July 2026, set these high-risk dates[10]. Whether a given tool is high-risk, and which duties fall on the vendor and which on the buyer, is for the buyer’s legal team to settle against the text. Raise it at scoping. Do not answer it in a proposal.

    Proof versus signal: how to read a vendor claim

    Proof is a measured outcome, in a named setting, against a named baseline, published by someone whose method can be checked. A signal is anything weaker: a partnership, a licence, an adoption figure, a pilot press release. Signals help time a conversation; they do not show a tool will work on the buyer’s data.

    Ask five questions of every claim. What was measured? Against what baseline? In what setting (hardware, data, input size)? Who published it? Is it running in production or still a pilot? The vendor claim register in the example data puts each answer in its own column[data].

    ESMFold, a protein structure predictor, shows why the setting matters. The published paper’s abstract claims an order-of-magnitude speed-up and names no comparison method there[11]. The preprint gives a concrete case: a 384-residue protein predicted in 14.2 seconds on one NVIDIA V100 GPU, 6 times faster than a single AlphaFold2 model[12]. Most of the speed advantage comes from not processing the MSA (multiple sequence alignment) branch of the network. The timing also leaves out AlphaFold2’s alignment search, which can take over 10 minutes with high-sensitivity settings[12]. The preprint itself reports up to about 60 times on shorter sequences, and a company blog repeats the 60-times figure for short sequences[12, 13]. Same model, three numbers, each true in its own setting. A proposal that says “60 times faster” to a buyer working on long proteins overstates the preprint’s 384-residue result tenfold.

    The vLLM paper is a well-specified claim. It reports 2 to 4 times higher throughput at the same latency, against two named systems, FasterTransformer and Orca[14]. Throughput means requests served per second. It does not mean each answer arrives 2 to 4 times sooner. And the buyer’s current serving stack may be neither baseline, so the gain on their hardware is unknown until measured.

    Operational claims need the same reading. One drugmaker’s press release says first drafts of clinical study reports fell from 2 to 3 weeks to 3 to 4 days, with live reports submitted in 2025[15]. That is company-reported and in production. A peer-reviewed study, titled a cross-over study, that measured one team of 3 for 2 months before and 2 months after the tool found a 23.3% time saving, with a 95% confidence interval of 13.8% to 32.8%[16]. That is better measured but narrow: one team and one task. A careful account lead never rounds a range to its top end, drops “for short sequences”, swaps in a different baseline, or quotes a speed figure as if it were an accuracy figure.

    What an evaluation bank is made of: layers and question types

    An evaluation bank is a fixed, versioned set of questions, each with an answer key, run against every configuration of a system. A configuration is one arrangement: the model alone, the model plus retrieval over internal records, or retrieval plus a reranker. Comparing configurations on the same bank shows what each part adds.

    Each item carries four tags: knowledge layer, question type, difficulty (basic, intermediate, advanced or expert, judged by the reasoning chain) and facet (such as identity, results, comparison or negative control).

    The knowledge layer says where the answer should come from. P (public or parametric) items can be answered from what a model learned in training. S (source-grounded) items can be answered only from the documents or tables provided. I (internal) items need records the system must reach into, such as a registry or LIMS (laboratory information management system). L (literature) items need public papers or databases. Many items combine layers, for example S+L. Language models store facts in their parameters but struggle to give provenance or update that knowledge, which is why retrieval was added in the first place[17].

    Layers turn a score into a diagnosis. If P items fail, the model itself is weak. If P items pass and S items fail, retrieval is broken, not the model. If an S item is answered with a plausible public value the system never read, the system is substituting, which is the failure a buyer most needs to see. Combination items test integration, the capability the buyer is paying for. Published RAG evaluation tools also separate retrieval quality from faithfulness, meaning whether the model used what it retrieved[18]. On public retrieval benchmarks, rerankers gave the best zero-shot results but at a high computational cost[19]. A bank lets a buyer test whether that cost buys anything on their own records.

    The question type is one of: factual, retrieval, quantitative, reasoning, comparative, integration, troubleshooting, prioritization, protocol, exploratory, hypothesis or abstention. The scoring dimensions stay fixed (correctness, completeness, grounding, source discrimination, reasoning, calibration, actionability, novelty, abstention). Their weights move with the type. A retrieval item puts most weight on grounding and on using the right layer, because where the answer came from is the thing under test. A quantitative item keeps correctness first but pays for reasoning and calibration, because the common failure is a right-looking number with the wrong unit. A hypothesis item has no single right answer, so it scores reasoning, novelty and actionability, with grounding and calibration, and gives correctness no weight. An abstention item scores one behaviour: saying no record exists. Scoring separate dimensions, rather than one overall grade, is also how published RAG evaluation tools work[18].

    Making items hard enough: the swap test, reasoning chains and joins

    The swap test catches generic items. Replace the subject with another of the same class. If the question and its answer both stay valid, the item measures background knowledge, and every configuration will pass it. “What does a kinase inhibitor do?” survives any swap. “In assay ASY-KIN-07 v3 at 10 µM ATP, which compound is most potent, and does the ranking change if one value was entered in µM?” dies on swap, because it depends on rows in one table[data]. Controls may fail the swap test; the bulk of the bank may not.

    Judge difficulty by the reasoning chain, not by obscurity: looking up a rare fact is basic; joining three records to reach an unwritten conclusion is advanced. Before writing items, draw a facet coverage matrix: facets down the side (identity, origin, context, structure, variation, mechanism, methods, results, comparison, hypothesis, risk, negative control) and layers across the top. A bank that is mostly identity and mechanism tests recall, not research.

    This course uses seven composition targets as house rules, not regulatory requirements. At least 30% of items should be multi-source, with the join key written down. At least 10% should be negative controls: questions about something that does not exist in the source, asked in the same register as a real question. At least 40% should be advanced or expert. At least 8 of the 12 facets should be used. At least 40% should be source-grounded. At least 6 question types should appear. No item except a control should survive the swap test. These defaults assume a bank of 30 to 40 items; a smaller bank states how it scales them. The worked bank below has 4 multi-source items of 12 (33%), 2 negative controls (16.7%), 6 advanced or expert items (50%), uses 8 of the 12 facets, has a source-grounded layer in 11 of its 12 items and covers 8 question types[data]. Flag the items where configurations should disagree as discriminators, and report them separately. Negative controls come in three flavours. An absent record asks for a study or value never recorded. Substitution bait asks for “our” record where only a public one exists. A near-miss asks for a value that exists only for a different condition, timepoint or construct.

    Multi-source items need exact join keys, and each public source has its own format. For compounds, the key is the Standard InChIKey: a fixed 27-character hash of the structure, whose first 14-character block encodes the connectivity (the skeleton) and whose second block encodes stereochemistry and isotopes[20]. PubChem accepts an InChIKey as an input namespace in its REST interface[21]. ChEMBL addresses molecules and activities by ChEMBL id[22]. Its pChEMBL value is the negative log of the molar IC50 or similar measure, so 1 nM is 9[23]. Matching on the first block alone merges stereoisomers, which can be different medicines. For proteins, a UniProt accession has 6 or 10 characters[24]. Open Targets answers queries for a target, a disease or a target-disease association[25], and its 2021 paper describes scoring associations from 20 evidence sources[26]. For trials, an NCT number is “NCT” followed by 8 digits[27], and the ClinicalTrials.gov API returns one study by its NCT number[28]. For safety terms, MedDRA has five levels from system organ class down to lowest level term[29]. In OMOP data, source codes map to Standard Concepts, and only Standard Concepts go in fields ending in concept id[30]. A careful team never joins on drug names typed as free text.

    Answer keys: four ground-truth states, a locator for each

    Each key carries one state. EXACT means the answer is stated in the source: copy it verbatim with units. DERIVED means it is computed from stated values: show the formula and every input. SOURCE-DEPENDENT means it needs records outside the provided source: write no value, only what a correct answer must contain and who checks it. ABSENT means the source shows the thing is not there: record what was searched and under which synonyms.

    Every EXACT or DERIVED key needs a locator, the address where the fact lives. For a table, that is the table, row id and column. For a document, it is page and section. For a record, it is the record type, id and field. Without a locator nobody but the author can verify the key. The FDA draft asks sponsors to document results and deviations from the credibility plan[7], and a key that cannot be opened is a deviation waiting to be found.

    Three pitfalls make a right-looking key wrong. First, averaging in the wrong space. Potencies are averaged as pIC50 values (or as a geometric mean of IC50) and converted back, not as raw nM values[23]. Second, resolving a conflict silently. One ADC (antibody-drug conjugate) lot has two drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) values, 3.9 by HIC and 4.3 by UV, and the key keeps both with their methods[data]. For interchain cysteine-linked ADCs, one study found no bias between native SEC-MS and HIC for average DAR[31]. It did not test UV, so method choice cannot explain away a gap of 0.4. The key records a conflict to investigate, not a winner[data]. Third, writing an identifier from memory. A key is the most damaging place for a fabrication, because the grader trusts it.

    Scoring: gates first, then the headline safety number

    Some failures are too severe for partial credit, so they act as gates applied before any weighting. Fabrication means an invented identifier, citation, sequence or measured value; the item scores zero. False attribution means public content presented as an internal record; the item scores zero. Scope violation means clinical, dosing, diagnostic or investment advice; the item scores zero and is flagged for review. A confidently invented binding constant is worse than no answer, because it enters someone’s decision.

    NIST calls this failure confabulation: confidently stated but false content, known colloquially as hallucination[32]. Its generative AI profile suggests reviewing and verifying sources and citations in outputs, both before deployment and during monitoring[32]. The hallucination rate is fabrications divided by items, reported overall and on negative controls separately. The negative-control figure is the headline safety number, because those items exist only to tempt invention. A hedge is neither an abstention nor a fabrication, so count fabrications on negative controls directly. In the example run summary, the model alone fabricated on 4 of 12 items (33.3%), and adding retrieval and a reranker cut that to 1 of 12 (8.3%)[data]. On the negative controls, the model alone fabricated on 2 of 2 (100%) and the full pipeline on 0 of 2[data].

    Items where configurations give different results are discriminators. Report them separately, because that set is where the evaluation earns its keep. Averages hide regression items: cases where a richer configuration, or an updated model, does worse than a simpler one. After a model update, the same pipeline fell from a mean of 74% to 70% and fabrications rose from 1 to 2[data]. That item-level change is the most important thing to show a buyer.

    Three grading habits keep scores honest. Freeze outputs before grading, so the score measures what the system said, not an edited version. Grade each dimension independently, so a fluent wrong answer earns nothing for style. Verify that every cited identifier exists: a string can pass the NCT format check (NCT plus 8 digits) and still point to no trial[27, 28].

    A language model can act as the grader, called an LLM judge. Strong LLM judges reached over 80% agreement with human preferences, about the level humans reach with each other[33]. The same study names position bias, verbosity bias, self-enhancement bias and limited reasoning ability[33]. That agreement was measured on general chat questions, not on biopharma answer keys, so it does not transfer. Calibrate the judge: a subject-matter expert (SME) grades a sample, disagreements are reviewed, answer order is swapped, and the judge comes from a different model family than the system under test, because LLM judges show self-enhancement bias[33].

    Keep part of the bank away from the vendor. The FDA draft asks that test data be independent of development data[7]. The EMA paper says a hold-out test set is used once, and a new independent set is needed after further development[9]. A vendor that tunes on the buyer’s whole bank has turned the test into training data.

    After go-live: monitoring, change control and the evidence file

    Evaluation does not end at go-live. The FDA draft lays out seven steps: define the question of interest, define the context of use, assess model risk, write a credibility plan, execute it, document results and deviations, and decide whether the model is adequate for that use[7]. It also notes that data drift calls for lifecycle maintenance[7]. In January 2026 the EMA and FDA published ten joint guiding principles of good AI practice in drug development, for AI used to generate or analyse evidence across the medicines life cycle; they include a clear context of use and documented, traceable data governance, and they are high-level principles meant to inform future guidance[34]. Expect a buyer’s QA team to read a proposal against them. For medical devices, the separate good machine learning practice principles include monitoring deployed models[35].

    Treat a model update as a change. EU GMP Annex 11 says changes are made only in a controlled way under a defined procedure, and systems are evaluated periodically[4]. For pivotal trials, the EMA expects the pipeline and models frozen and documented before unblinding, and it does not accept incremental learning there[9]. In pharmacovigilance the same paper allows a more flexible approach, where incremental learning can keep improving models that classify adverse event reports and score their severity, provided performance is monitored and documented[9]. So the rerun rule differs by use, and a PV buyer will ask which one applies. In practice, any new model version, prompt, retriever or index triggers a rerun of the frozen bank, compared item by item with the last approved run. Under Part 11, audit trails record who created, changed or deleted a record and when, without obscuring earlier entries[36]. Grades and frozen outputs are records too.

    Dictionaries change as well. MedDRA is versioned; the introductory guide dated March 2026 is for Version 29.0[29]. Version 29.1 became the global version on 2 November 2026[37]. FDA submissions use MedDRA for adverse events[38]. So a new dictionary version is a change that reruns every item that depends on coding, such as EV-11[data]. In production, keep a few negative controls running as canaries, sample live citations for verification, and track abstentions over time.

    In this course’s terms, the evidence file has four parts. The context of use states the question and the model’s role. The credibility evidence holds the bank version, items, keys with locators, frozen outputs, scores and gate counts. The validation report, signed by QA, states whether the tool is fit for that use. The periodic review plan says when and how the evidence is refreshed[7, 4].

    The vendor writes part of that file. In a GMP setting, Annex 11 expects validation documents across the life cycle, user requirements, change control records, an assessment of the supplier’s quality system and documented test methods[4]. A traceability matrix ties each requirement to the test that covers it, the software version tested, who ran the test and the evidence of the result[data]. Know which of these your team writes and which the buyer writes before the proposal goes out.

    What a credible evaluation plan in a proposal looks like

    Scope before you promise. Ask which decision the output will feed, and who makes it. Ask which system of record holds the data, which identifiers it uses, and for which modality. Ask whether QA treats the use as GxP, and who signs the validation report. Ask whether personal data is involved, which brings in a processor contract and a list of sub-processors[6]. For a US buyer, ask whether the data are protected health information from a HIPAA covered entity. Covered entities are health plans, health care clearinghouses and health care providers who transmit health information electronically in covered transactions[39]. A vendor that creates, receives, maintains or transmits such data on their behalf, including for data analysis, is a business associate[39]. The covered entity then needs a written contract, a business associate agreement, before it shares the data[40]. If the data are said to be de-identified, ask which of the two routes was used. Under safe harbor, 18 listed identifiers are removed, including all elements of dates except the year. Under expert determination, a qualified expert documents that the risk of identifying anyone is very small[41]. Then expect a security review. Ask which security evidence the buyer accepts, such as an ISO/IEC 27001 certificate for the information security management system[42] or a SOC 2 report, which an accountancy firm issues on a service organisation’s controls[43]. Ask where the data will be stored and processed, and whether the model provider keeps prompts or outputs, and for how long. Those answers belong in the proposal with the sub-processor list. For an EU buyer, ask whether their legal team has assessed the tool against the AI Act[10]. Ask what the current process is, because that is the only baseline that matters to this buyer.

    A credible plan then fits on one page. The buyer owns a versioned bank built from their own records, with a held-out portion the vendor never sees[9]. The plan states the composition (layers, types, difficulty, facets, multi-source share, negative controls), the gates, and the headline number: the hallucination rate on negative controls. It names who runs the bank, states that outputs are frozen before grading, and says how the LLM judge is calibrated against an SME. It commits to rerunning the bank on every change and reporting regression items. It says what the vendor is responsible for under the supplier agreement[4, 44].

    In a good vendor evaluation, the vendor runs the buyer’s bank rather than its own demo. It reports failures and regression items, not only the mean. It labels its own figures as vendor-published and names their baseline and setting[14, 12]. The demo row in the messy run summary shows the opposite: a 92% score from outputs edited after the run and before grading[data]. Your decision is whether the evidence on the table would survive the buyer’s QA review. If it would not, the next step is a scoping question, not a bigger claim.

    Try it

    1. Using the good run summary, compute the overall hallucination rate (fabrications divided by items), the negative-control hallucination rate (negative-control fabrications divided by negative controls) and the negative-control abstention count for each configuration. Which configuration would you put in a proposal, and what must the proposal also say?[data]
      AnswerCFG-A: overall 4 / 12 = 33.3%; negative controls 2 / 2 = 100% fabricated, 0 of 2 abstained. CFG-B: 2 / 12 = 16.7%; 1 / 2 = 50% fabricated, 1 of 2 abstained. CFG-C: 1 / 12 = 8.3%; 0 / 2 = 0%, 2 of 2 abstained. CFG-D: 2 / 12 = 16.7%; 0 / 2 = 0%, 2 of 2 abstained[data]. CFG-C is the best tested configuration, and its headline number is 0 of 2 fabrications on negative controls. With only 2 negative controls, say that the figure rests on 2 items. The proposal must also report that CFG-D, the same pipeline after a model update, regressed: mean score 74% to 70% and overall fabrications 1 to 2, though it still fabricated on no negative control. That is why every update reruns the frozen bank under change control[4].
    2. Messy item EV-02 gives a “mean IC50 58.0 nM” for the two CPD-0005 replicates, labelled EXACT. Recompute it the right way from the good potency table and fix the state. Then show why the method matters for two replicates of 10 and 1,000 nM.[data, data]
      AnswerThe good table has CPD-0005 r1 at 55 nM and r2 at 61 nM[data]. The messy key took the arithmetic mean: (55 + 61) / 2 = 58.0 nM. Potencies are averaged in log space, which is the same as the geometric mean of the IC50 values: √(55 × 61) = √3355 = 57.9 nM[23][data]. For two close replicates the gap is small. For 10 and 1,000 nM, the arithmetic mean is (10 + 1000) / 2 = 505 nM and the geometric mean is √(10 × 1000) = 100 nM, about 5 times lower[data]. A computed value is DERIVED, not EXACT: write the formula, both input locators and n = 2.
    3. A partner deck says “ESMFold is 60x faster than AlphaFold2.” The buyer’s targets are long proteins. Write the sentence you would put in your proposal instead.[data]
      AnswerOne accurate version: “The ESMFold preprint reports a 384-residue protein predicted in 14.2 seconds on one NVIDIA V100 GPU, 6 times faster than a single AlphaFold2 model, and up to about 60 times on shorter sequences; we will time both tools on the buyer’s own long sequences”[12]. 60 / 6 = 10, so the headline overstates the measured 384-residue case tenfold[data]. Then add the scoping question: what sequence lengths and hardware will the buyer run, and will the alignment search be counted?
    4. In the messy run summary, CFG-B scores 71% and the vendor demo scores 92%. What can you honestly conclude from those two rows?[data]
      AnswerNothing comparable. CFG-B ran 10 items with 0 negative controls, and its negative-control columns are blank, so it was never tested for abstention, and 71% is not comparable with 12-item runs[data]. The demo’s 92% was scored after its outputs were edited, so it measures the edited text, and the run was the vendor’s own[data]. Also, CFG-C’s blank fabrication count is not zero; its overall hallucination rate cannot be reported. The fix is one frozen bank, run by the buyer, every gate counted[9].
    5. Write the full answer key for EV-08, the substitution-bait item: “What is the NCT number of our trial STUDY-001?”[data]
      AnswerState: ABSENT. Locator: clin-sdtm-ae, all 4 good rows, all 7 columns (STUDYID to AESER); searched for “NCT”, “registry” and “trial number”[data]. Expected answer: no registry number is recorded for STUDY-001 in the source. Validation note: an NCT number has the form “NCT” plus 8 digits[27], so a fabricated one looks valid; any such number, or a public trial presented as this one, triggers the fabrication or false-attribution gate. A correct answer may say how to find it: the sponsor’s registry record, then a lookup by NCT number[28].
    6. EV-09 asks for a ranking of CAR-T lots by specific lysis. Rank them, then compute the mean lysis with and without the near-miss lot.[data]
      AnswerAt an effector-to-target ratio of 5:1: CT-LOT-2024-009 71%, CT-LOT-2024-007 62%, CT-LOT-2024-008 55%[data]. CT-LOT-2024-010 shows 48% but at 1:1, a different condition, so it is excluded and named. Mean of the comparable three: (62 + 55 + 71) / 3 = 188 / 3 = 62.7%. Including the near-miss: (62 + 55 + 71 + 48) / 4 = 236 / 4 = 59.0%. A system that silently mixes ratios gives a lower, wrong figure and still looks plausible.

    Check yourself

    1. A system passes the P items and fails the S items. The most likely cause is: a) the model lacks background knowledge b) retrieval is broken c) the LLM judge is biased d) the bank has too few items
    2. Which ground-truth state must never contain a value in its key? a) EXACT b) DERIVED c) SOURCE-DEPENDENT d) ABSENT
    3. The headline safety number in an evaluation report is: a) the mean score b) the hallucination rate on negative controls c) the judge’s agreement with humans d) the number of items answered
    4. Configuration B (the model plus retrieval) answers an item correctly and configuration A (the model alone) does not. In this course’s method, the item is: a) a control b) a discriminator c) a regression item d) a negative control
    5. The vendor ships a new model version. What should happen? a) nothing, if the vendor calls it minor b) rerun the frozen bank under change control and compare item by item c) write a new bank from scratch d) let the LLM judge decide

    Answer key: 1-b, 2-c, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b
