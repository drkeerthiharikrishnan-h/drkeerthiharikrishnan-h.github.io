Which level fits you?

Three quick questions. Answer honestly; nothing is recorded.

  1. Can you name the main stages a medicine goes through before approval?
  2. Do you know which systems and data formats each department works with?
  3. Have you built, delivered or assessed an AI system on regulated life-science data?

Biopharma for Sales, BD and account leads

Map · Machine · Frontier

Most drugs fail in Phase II, so evidence of efficacy is what buyers pay for

  • Of 100 drugs that start Phase I, about 8 are approved
  • Phase II is the biggest drop: 28.9% move on
  • Phase I to approval takes 10.5 years on average

Source: BIO, Informa Pharma Intelligence and QLS Advisors, 2021

Speaker notes

A 2011 review puts idea to launch at 12 to 15 years and over $1 billion. The clinical numbers come from 9,704 programmes tracked from 2011 to 2020. A client's pipeline is a portfolio in which most projects stop. Ask first which stage a buyer works in.

Drugs fail on efficacy and safety, so question any promise of higher success

  • Lack of efficacy: 40 to 50% of failures
  • Unmanageable toxicity: about 30%
  • Commercial and strategy reasons: about 10%

Source: Sun, Gao, Hu and Zhou, 2022

Speaker notes

These shares come from a 2022 review of clinical failures in 2010 to 2017. Poor drug-like properties explain another 10 to 15%. If a pitch promises higher success rates, ask for the evidence behind it.

Late trials are large and long, so that is where the money goes

  • Phase I: 20 to 100 people, 2.3 years on average
  • Phase III: 300 to 3,000 people, 3.3 years
  • Phase III is the largest share of clinical cost

Source: US FDA, 2018; BIO, Informa Pharma Intelligence and QLS Advisors, 2021; Sertkaya et al., 2024

Speaker notes

Per candidate, out-of-pocket spend averaged $7.1 million in Phase 1, $21.0 million in Phase 2 and $89.3 million in Phase 3. With failures and the cost of capital, one new drug cost $879.3 million in that 2024 study; other estimates run over $1 billion. Durations differ by disease: cardiovascular Phase III averages 4.2 years. When you quote a cost, name the source and say whether it includes failures.

Approval runs through one regulatory file, so filings set buyers' deadlines

  • IND filed before the first trial in people
  • NDA or BLA sent in eCTD; review: 6 to 10 months
  • Priority Review: FDA aims to act within 6 months

Source: US FDA, 2018; US FDA, 2018; US FDA, 2026; US FDA, 2023

Speaker notes

The NDA holds all preclinical and clinical data, the proposed label and safety updates. Clinical study reports go in Module 5 of the five-module Common Technical Document. The FDA has four faster routes for serious conditions, including Fast Track and Breakthrough Therapy. Ask a client which upcoming filing sets their deadlines.

Each stage runs its own systems, so the buyer is whoever owns the problem

  • Lab: LIMS and ELN; trials: EDC, CTMS, eTMF
  • Manufacturing: MES and ERP, at ISA-95 Levels 3 and 4
  • Data reaches regulators in SEND, SDTM, ADaM, E2B(R3)

Source: Raviteja and Gupta, JSS College of Pharmacy, 2013; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; ISA; CDISC, 2021; CDISC, 2024; CDISC, 2021; European Medicines Agency, 2020

Speaker notes

LIMS is a laboratory information management system; EDC is electronic data collection. Market access buys too: since 12 January 2025, new EU cancer medicines and advanced therapies get a joint clinical assessment. Ask what a new tool reads from and writes to on this map. Then ask who owns the problem and who owns the budget.

Regulated buyers must validate and assess suppliers, so price that work in

  • GMP systems validated; effort set by documented risk assessment
  • Supplier responsibilities go in a formal written agreement
  • Changes only under a defined procedure, before use

Source: European Commission, 2011; ISPE, 2022

Speaker notes

Annex 11 says risk decides whether a supplier is audited, and counts a company's own IT department as a supplier. In trials, EMA says a vendor that refuses audits or key documents should not be used. GAMP 5, second edition 2022, is the industry's risk-based method. Ask the client how long their last validation took.

AI in production: named users, with oversight where sources show it

  • Merck: report first drafts in 3-4 days, not 2-3 weeks
  • Signal only: a Bayer study, one team of 3
  • FDA: more than 70% of staff used Elsa voluntarily

Source: Merck & Co., 2025; Benaiche et al. (Bayer Healthcare SAS France), 2025; US FDA, 2025

Speaker notes

Merck's platform is designed to operate with oversight by qualified medical writers. The Bayer study (a signal, not yet production) covered one French drug-safety team of 3 over about four months; the 95% confidence interval was 13.8% to 32.8%. The FDA figure is internal data; Novo Nordisk and Sanofi figures are vendor-published. When you cite a case to a client, say who published it.

Announcements are signals, not results, so ask for evidence first

  • Merck and Google Cloud: agentic AI platform, "up to $1 billion"
  • Chai Discovery: 2026 deals with Lilly, Pfizer, Novartis, others
  • Ask: faster than what, measured how, published by whom?

Source: Merck & Co., 2026; Chai Discovery; Kwon et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2023

Speaker notes

Signal, not yet shown in production: these are announcements, not proof of routine use. IQVIA and Veeva also launched AI products in 2026. The vLLM paper names its baselines; the ESMFold abstract does not. Ask the room which of the two claims they could check.

Biopharma on one page, for Sales, BD and account leads

The chain, ten terms, five questions, cases.

The chain in one line

Discovery → preclinical research → clinical research → FDA review → post-market safety monitoring[1]. Idea to launch can take 12 to 15 years and over $1 billion[2]. About 8 of every 100 drugs that start Phase I are approved[3]. Phase 3 is the largest share of clinical cost[4].

Ten terms

  • IND: the filing that lets a company start trials in people[5].
  • NDA / BLA: New Drug Application or Biologics License Application, the request for approval with all data and the proposed label[6, 7]. FDA review takes 6 to 10 months[6].
  • eCTD: the electronic Common Technical Document, the five-module submission sent electronically[8, 7].
  • GxP: shorthand for the good-practice rules for labs, trials and manufacturing (GLP, GCP, GMP)[9, 10, 11].
  • Part 11: US rule for electronic records and signatures kept under FDA rules[12].
  • Annex 11: EU rule that computer systems used in manufacturing (GMP) must be validated[13].
  • Supplier agreement: a formal agreement stating what the supplier is responsible for (Annex 11, 3.1)[13].
  • Change control: system changes made only under a defined procedure (Annex 11, 10)[13].
  • ICSR: individual case safety report, one report per patient case, in E2B(R3) format in the EU[14].
  • MLR review: medical, legal and regulatory review. Veeva describes it as checking promotional and medical materials against approved labels[15]. Under the EFPIA Code, a self-regulatory industry code, a doctor or pharmacist approves and certifies each promotional piece[16].

Five questions to ask

  1. Which department owns this problem, and which systems does the answer have to fit?
  2. Is the data or decision covered by GxP rules: Part 11, Annex 11, or both?
  3. What will your quality team need from us before go-live: agreement, audit, validation documents?
  4. How is each new release tested and approved before your staff use it?
  5. If AI is involved, who checks the output, and where is the evidence published?

Cases and one signal

  • Merck & Co. drafts clinical study reports with generative AI. The platform is designed to operate with oversight by qualified medical writers. First drafts take 3 to 4 days instead of 2 to 3 weeks (company-reported)[17].
  • Signal, not yet shown in production: Bayer. A peer-reviewed study measured a 23.3% time saving when one French drug-safety team drafted case documentation letters with its internal AI tool. The study ran for about four months. The team kept using the tool afterwards[18].
  • US FDA staff use Elsa, an internal generative AI tool. More than 70% of staff had used it voluntarily, per FDA internal data in a December 2025 release[19].

Deals and launches are signals, not yet shown in production. One example: Chai Discovery’s 2026 deals with six drug companies, one of them a licence[20].
