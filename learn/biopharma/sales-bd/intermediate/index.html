    Sales, BD and account leads, level 2 of 4: identifiers, units, pitfalls and joins

    An IC50 belongs to the compound and the assay together

    • IC50 rises with substrate for a competitive inhibitor
    • Ki = IC50 / (1 + S/Km) removes that dependence
    • Summarise replicates, then average on the pIC50 scale

    Source: Kalliokoski, Kramer, Vulpetti and Gedeck (PLOS ONE), 2013; EMBL-EBI ChEMBL

    Speaker notes

    With CPD-0005's two replicates summarised as 57.9 nM, the five compounds average 430.7 nM, but their mean pIC50 of 7.153 is 70.3 nM [D:sm-potency]. ChEMBL keeps "greater than" values as a symbol plus a value and computes pChEMBL only when the relation is "=". Ask the room: which ATP concentration did the client's assay use?

    A biologic's number is meaningless without its method

    • KD must equal koff / kon; check it
    • DAR carries its method; one is the release assay
    • Plasma stability needs species and timepoint

    Source: mAbs (Taylor & Francis), 2023; mAbs (Taylor & Francis), 2020; ACS Omega (American Chemical Society, open access), 2017

    Speaker notes

    In the messy binding table, AB-0104 gives 6.0E-04 / 1.2E+05 = 5.0 nM against a reported 0.5 nM [D:ab-binding]. For interchain cysteine-linked ADCs, one study found no bias between native SEC-MS and HIC for average DAR, so the defect to find is a DAR with no recorded method. Ask: which DAR method does the client report on the certificate?

    In cell therapy, every percentage needs its denominator

    • % lysis needs the effector-to-target ratio
    • VCN is reported per CAR-positive cell
    • MFI is in arbitrary units; beads give antibodies per cell

    Source: US FDA (CBER), 2024; Poncelet, Lecompte, Bauters and Mullier (MDPI IJMS), 2026

    Speaker notes

    CT-LOT-2024-007 shows 62% lysis at 5:1 and CT-LOT-2024-010 shows 48% at 1:1, so they cannot be ranked [D:cart-release]. For CT-LOT-2024-009, 3.1 / 0.520 = 5.96 copies per CAR-positive cell [D:cart-release]. Ask: does the client's potency assay record E:T and timepoint in the same row?

    A titre or a lot comparison carries its method and its margin

    • AAV titre is vg/mL; dose in vg/kg is a separate record
    • A qPCR titre needs its reference standard
    • FDA influenza guidance: each lot-pair 95% interval within 0.67 to 1.5

    Source: CADTH (NCBI Bookshelf); Frontiers in Microbiology, 2019; US FDA (CBER)

    Speaker notes

    In one study qPCR titres averaged 64 times ddPCR; correcting the qPCR standard cut the gap to 5.5 times. In the vaccine table, Strain 2, Lot A / Lot C has a ratio of 1.24 but an upper limit of 1.53, so it fails [D:vaccine-lot-consistency]. For an mRNA vaccine, release and shelf-life criteria differ only for RNA integrity, so every integrity result needs its test point [D:rna-lnp-stability]. Ask: which titre method is named in the client's specification?

    Trial data follows one path, joined by USUBJID

    • CDASH collects, SDTM tabulates, ADaM analyses
    • Define-XML describes every dataset in a submission
    • An improper USUBJID often forces resubmission

    Source: CDISC, 2023; CDISC, 2024; CDISC, 2021; CDISC; US FDA (CDER, CBER), 2026

    Speaker notes

    The FDA wants SAS XPORT version 5 files and USUBJID identical across SDTM and ADaM. SDTM model version 2.1 is required by the FDA and PMDA. Ask: who in the client's biostatistics group owns the define.xml?

    A safety case is valid only with four elements

    • Reporter, patient, suspect reaction, suspect medicine
    • Code reactions in MedDRA and keep the version
    • E2B(R3) format mandatory in the EU since June 2022

    Source: European Medicines Agency and Heads of Medicines Agencies, 2017; MedDRA MSSO (ICH), 2026; European Medicines Agency, 2020

    Speaker notes

    In the EU, serious cases are due within 15 days of receipt, non-serious within 90. Other regions set their own clocks. MedDRA has five levels and 27 system organ classes; version 29.1 became the global version on 2 November 2026. Ask: which MedDRA version is the client's safety database on today?

    Real-world and regulatory data each have one common model

    • OMOP: concept id 0 means no concept was assigned
    • Local codes get custom concepts above 2 billion
    • Submissions use the five-module CTD, sent as eCTD

    Source: OHDSI; OHDSI CDM Working Group; US FDA, 2017; US FDA, 2026

    Speaker notes

    DARWIN EU reports about 40 data partners and about 250 million patients (2026 figures), with partners converting to OMOP. Custom concepts are never standard and map to a standard concept through "Maps to". Ask: does the client keep the source code next to every mapped concept?

    Careful teams never overwrite a source value

    • Keep original and standardised values side by side
    • Store units, censoring symbols and dictionary versions
    • Add provenance columns to every cleaned table

    Source: ChEMBL team blog (EMBL-EBI), 2020; MHRA, 2018; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023

    Speaker notes

    EMA's trial-systems guideline lists ten ALCOA++ attributes, ending in traceable. Annex 11 asks companies to consider, based on risk, an audit trail of GMP-relevant changes and deletions. Ask: if a vendor cleans the client's data, where does the original go?

    Internal records join public sources on identifiers, not names

    • Compound: InChIKey to PubChem CID and ChEMBL id
    • Target: UniProt accession to Ensembl id in Open Targets
    • Trial: NCT plus 8 digits to ClinicalTrials.gov

    Source: InChI Trust (IUPAC); US National Library of Medicine, NCBI PubChem; UniProt Consortium (Nucleic Acids Research), 2025; Open Targets; US National Library of Medicine, ClinicalTrials.gov

    Speaker notes

    Aspirin's InChIKey BSYNRYMUTXBXSQ-UHFFFAOYSA-N returns PubChem CID 2244, and ChEMBL lists it as CHEMBL25. UniProt P00533 is EGFR, which Open Targets addresses as ENSG00000146648. Ask: does the client's registry store InChIKeys, or only names?

    Medical and commercial data sit on two sides of one line

    • Medical science liaisons do scientific exchange, not promotion
    • Ask which team owns each record and content type
    • EU joint clinical assessments began in January 2025

    Source: APPA, IFAPP, MAPS and MSLS, 2021; European Commission

    Speaker notes

    From 12 January 2025, new cancer medicines and ATMPs undergo EU joint clinical assessments of relative effects on health outcomes. Ask: does the client's medical team share any system with its commercial team?

    Each reviewer asks for a different piece of evidence

    • QA: a supplier audit and a traceability matrix
    • IT: audit trails on, no shared logins
    • Legal: data ownership and access in the contract

    Source: European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; MHRA, 2018; European Commission, 2011

    Speaker notes

    Expect the client to assess you, often through an audit. Vendor validation data alone, apart from configuration and intended use, is not acceptable to the MHRA. In the example matrix, URS-003 failed and raised DEV-0007 [D:val-trace-matrix]. Ask: who signs acceptance at this client, and have we met them?

    Test a claim on the client's data, with the plan fixed first

    • Name the baseline and setting: ESMFold 6x or about 60x
    • Client's own held-out data, used once
    • Threshold, versions and graders agreed in advance

    Source: Lin et al. (bioRxiv), 2022; European Medicines Agency, 2024; US FDA, 2025

    Speaker notes

    The ESMFold preprint reports 6 times faster at 384 residues and about 60 times on shorter sequences. The FDA draft sets seven steps from question of interest to adequacy for the context of use. The January 2026 EMA-FDA principles of good AI practice in drug development ask for a clear context of use. Ask: what result would make this client say no?

    What you will be able to do

    • For each modality family and department, name the record identifier, the core measurement and its unit, and the pitfall that makes a right-looking number wrong.
    • Trace a join from an internal record to a public source (PubChem and ChEMBL by InChIKey, UniProt and Open Targets by accession and Ensembl id, ClinicalTrials.gov by NCT number) and say what breaks it.
    • Ask scoping questions of the team that owns the data, and write an evaluation plan that its QA, IT validation and legal reviewers can accept.

    Learn

    A client conversation turns on one table: the one the client already has. This module walks through those tables by modality and by department. For each one it gives the identifier, the measurement, the unit and the two or three mistakes that make a number look right when it is wrong. The example tables are illustrative records in the real format, with internal-style ids such as CPD-0001[data].

    Small molecules and chemical probes: a potency number carries its conditions

    A small molecule (a chemically made drug, usually taken as a tablet) is registered as a compound with an internal id. Each synthesis of it is a batch. Each test is an assay with its own id and protocol version. Results sit in an assay database linked to the ELN (electronic lab notebook) and the LIMS (laboratory information management system, the software that tracks samples and results)[1]. ChEMBL, the public bioactivity database run by EMBL-EBI, uses the same shape: molecule, assay, activity and target records, each with its own ChEMBL id[2].

    The headline number is the IC50: the concentration of compound that halves the activity measured in that assay, usually in nM (nanomolar). It belongs to the compound and the assay together. For a competitive inhibitor, the IC50 rises as the assay adds more of the natural substrate. Cheng and Prusoff showed in 1973 that the inhibition constant Ki, which does not depend on substrate, equals IC50 / (1 + S/Km). Here S is the substrate concentration and Km is the substrate concentration at which the enzyme runs at half its top speed[3, 4]. For an inhibitor that competes with ATP, the IC50 therefore depends on the ATP level. That is why the example potency table stores the ATP concentration (10 µM) next to every IC50[data][3, 5]. Two IC50s for one compound from assays run at different ATP levels are different measurements, not a disagreement.

    Potency spans orders of magnitude, so teams also store pIC50, the negative log10 of the molar IC50: 1 nM is pIC50 9[6]. The arithmetic follows from that scale. The good potency table has six rows for five compounds, because CPD-0005 was run twice (r1 at 55 nM, r2 at 61 nM). Summarise the replicates first: their geometric mean is 57.9 nM, n = 2[data]. The five compounds then average 430.7 nM, but their mean pIC50 is 7.153, which is 70.3 nM[data]. The plain average is dragged up by the one weak compound at 1,600 nM. A dashboard that averages nM values reports a series about six times weaker than it is. A dashboard that averages all six rows counts CPD-0005 twice. Replicates need one row each with a replicate id, or one summary row with n, never two unlabelled rows for the same compound and batch[data][6].

    Some results are not numbers. “Greater than 100 µM” means the curve never reached half activity at the top concentration tested. ChEMBL stores that symbol in its own field (standard_relation), apart from the value, and records the end-point type, such as IC50 or % inhibition, as it appears in the source[7]. ChEMBL computes its pChEMBL value only when the relation is ”=” and the unit is nM[8]. A pipeline that drops the symbol and keeps 100,000 nM as a measured value invents a precise number. A single-concentration result, such as 42% inhibition at 10 µM, is a different result type and never belongs in an IC50 column[data][6].

    DMPK results (drug metabolism and pharmacokinetics: how the body handles the compound) join to potency on compound_id and batch_id[data]. Two of them trip outsiders. LogD, the split of a compound between octanol and water, changes with pH for compounds that carry a charge, so a logD without its pH (usually 7.4) cannot be compared[9]. Intrinsic clearance from liver microsomes is reported in µL/min/mg protein, and from hepatocytes in µL/min/10^6 cells. The two are not interchangeable, and a scaled in vivo clearance in mL/min/kg is a third quantity for a different column[10].

    A chemical probe is a compound used to test a biological question, not a drug candidate. For epigenetic targets, one academic and industry consortium set minimum criteria: in vitro potency under 100 nM, more than 30-fold selectivity over related proteins in the same family, profiling against a standard panel of off-targets, on-target activity in cells under 1 µM, and an inactive close analogue as a negative control. Probes for other targets need criteria suited to them[11]. When a discovery client asks for “probe-quality” annotation, they are asking for all five, plus the version of the off-target panel used[data].

    Biologics: antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates and recombinant proteins

    A monoclonal antibody (a protein drug that binds one target, usually given by infusion) is tracked as a clone in discovery and as a lot in manufacturing[data, data]. Binding is measured by SPR (surface plasmon resonance, a sensor that records binding in real time). It gives an on-rate kon (per molar per second), an off-rate koff (per second) and the affinity KD, which equals koff / kon[12]. That relation is a free consistency check, and the exercises use it. The set-up matters too. A two-armed antibody flowing over a surface coated with antigen can look much tighter than it is, through avidity, so the antigen form and the capture format belong in the record[13].

    An antibody-drug conjugate, or ADC (an antibody that carries a cell-killing payload to the target cell), adds two numbers. DAR, the drug-to-antibody ratio, is the average number of payload molecules per antibody. Each DAR carries its method. One study compared native SEC-MS (size-exclusion chromatography with mass spectrometry) with HIC (hydrophobic interaction chromatography), the release assay. For interchain cysteine-linked ADCs it found no bias between the two for average DAR, so SEC-MS data could be bridged to HIC without a correction factor[14]. Methods can agree, so the defect to look for is a DAR with no recorded method. The specification names one release method; values from other methods are characterisation data[data][14]. In the messy ADC table, ADC-LOT-2024-001 appears twice, at 3.9 with no method recorded (row 1) and at 4.3 by UV (row 4), with no flag for the reportable value; the good table shows 3.9 is the HIC release value, so the UV value is 0.4 higher (about 10% of 3.9)[data]. Conjugation through lysine residues produces a wide spread of DARs, so the average hides a distribution[15]. Plasma stability is followed over time, separately in human, monkey, rat and mouse plasma; in one study an ADC released under 1% of its drug in human plasma over six days and nearly 25% in mouse plasma[16]. The good ADC table reports ADC-LOT-2024-001 at DAR 3.9 by HIC with 92% retained after 168 hours in human plasma; a “92%” with no species and no timepoint is not the same claim[data].

    Recombinant proteins are judged first on titer, the amount of protein made per litre of culture. Titer is usually in mg/L, and 0.12 g/L is 120 mg/L, a factor of 1,000 that sorts badly when units mix[17]. Endotoxin (bacterial residue that causes fever) is expressed in endotoxin units (EU), with limits set from the maximum dose that can be given within one hour[18]. ICH Q6B lists purity, quantity and potency among the attributes that a biological product’s specification covers[19].

    Cell therapy and functional genomics: every percentage needs its denominator

    A CAR-T product (a cell therapy: the patient’s own T cells, engineered to carry a chimeric antigen receptor that recognises a cancer antigen) is made one lot per patient[20]. FDA guidance on CAR T cell products covers the analytical tests and comparability these lots need[21]. A release record holds the share of T cells that carry the CAR, the vector copy number (VCN), viability, the CD4 to CD8 ratio and a potency result such as % specific lysis of target cells[data]. The FDA guidance asks for the average VCN per CAR-positive cell, which can be calculated from the VCN per cell and the share of CAR-positive cells[21]. For CT-LOT-2024-009, 3.1 copies per cell / 0.520 = 5.96 copies per CAR-positive cell[data].

    Three pitfalls recur. VCN reported per µg of DNA is a third denominator, from which neither per-cell value can be derived[data][21]. Potency must be tested, for example with a cell killing assay[21], and a % specific lysis cannot be compared across lots without its effector-to-target (E:T) ratio[data]. Antigen density on the target cells reported as MFI (mean fluorescence intensity) is in arbitrary units and needs standardisation between instruments. Calibration beads convert it into antibodies bound per cell. A 2026 review, whose first author worked partly for a maker of calibration kits, reports that this unit reproduces across instruments and laboratories under set conditions, such as saturating staining, unchanged reagents and a maintained instrument[22, 23].

    The lot also carries chain of identity: the lot id must trace to a donor id and an apheresis collection, because the product goes back to the person it came from[21]. A row with a blank donor field is a traceability failure, not a missing detail[data].

    A pooled CRISPR knockout screen (a lab method that switches off thousands of genes at once and counts which cells survive) reports, per gene, a log2 fold change (LFC) of guide counts against a reference sample and a false discovery rate (FDR)[24]. The reference must be named: a fold change against the plasmid pool and one against the day 0 cells are different numbers[24]. A nominal p-value written into the FDR column overstates confidence when thousands of genes are tested[data]. Gene symbols follow HGNC (the HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee); an alias in free text splits one gene into two[25].

    Gene therapy, RNA and vaccines: the unit tells you which record you hold

    Gene therapy. Onasemnogene abeparvovec (Zolgensma) is an AAV9 vector that delivers a working SMN gene in one intravenous infusion, with the dose expressed in vector genomes per kilogram (vg/kg)[26]. The lot itself is measured as a titre in vg/mL; the dose in vg/kg is calculated from it for one patient’s weight, in the dosing record[data]. The titre carries its method. qPCR reads the titre off a standard curve, so the reference standard is part of the number; ddPCR counts copies without one[27]. In one study, qPCR titres for five samples averaged 64 times the ddPCR titres. The qPCR standard held 11.5 times fewer copies than its label stated; with the re-measured value, the gap fell to 5.5 times. Nine other samples, run against a standard measured by ddPCR, read 11% higher by qPCR[27]. Another found ddPCR up to 4-fold higher than standard qPCR for single-stranded genomes[28]. So one method is the reportable titre per lot, and the others are characterisation data[data].

    RNA. Two EU-authorised examples show how different RNA medicines are. Nusinersen (Spinraza) is an antisense oligonucleotide acting on SMN2, given by intrathecal injection[29]. Comirnaty is an mRNA vaccine encoding the spike protein, given intramuscularly[30]. For that vaccine, the EU assessment report lists the finished-product tests. They include RNA content and RNA encapsulation by a fluorescence assay, the size and polydispersity of the lipid nanoparticles (LNP, the fat particles that carry the mRNA) by dynamic light scattering, and RNA integrity by capillary gel electrophoresis[31]. Polydispersity says how uniform the particles are; integrity is the share of full-length RNA. For every attribute tested on stability except RNA integrity, the release and shelf-life acceptance criteria are the same[31]. So an integrity result means little until you know whether it was a release or a stability test[data]. Ask the same questions as for any other table: identifier, unit, method and version.

    Vaccines. A clinical lot consistency study checks that consecutive manufactured lots give equivalent immune responses. FDA guidance for seasonal inactivated influenza vaccines recommends comparing three consecutive lots pair by pair for each viral strain. Each pair’s ratio of geometric mean titres (GMT) should have a two-sided 95% confidence interval entirely within 0.67 and 1.5[32]. A GMT is a group average computed geometrically[33]. The point estimate inside the margin is not enough. In the good table, Strain 2, Lot A / Lot C has a ratio of 1.24 but an upper limit of 1.53, so consistency is not shown for that strain[data].

    Department data: clinical, safety, manufacturing, real-world and regulatory

    Clinical data follow one path. Sites collect to CDISC CDASH (version 2.3 of its implementation guide), which is traceable into SDTM, the tabulation standard (model version 2.1, dated June 2024) that the FDA and Japan’s PMDA require[34, 35]. Analysis datasets follow ADaM (implementation guide version 1.3) and trace back to SDTM[36]. Define-XML describes every dataset’s structure and is required for each study in an electronic submission[37]. The FDA wants each dataset as a SAS XPORT version 5 file, and USUBJID, the subject id, identical across SDTM and ADaM; an improper USUBJID is a common error that often requires resubmission[38].

    Safety data, or pharmacovigilance (PV), run on the individual case safety report (ICSR). A valid ICSR needs four elements: an identifiable reporter, a single identifiable patient, at least one suspect reaction and a suspect medicine[39]. In the EU, serious cases are due within 15 days of receipt and non-serious ones within 90 days[39]. Other regions set their own clocks. Reactions are coded in MedDRA, a five-level terminology from system organ class (27 of them) down to lowest level term; the guide checked here is version 29.0[40]. MedDRA 29.1 became the global version on 2 November 2026[41]. A coded term means little without the MedDRA version it was coded in. Since 30 June 2022, EudraVigilance accepts ICSRs only in the ISO format based on ICH E2B(R3)[42]. Case id joins across intake, coding and submission; trial serious adverse events join to cases through USUBJID[data].

    Manufacturing data, or CMC (chemistry, manufacturing and controls), sit in the executed batch record in the MES (manufacturing execution system, Level 3 of the ISA-95 model) and in the QC LIMS[43, 1]. A specification lists each test, its analytical procedure and a numerical acceptance criterion, under ICH Q6A for chemical products[44]. Any result outside the criterion is out of specification (OOS) and starts a documented investigation; a retest cannot replace the original result without a documented laboratory error[45]. ICH Q1A sets how stability studies are run, so that stored batches are tested over time to support a shelf life[46]. A lot id is unique only within its product. In the example tables, tablet lots (LOT-2024-007) and CAR-T lots (CT-LOT-2024-007) carry different prefixes, so a join on product plus lot never attaches a tablet’s water result to a patient’s cell therapy[data, data].

    Real-world data come from health records, claims, registries and devices[47]. Many networks convert them to the OMOP common data model[48]. In OMOP, condition_concept_id holds the standard concept and condition_source_value keeps the original code; a concept id of 0 means no concept was assigned[49]. Only standard concepts (flagged “S”) are used in fields ending in _CONCEPT_ID[50]. A local code with no vocabulary entry can get a custom concept with an id above 2,000,000,000. Such concepts are never standard, sit only in fields ending in _source_concept_id and map to a standard concept through a “Maps to” relationship[51]. The good example table uses local source concepts such as 2000000341[data]. DARWIN EU, the EMA’s real-world network, reports about 40 data partners and about 250 million patients (2026 figures), with partners converting to OMOP[52].

    Regulatory data end in the CTD (common technical document): five modules, Module 1 regional and Modules 2 to 5 common to all regions[53]. eCTD, its electronic form, is required for NDAs, BLAs and commercial INDs[54]. Product data follow IDMP, five ISO standards that the EMA runs through its SPOR services[55].

    Commercial and medical data: two sides of one line

    Medical affairs gives healthcare professionals accurate, unbiased scientific information. Medical science liaisons do scientific exchange, not promotion[56]. So medical and commercial records belong to different teams under different rules. A tool that serves both must keep the line visible: ask which team owns each record and each content type before you scope it.

    Market access teams now face a new data demand in the EU. From 12 January 2025, new cancer medicines and advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs) undergo EU joint clinical assessments of their relative effects on health outcomes[57]. That work runs on comparative clinical evidence, and its owner is the market access or HTA (health technology assessment) team.

    Cleaning as a discipline: what a careful team never does

    Tidy data is the starting rule: each variable a column, each observation a row, each kind of unit its own table[58]. Beyond it, careful teams keep six habits. They put the unit in the column definition and convert at entry[17]. They keep censored values as symbol plus value, as ChEMBL does[7]. They record replicates explicitly[6]. They store dictionary versions with every coded term[40]. They resolve identifiers to one registry before joining[38]. And they de-duplicate only with a rule they can show to QA[59].

    The habit that matters most is that source values are never overwritten. For some common activity types, such as IC50, ChEMBL’s database downloads keep standardised fields (standard_value, standard_relation, standard_units) next to the original value, relation and units[60]. A GMP lab keeps the failing first result next to the retest[45]. The MHRA expects records to be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available (ALCOA+)[59]. EMA’s guideline for computerised systems in clinical trials lists ten attributes, ALCOA++, adding traceable[61]. Annex 11 asks companies to consider, based on a risk assessment, a system-generated audit trail of all GMP-relevant changes and deletions, with the reason for each change recorded[62]. In practice that means provenance columns on every cleaned table: source system, source row id, transformation, version and who ran it[61].

    Joining internal records to public sources

    Compound join. Names and drawings do not join; structure identifiers do. An InChIKey is a fixed 27-character hash of the InChI structure string: uppercase letters and two dashes. Its first 14 characters encode the skeleton and the next block the stereochemistry and isotopes[63]. Take an internal reference standard, CPD-REF-01, registered as aspirin. Its InChIKey, BSYNRYMUTXBXSQ-UHFFFAOYSA-N, sent to PubChem’s REST interface in the inchikey namespace, returns compound CID 2244[64]. In ChEMBL the same molecule is CHEMBL25[2]. A match on the first block alone means same skeleton, possibly different stereochemistry: flag it for a chemist, never merge it[63].

    Target join. An internal target record should hold a UniProt accession: 6 or 10 letters and digits[65]. The UniProt REST API returns, for P00533, the gene EGFR, HGNC:3236 and a sequence of 1,210 amino acids[66]. Open Targets addresses the same target by its Ensembl gene id, ENSG00000146648[67]. Ensembl ids follow a fixed form, ENS plus a type letter (G for gene, T for transcript, P for protein) plus 11 digits, with no species code for human and an optional version suffix[68, 69, 70]. Open Targets scores each target-disease pair by combining evidence from 20 sources (in its 2021 description) in a weighted harmonic sum[71]. Its documentation sends bulk work to the data downloads rather than one query per target[72]. The AlphaFold database model for the same accession is AF-P00533-F1, with a mean confidence (pLDDT) of 75.94[73].

    Trial and concept joins. A CTMS (clinical trial management system) study record should carry its NCT number, “NCT” followed by 8 digits[74]. ClinicalTrials.gov’s version 2 API returns one study at /api/v2/studies/ followed by that number, with no key[75]. On the real-world side, a local diagnosis code maps to an OMOP standard concept through the vocabulary tables, and several source codes can map to one standard concept[50]. Each join should land in a mapping table with the date, the source release and the rule used, so that the join can be rerun[76].

    Who owns which table, and what each reviewer asks to see

    Each table has an owner, and that owner is your first conversation. Discovery and DMPK scientists own the assay database and ELN[1]. Biostatistics and data management own SDTM and ADaM[35, 36]. The PV unit owns the safety database and MedDRA coding[39]. Manufacturing and QC own the batch record and LIMS, and QA owns release[77]. Annex 11 asks for close cooperation between relevant staff such as process owners, system owners, Qualified Persons and IT[62].

    The beginner module named the four gates. At this level, know the evidence each gate asks for. QA assesses the supplier, so expect the client to assess you, often through an audit[61]. Vendor-supplied validation data alone, apart from the client’s configuration and intended use, is not acceptable to the MHRA[59]. QA also expects validation documents across the life cycle, justified by risk, with change control records and a system inventory[62]. The core of a validation pack is a traceability matrix: each user requirement, its test, the software version tested and the evidence. In the example matrix, URS-003 failed and raised deviation DEV-0007, and URS-004 is covered by an SOP rather than a test[data]. QA decides and documents which records are Part 11 records[78]. IT checks that audit trails are switched on and cannot be switched off by users, and that no shared logins touch GxP data[59]. Legal reviews data ownership, governance and access in the third-party contract[59].

    Scoping questions follow from the tables. Which system of record holds the data, and who owns it? Which identifiers join it to the next table? Which units and dictionary versions does it use? Which fields are GxP records? Who signs the acceptance? These questions map to the reviewer list above[62, 78].

    Reading a claim against the client’s data, and writing an evaluation plan

    The beginner rule still holds: every speed or accuracy claim has a baseline, a setting and a publisher. The ESMFold preprint, for example, reports 6 times faster than one AlphaFold2 model at 384 residues and about 60 times on shorter sequences[79]; a company blog gives only the 60, for short sequences, with no length, hardware or comparator[80]. At this level the setting is usually the client’s own data. A titre method that reads 24% above the release method may be a calibration question, not a better assay: the AAV studies above show that the reference standard alone can move qPCR titres many-fold[data][27].

    A signal says something may work; proof shows it worked on the client’s data under agreed rules. The FDA’s draft AI credibility guidance sets seven steps, starting with the question of interest and the context of use. It excludes drug discovery and operational drafting that does not affect patient safety, drug quality or study reliability[81]. The EMA reflection paper asks that a hold-out test set be used once, with a new independent set after further development[82]. In January 2026 the EMA and FDA published ten joint principles of good AI practice in drug development. They are high-level principles meant to inform future guidance and standards, and they include a clear context of use and documented data governance[83].

    A credible evaluation plan in a proposal states six things. It names the question and the decision it supports. It names the dataset, drawn from the client’s own tables and held out[82]. It sets the acceptance threshold before the run[81]. It fixes the versions of the model, the index and the dictionaries, and changes them only under change control[62]. It keeps the original outputs as records[59] and names who grades them. And it says how deviations will be recorded[81]. This module does not cover pricing.

    Example data

    The potency table holds kinase IC50s measured at one ATP concentration, with CPD-0005 run twice as labelled replicates. In the messy version, look for a pIC50 typed into the IC50 column (it reads about 1.6 times more potent than the real value), a µM value in a nM column, a % inhibition stored as an IC50 and a replicate with no replicate id[data].

    The binding table holds kon, koff and KD for four clones against one antigen. In the messy version, check KD against koff / kon, look for a KD in pM among nM rows, a unit typed into the value cell and an antigen form missing where the assay orientation changed.

    The ADC table shows that DAR and plasma stability are only meaningful with their method, species and timepoint. In the messy version, find the DAR with no method and the lot that appears twice with two averages and no flag for the reportable one.

    The CAR-T release table holds one lot per donor, with VCN per cell and per CAR-positive cell. In the messy version, look for antigen density given as MFI, lysis without an E:T ratio, VCN per µg of DNA and a missing donor id.

    The vector titre table holds AAV lots measured by ddPCR (the release method) and qPCR. In the messy version, look for a qPCR titre without its reference standard, a titre labelled as a dose, a titre with no method and a lot with two reportable titres.

    The vaccine table holds pair-wise GMT ratios for three lots and two strains. In the messy version, look for a missing timepoint, a margin widened for one pair, a ratio with no named numerator and a pair marked consistent although its interval crosses the margin.

    The mRNA-LNP table holds one lot’s finished-product results at release and after 6 months on stability. In the messy version, look for a unit that changed, a result with no method, a size with no polydispersity, an integrity result with no test point and a lot id that will not join[data].

    The medical information log shows who answered which question. Look for the off-label (outside the approved label) question answered by sales, the request with no record, the off-label information given in a planned meeting and the answer built from promotional material[data].

    Try it

    1. A client’s dashboard shows the “average IC50” of the five compounds in the good potency table. Compute it in nM and on the pIC50 scale, and say which to report.[data]

      AnswerFirst summarise CPD-0005’s two labelled replicates: √(55 × 61) = 57.9 nM, mean pIC50 (7.26 + 7.21) / 2 = 7.235, n = 2[data][6]. Arithmetic mean of the five compounds: (12.5 + 480 + 3.1 + 1,600 + 57.9) / 5 = 2,153.5 / 5 = 430.7 nM. Mean pIC50: (7.90 + 6.32 + 8.51 + 5.80 + 7.235) / 5 = 35.765 / 5 = 7.153, and 10 to the power of minus 7.153 molar is 70.3 nM[data][6]. Report the pIC50 mean (the same number as the geometric mean). The arithmetic mean is about 6.1 times higher because CPD-0004 at 1,600 nM dominates it. Averaging all six rows instead gives 368.6 nM and counts CPD-0005 twice.

    2. CPD-0001 has an IC50 of 12.5 nM at 10 µM ATP. Assume, for this exercise only, that the kinase’s Km for ATP is 10 µM and the compound competes with ATP. What is its Ki, and what would a vendor need to state before comparing it with a public Ki?[data]

      AnswerKi = IC50 / (1 + S/Km) = 12.5 / (1 + 10/10) = 12.5 / 2 = 6.25 nM[data][3]. The Km here is an assumption; the table does not record it. The vendor must state the ATP concentration, the Km used and that the compound is competitive, because this form of the equation is stated for competitive inhibition[3, 5]. Without those, compare IC50s only within one assay and version.

    3. A buyer asks which CAR-T lot is most potent. CT-LOT-2024-007 shows 62% specific lysis and CT-LOT-2024-010 shows 48%. Can you rank them? Then check the VCN per CAR-positive cell for CT-LOT-2024-007.[data]

      AnswerNo. CT-LOT-2024-007 was tested at an E:T ratio of 5:1 and CT-LOT-2024-010 at 1:1, so CT-LOT-2024-010 had five times fewer effector cells per target cell[data]. Lysis is only comparable at the same E:T ratio and timepoint[data]. Among the 5:1 lots, CT-LOT-2024-009 (71%) is above CT-LOT-2024-007 (62%) and CT-LOT-2024-008 (55%)[data]. VCN per CAR-positive cell for CT-LOT-2024-007: 1.8 / 0.452 = 3.98, which matches the table[data][21].

    4. In the messy binding table, compute KD = koff / kon for AB-0101 and AB-0104, and convert AB-0101’s reported 710 pM to nM. Which row fails the check?[data]

      AnswerAB-0101: 1.5E-04 / 2.1E+05 = 7.1E-10 M = 0.71 nM, and 710 pM = 0.71 nM, so it agrees once the units match[data][17]. AB-0104: 6.0E-04 / 1.2E+05 = 5.0E-09 M = 5.0 nM against a reported 0.5 nM, so one of the three numbers is wrong[data][12]. AB-0104 fails; the good table reports 5.0 nM.

    5. A data team joins the potency table to the DMPK table on compound_id. The good DMPK table has rows for CPD-0001, CPD-0002, CPD-0003 and CPD-0005. How many rows does an inner join return from the good potency table (6 rows) and from the messy one (6 rows), and what should the team do first?[data, data]

      AnswerBoth return 5 rows: CPD-0004 has no DMPK result and drops out, and CPD-0005 appears twice[data, data]. In the good table the two CPD-0005 rows are labelled r1 and r2, so the team can see that its clearance of 31 µL/min/mg protein is repeated. In the messy table the second row has no replicate id, so nobody can tell a replicate from a duplicate[data]. Summarise replicates to one row per compound first (CPD-0005: 57.9 nM, n = 2); the join then returns 4 rows, one per compound[data, data][6].

    6. A contract lab’s slide says its qPCR titre for AAV-LOT-2025-001 is 2.6E+13 vg/mL, “higher than the ddPCR release value”. By how much, what do you ask, and what evaluation-plan line do you propose?[data]

      Answer2.6E+13 / 2.1E+13 = 1.24, so 24% higher[data]. Question: “Which reference standard and stated concentration produced the qPCR curve, and was the standard itself measured?” qPCR depends on its standard; ddPCR does not, and in one study a mis-stated standard made qPCR read 64 times higher; correcting the standard cut the gap to 5.5 times[27]. The release value stays the ddPCR result marked reportable[data]. Plan line: “Measure both methods on the same held-out lots, with the standard id and version recorded, against an agreement criterion set before the run”[81]. Method comparisons in the literature report several-fold gaps, so the criterion matters[28].

    7. In the messy vaccine table, row 5 (Strain 2, Lot A / Lot C) is marked consistent. Is it, and what else in the table would a reviewer reject?[data]

      AnswerNo. Its 95% interval runs from 1.03 to 1.53, and 1.53 is above the 1.5 limit, so the pair fails even though the ratio of 1.24 sits inside the margin[data][32]. Row 2 uses a wider margin (0.5 to 2.0) than the other pairs; the guidance gives one range, 0.67 to 1.5, for the comparison[32]. Row 1 has no timepoint. Row 3, “Lot B vs Lot C”, does not name the numerator: 0.93 for B / C is 1 / 0.93 = 1.08 for C / B[data].

    8. A dashboard tracks RNA integrity for RNA-LOT-2025-001 from the messy mRNA-LNP table. What does it show, and what does the good table show? Then convert the messy RNA content to the unit of the good table.[data]

      AnswerThe messy table gives the dashboard nothing to trend. The 85% row has no test point, so it cannot be judged against release or shelf-life criteria, which differ for RNA integrity[31]. The 79% row is filed under RNA-2025-001, so a join on RNA-LOT-2025-001 drops it[data]. The good table shows 85% at release and 79% after 6 months: a fall of 85 − 79 = 6 percentage points, or 6 / 85 = about 7% of the release value[data]. RNA content: 500 µg/mL ÷ 1,000 = 0.50 mg/mL, the same amount as the good table[17]. Ask the client which unit the specification uses, and fix it at entry.

    9. A buyer’s team wants an AI tool to draft answers from this log. Which messy rows show a process it should not learn from?[data]

      AnswerAll four: sales answered, no record, proactive off-label, and an answer built from promotional material[56].

    Check yourself

    1. An IC50 of the same compound doubles when the assay’s ATP concentration is raised. The most likely explanation is: a) the compound degraded b) IC50 depends on substrate concentration for a competitive inhibitor c) a unit error d) a different batch
    2. Which identifier must be the same for one trial subject in SDTM and ADaM? a) NCT number b) case id c) USUBJID d) person_id
    3. A % specific lysis of 62% cannot be compared across CAR-T lots without: a) the MFI b) the E:T ratio c) the VCN d) the CD4 to CD8 ratio
