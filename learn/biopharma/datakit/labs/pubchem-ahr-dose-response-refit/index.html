1. The question

For the curves that the depositor of PubChem AID 743085 classed as complete and of high efficacy, does a four-parameter Hill fit to the deposited titration points give the same log AC50 as the depositor's own fit?

An AC50 is a fitted number, not a measured one. Refitting the same points shows how much of a reported potency comes from the data and how much from the fitting choices, such as which points were left out.

From: Data table of AID 743085, columns Fit_LogAC50-Replicate_1 to Fit_LogAC50-Replicate_46, rows with Fit_CurveClass 1.1[1]

6. What goes wrong in practice

  • The depositor left points out of the fit. The Excluded_Points column marks the points the depositor removed before fitting. 303 of the 833 curves in this lab have at least one. With the flags, 275 of those 303 refits are within half a dilution step of the deposited AC50. Ignoring the flags, 209 are. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • A complete curve does not always fix the AC50. For 162 of the 833 curves, AC50 values more than one dilution step apart fit the points within 10 percent of the best sum of squares. 60 of the 71 refits that differ from the deposit are among them. A curve class is a label, not a guarantee that the potency is well determined. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • Shallow curves and a free top plateau. Of the 32 curves whose refit slope is below 0.6, only 7 match the deposit. Where the two fits differ, the refit's top plateau is higher, at a median of 1.31 times the deposited one, against 1.0 where they agree. The record does not say how the depositor's program limits the plateaus, so the cause is not established. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • The same class is written two ways. Fit_CurveClass is stored as text. Class 1.1 appears as "1.1" for 804 curves and as "1.1000" for 29. Compare it as a number, or 29 curves are lost. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • Units differ between columns. The concentrations in the column names are in micromolar. Fit_LogAC50 is the log of the AC50 in molar units. Convert before comparing, or every value is off by 6. [2]
  • The first rows are not substances. The first five rows of the file describe the columns (type, description, unit). The 10,486 substance rows start after them. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • The activity call belongs to the substance, not the curve. PUBCHEM_ACTIVITY_OUTCOME is one call per substance across all its replicates. 27 of the 833 class 1.1 curves sit on substances called Inactive overall. Select curves by their own curve class. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • An extrapolated AC50 is not a measured one. The record says a partial curve may not have reached its second plateau, and that extrapolated AC50s are reported with the highest efficacy observed. This lab checks only complete curves, where both plateaus were seen. [2]

  • Open source executed here The lab's notebook reads the AID 743085 data table with pandas, reshapes it to one row per curve and keeps the curves of class 1.1. It fits a four-parameter Hill curve to each with numpy and scipy (curve_fit) and compares each log AC50 with the depositor's fit. This site's check runs it in a fresh Python process.[4][5][6]
