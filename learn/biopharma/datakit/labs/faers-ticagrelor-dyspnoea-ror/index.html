1. The question

Is dyspnoea reported more often with ticagrelor than with all other drugs in the US FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), in reports received 1 October 2010 to 31 March 2023?

Signal detection in pharmacovigilance starts from counts like these. A disproportionality measure asks whether an event makes up a larger share of the reports for one drug than of the reports for all other drugs. It is a screen for signals that need review, not an incidence and not evidence that the drug causes the event.

From: Results 3.1 (report totals) and Table 2, row Dyspnea (reports, ROR with 95% CI, PRR with chi-squared)[1]

openFDA's overview says that in the United States healthcare professionals and consumers report voluntarily, either to FDA or to the product's manufacturer, and that a manufacturer that receives a report is normally required to send it to FDA.
Standards
E2B(R3)

openFDA says the reports follow ICH E2B/M2 version 2.1 and that openFDA adds harmonised drug fields and serves the reports as JSON. The kit's registry has E2B(R3) only, the closest id; the version these reports follow is 2.1. Each report lists drugs with a reported role and reactions as MedDRA terms; no drug is tied to one reaction.[2][3][4]

6. What goes wrong in practice

  • Suspect is not primary suspect. openFDA's drug role field has three values, suspect, concomitant and interacting. It does not separate the primary suspect from other suspects. The paper counted only reports where the reporter named ticagrelor the primary suspect, so the openFDA counts start larger. [3][1]
  • Drug names are mapped in different ways. openFDA adds a harmonised generic name only where the product name matches exactly. The paper's tool mapped free-text names to US Adopted Names by its own rules. The two sets of reports overlap but are not the same. [4][1]
  • Which date defines the window. The paper gives a window but not the date field. This lab uses receivedate, the date FDA first received the report. receiptdate, the date of the most recent information, would give another set. Pick one before you look at the numbers. [3][1]
  • Reports change after the paper. openFDA returns only the latest version of each report, and the data are refreshed. The paper used an extract that ended on 31 March 2023 and counted whole cases across versions. Note meta.last_updated with every number. [3][1]
  • British spelling and many MedDRA versions. Reaction terms are stored in British English, so the term is Dyspnoea, while the paper prints Dyspnea. Each report carries the MedDRA version it was coded with; the paper used version 24.0 throughout. Match the whole term, not a prefix, or you also catch terms such as Dyspnoea exertional. [3][1]
  • The b and c labels are swapped in the paper's footnote. The equations in the paper's Table 1 put drug reports in the first row (a, b) and other-drug reports in the second (c, d). The footnote describes b and c the other way round. ROR and chi-squared do not change; PRR does. The paper's printed PRR follows the equations. [1]
  • A count call returns a capped list. The query-parameter page says count returns the 1000 most frequent values. The two count calls in this lab, sent without a limit, returned 100 terms each. A rarer reaction may not be in the list at all. Check that the term is present before you read its count. Seen in this lab's data; the notebook shows it.
  • A signal is not a rate. The 2x2 table counts reports, not patients or prescriptions. openFDA says the reports are not verified for cause and effect and cannot be used to estimate incidence. A high ROR says the event makes up more of this drug's reports; it does not say how often the event happens or that the drug causes it. [2]

7. The same job on each platform

  • Open source executed here The lab's notebook reads four openFDA API answers (two report totals and two term counts), builds the 2x2 table for ticagrelor and dyspnoea with pandas, computes the reporting odds ratio and the proportional reporting ratio, and compares them with the published analysis. This site's check runs it in a fresh Python process.[6]
  • AWS documented, not executed herevendor-published Amazon S3 is object storage, with management features to organise data and configure access. A learner with an account could keep the four openFDA API responses as objects in a bucket, as the stored inputs behind the 2x2 table. Not done here.[7]
  • Databricks documented, not executed herevendor-published Databricks SQL is a data warehouse that runs on the data lake and supports ANSI SQL with Delta Lake extensions. A learner with an account could load the four report counts into a table and compute the two ratios in a SQL query. Not done here.[8]
  • Microsoft documented, not executed herevendor-published Power BI connects to data, visualises it and shares it, with Power BI Desktop for building reports and the Power BI service for viewing and sharing them. A learner could build a report that shows the 2x2 table and the two ratios with their confidence intervals. Not done here.[9]
