SEND cross-study example files, four 1-month rat and dog toxicology studies of Compound A and Compound B (PHUSE phuse-scripts)

Four de-identified repeat-dose toxicology studies in SEND format, one rat and one dog study for each of two compounds, kept in the PHUSE phuse-scripts repository in a folder named for a 2024 cross-study analysis paper.

Dataset card Preclinical · PHUSE (phuse-org organisation on GitHub)

The PHUSE repository phuse-scripts describes itself as delivering standard industry analyses built upon CDISC standards for analysis data. Its data/send folder holds 16 subfolders. One is named "TOXSCI-24-0062 Cross Study Analysis XPT Files" and has four study folders, named "35449 1 month dog- Compound B-xpt" (24 files), "43066 1 month dog- Compound A-xpt" (21 files), "87497 1 month rat- Compound B-xpt" (24 files) and "96298 1 month rat- Compound A xpt" (24 files). The 93 files all end in .xpt and are named by SEND domain, for example bw, lb, mi, om, dm and ts; the dog study of Compound A has no bg, eg or fw file. The paper by Carfagna and others in Toxicological Sciences 2024 describes a platform to harmonise and compare SEND data across studies. It says de-identified SEND data, donated by companies taking part in BioCelerate's Toxicology Data Sharing Initiative, were used, and that they included 1-month rat and 1-month dog datasets for two compounds meant for the same pharmacological target. It analysed the BW, FW, LB, OM and MI domains, normalised by Z-score. The paper names a Dryad record (doi 10.5061/dryad.s1rn8pkgr) as the place the data are available, and a separate PHUSE GitHub repository for its R code; neither was opened. The paper text does not contain the string TOXSCI-24-0062 and does not name phuse-scripts. The link between this folder and the paper rests on the folder name and the matching study design. No XPT file was downloaded for this card.

Good for
  • Seeing how one repeat-dose toxicology study is split into SEND domain files, and which domains differ between studies.
  • Seeing what a cross-study comparison needs, with two species, two compounds and the same domains in each study.

Why this is not a lab: No published result located. The paper's results are plots, a summary radar plot and Z-score plots of body weight and clinical pathology, and its text gives directions only. It says Compound A produced significant decreases in rat and dog body weight that progressed with time, and that compound B did not produce significant body-weight changes. No number is given to check a computed value against. A scouting probe recorded in this site's shortlist on 10 Nov 2026 reported the opposite body-weight pattern by folder label; that probe was not repeated for this card, and the question must be settled before any lab is specified.
