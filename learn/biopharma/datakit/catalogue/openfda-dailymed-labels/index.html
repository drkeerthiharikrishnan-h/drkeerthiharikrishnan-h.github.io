Drug labels as structured text: the openFDA drug labeling API, with the DailyMed SPL web services

Drug product labels submitted to FDA in Structured Product Labeling format, served by openFDA as JSON split into label sections, and listed by version through the DailyMed web services.

Dataset card Regulatory · U.S. Food and Drug Administration (openFDA); DailyMed web services by the National Library of Medicine

The openFDA drug labeling page says manufacturers and distributors submit documentation about their products to FDA in the Structured Product Labeling (SPL) format, and that the API returns data from these submissions for prescription and over-the-counter drugs, broken into sections such as indications for use or purpose, adverse reactions and so forth, with considerable variation between products. openFDA annotates the records with harmonised fields and converts them to JSON; the bulk of the data runs from June 2009 to the present, updated weekly. The page's disclaimer says the labeling has been reformatted but neither altered nor verified by FDA, and may not be the labeling on currently distributed products or identical to the approved labeling. On 12 Nov 2026 the API reported 264,736 labels (last_updated 2026-11-12). Every response carries openFDA's disclaimer: do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care, and assume all results are unvalidated. DailyMed, at the National Library of Medicine, offers a RESTful API for current SPL information in XML or JSON (version v2); a query for ticagrelor listed 37 SPLs with their set ids, versions and publication dates. The DailyMed terms of use were not opened, so this card's licence reading covers the openFDA side only.

Where
openFDA endpoint /drug/label (https://api.fda.gov/drug/label.json); DailyMed REST API v2 (https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/)
Access
Open, no login
Size and format
264,736 records in 14 zip partitions totalling 1,787.46 MB as listed in the openFDA download index (export date 2026-11-12). JSON (API responses and zipped bulk files), SPL XML (DailyMed, per its web services page).
Licence
Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain dedication), unless otherwise noted. The openFDA terms say the data are public domain under CC0 unless otherwise noted, that some data may not be public domain (for example copies of copyrightable works made available to FDA by private entities), and that such works are marked by a warning in the documentation; no such warning was seen on the endpoint page opened for this card. The service is offered subject to the terms of service and to the FDA Website Policies, which were not opened. FDA asks for credit, for example "Data provided by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (https://open.fda.gov)". The DailyMed terms were not opened; use the openFDA endpoint, or check DailyMed's terms first.
Data dictionary
openFDA drug label searchable fields (reference also as PDF, XLSX and YAML), DailyMed web services page
Good for
  • Seeing how a drug label is cut into named sections (boxed warning, indications, adverse reactions) and how those sections differ between products with the same active ingredient.
  • Practising text extraction on regulatory documents, with the label version and set id that let a reader find the same label again.

Why this is not a lab: No published result located. The labels are a live collection that openFDA updates weekly, and no published figure for a dated snapshot was located: the clinical scout found none on 10 Nov 2026, and this card's author did not search further. A count taken today could not be checked against anything in print.
