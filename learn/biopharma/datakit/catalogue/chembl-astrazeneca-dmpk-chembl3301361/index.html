Experimental in vitro DMPK and physicochemical data on a set of publicly disclosed compounds (ChEMBL document CHEMBL3301361)

Measured drug-property data that a pharmaceutical company deposited in the open ChEMBL database, including lipophilicity (logD at pH 7.4). A widely used machine-learning benchmark for lipophilicity cites a ChEMBL data set of the same title.

Dataset card Discovery · ChEMBL, EMBL-EBI; deposit by Mark Wenlock and Nicholas Tomkinson, as given on the ChEMBL document page

The ChEMBL document page gives the title above, the authors Mark Wenlock and Nicholas Tomkinson, and an abstract. In the abstract's words the data were determined at AstraZeneca in these assays: pKa, lipophilicity (LogD7.4), aqueous solubility, plasma protein binding in five species, and intrinsic clearance in human liver microsomes and in human and rat hepatocytes. The MoleculeNet benchmark paper (Wu and others, 2018) describes its Lipophilicity set as "curated from ChEMBL database", with octanol/water distribution coefficients at pH 7.4, and lists 4200 compounds for it in Table 1. Its reference 45 has the same title as this ChEMBL document and gives no ChEMBL id, DOI or address. So the title links the two; that the benchmark file holds the same compounds and values as the ChEMBL assay was not checked.

Good for
  • Seeing how a company's measured property data look once deposited in a public bioactivity database, with one document, several assays and one value per compound and assay.
  • Tracing a machine-learning benchmark back to the deposit it was curated from, and asking what was changed on the way.
  • Reading beside the solubility lab (moleculenet-esol-xgboost-rmse), which reproduces a published benchmark score for another property with the same kind of model.

Why this is not a lab: Licence or access does not allow a lab. A lab was specified: train a gradient-boosted model on fingerprints and compare its test error with the score MoleculeNet prints for Lipophilicity (Table 3: XGBoost 0.799, graph convolution 0.655, RMSE). It needs the measured logD values from ChEMBL. On 12 Nov 2026 the ChEMBL web services answered HTTP 500 or timed out to four requests (three by the builder at about 07:25 UTC, one by the lead at 12:50 UTC), so no input file exists and the match between the ChEMBL assay and the benchmark set could not be checked. The licence is not the obstacle. The lab can be reopened when the service answers; its specification is kept in the lab folder.
