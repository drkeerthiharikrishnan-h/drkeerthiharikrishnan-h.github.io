SPL-ADR-200db: 200 FDA drug labels annotated for adverse drug reactions (NLM and FDA, TAC 2017)

Structured product labels with hand-made mention-level annotations of adverse reactions and their modifiers, released for the TAC 2017 adverse drug reaction extraction track.

Dataset card Safety, Regulatory · U.S. National Library of Medicine, with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration

The NLM page describes the TAC 2017 track as a test of natural language processing approaches for extracting adverse reactions from structured product labels, and gives the FDA's interest as comparing the reactions in labels from different manufacturers for the same drug and finding reactions not yet in the labels during post-marketing safety review. The gold standard holds mention annotations (AdverseReaction, Severity, DrugClass, Negation, Animal, Factor), relations between them (Negated, Hypothetical, Effect, Equiv), and a document-level list of unique reactions annotated with MedDRA Lower Level Terms and Preferred Terms. The Data Access section lists train_xml.tar.gz (101 files), gold_xml.tar.gz (99 files) and unannotated_xml.tar.gz (2,208 files, labels without annotations), and links annotation guidelines. The data paper's abstract (Sci Data 2018) says FDA and NLM created a pilot dataset for 200 FDA-approved drugs, annotated at the mention level and normalised to UMLS and MedDRA, and that the database SPL-ADR-200db contains 5,098 distinct adverse reactions; it gives the code at github.com/lhncbc/fda-ars. No file was downloaded for this card.

Where

Access
Open, no login
Size and format
train_xml.tar.gz 898,022 bytes and gold_xml.tar.gz 859,538 bytes (Content-Length, HEAD requests); unannotated_xml.tar.gz not sized. XML (one file per label, in tar.gz archives).
Licence
No licence statement was found on the NLM page; the data paper is published under CC BY (Europe PMC record), which covers the article, not the files. No licence or copyright wording appears on the dataset page opened on 12 Nov 2026. The gold annotations carry MedDRA terms, and MedDRA's own terms were not opened. This card gives links only and stores no copy, no preview and no MedDRA codes.
Data dictionary
Annotation guidelines (PDF) and the XML format on the dataset page
Good for
  • Seeing how adverse reactions in a drug label are annotated: the reaction span, its severity, negation and hedging, and the document-level list mapped to a coding dictionary.
  • Understanding why label text matters to pharmacovigilance: checking whether a reported reaction is already in the label.

Why this is not a lab: Licence or access does not allow a lab. No licence statement was found for the files: the dataset page has none, and the CC BY licence on the data paper covers the article. The paper's abstract does give a figure a lab could check (5,098 distinct adverse reactions in 200 labels), but the annotations carry MedDRA terms whose terms of use were not opened, and this is a card with links only and no stored preview.
