Which level fits you?

Three quick questions. Answer honestly; nothing is recorded.

  1. Can you name the main stages a medicine goes through before approval?
  2. Do you know which systems and data formats each department works with?

Biopharma for Delivery leads, PMs and BAs

Map · Machine · Frontier: regulated delivery, step by step

Each stage hands documents to the next, so treat every box as a hand-off

  • IND filed with the FDA before the first trial in people
  • NDA or BLA holds all the data; sent as eCTD
  • Review: 6 to 10 months once the file is complete

Source: US FDA, 2018; US FDA, 2018; US FDA, 2026

Speaker notes

Of 100 drugs that start Phase I, about 8 are approved. The FDA expects all data behind a filing to be reliable and accurate, and may inspect the sites that made it. Ask the room: which document does your project produce, and who checks it next?

Protocol amendments change trial systems, so plan several validated releases

  • Phase I: 20 to 100 people; Phase III: 300 to 3,000
  • Each amendment can change the EDC or IRT set-up
  • Changed set-ups are specified, documented and tested again

Source: US FDA, 2018; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023

Speaker notes

Phase III runs over 1 to 4 years. EMA says trial configuration is pre-specified, checked against the protocol and tested before release, and amendments follow the same process. EDC is electronic data collection; IRT handles randomisation and drug supply. Ask: how many amendments did the client's last study have?

Every arrow in the trial data path is a transfer that must be validated

  • Sites enter data in eCRFs; CDASH standardises collection
  • SDTM organises the data; required by the FDA and PMDA
  • ADaM traces back to SDTM; results go into the CSR

Source: CDISC, 2023; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; CDISC, 2024; CDISC, 2021; European Medicines Agency, 1996

Speaker notes

The EMA expects transfers between systems to be validated so the data stay accurate. Annex 11 adds that a migration must not change data in value or meaning. The CSR then goes into Module 5 of the submission. Ask who owns the mapping specification behind each arrow, and how it is tested.

Safety cases have a fixed format, so the deliverable is set before you start

  • One ICSR per case, coded in MedDRA and WHODrug
  • EU: ICH E2B(R3) format mandatory since 30 June 2022
  • Reports go to EudraVigilance, the EU repository

Source: European Medicines Agency, 2020; MedDRA MSSO (ICH), 2026; Uppsala Monitoring Centre; European Medicines Agency

Speaker notes

ICSR means individual case safety report. Submissions are just as fixed: five CTD modules, sent as eCTD. When a computer system records batch certification and release, Annex 11 says it must allow only Qualified Persons to certify, with an electronic signature. Ask which of these fixed deliverables your system touches.

Find which rule covers each system before you plan the work

  • EU manufacturing (GMP) systems: EU Annex 11
  • US electronic records under FDA rules: Part 11
  • Trial systems (EDC, eCOA, IRT, CTMS, eTMF): EMA 2023 guideline

Source: European Commission, 2011; US FDA, 2003; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023

Speaker notes

Part 11 scope is decided per record, in advance, and documented, for example in an SOP. FDA uses enforcement discretion on some Part 11 requirements, but predicate rules such as GLP and CGMP still apply in full. Trials follow ICH E6, whose R3 principles took effect on 23 July 2025. Ask the client for the document that says which rule applies to which system.

Validation is a lifecycle with named owners, so name them on day one

  • Risk assessment sets how much to validate
  • Every change, including configuration, follows a defined procedure
  • Periodic review confirms the system is still valid

Source: European Commission, 2011; ISPE, 2022

Speaker notes

Annex 11 clause 2 asks for close cooperation between Process Owner, System Owner, Qualified Persons and IT. In trials, the responsible party approves the validation report before release. GAMP 5 (ISPE, second edition, 2022) uses supplier documents where possible. Ask: who signed the last validation report, and how long did it take?

A "GxP-ready" label is a claim to check, not validation

  • Supplier responsibilities go in a formal agreement (clause 3.1)
  • Rely on vendor documents only after assessing them as adequate
  • Vendor tests without your configuration and use are not enough

Source: European Commission, 2011; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; MHRA, 2018

Speaker notes

Annex 11 counts internal IT departments as third parties under clause 3.1. EMA says a vendor that refuses audits or key documents should not be used in trials. MHRA says vendor testing apart from the user's process is likely to be functional checks only. Ask the vendor for the agreement and the documents, not only the label.

Production AI cases and the FDA's risk-based draft rules

  • Merck & Co. and Novo Nordisk draft study reports
  • Signal only: a Bayer study, one team of 3
  • FDA draft guidance: credibility judged per context of use

Source: Merck & Co., 2025; Anthropic, 2025; Benaiche et al. (Bayer Healthcare SAS France), 2025; US FDA, 2025

Speaker notes

Merck's platform is designed to operate with oversight by qualified medical writers; the Bayer study (a signal, not yet production) ran about four months; Novo Nordisk's figures are vendor-published. More than 70% of FDA staff used Elsa voluntarily, per FDA internal data. The FDA draft (January 2025, not final) weighs model influence and decision consequence; EMA wants models frozen before database lock in pivotal trials. Scope a review step, approved inputs and a record of who reviewed what.

Biopharma on one page, for Delivery leads, PMs and BAs

The chain, ten terms, five questions, cases.

The chain in one line

Discovery → preclinical research → clinical research → FDA review → post-market safety monitoring[1]. In EU manufacturing (GMP) work, every computer system follows the Annex 11 lifecycle below[2]. In the US, electronic records kept under FDA rules fall under Part 11[3].

Ten terms

  • Process Owner / System Owner: the person responsible for the business process / for the system’s availability, maintenance and data security[2].
  • Risk assessment: justified and documented; it decides how much validation a system needs[2].
  • Supplier agreement: a formal agreement that states what a supplier, or internal IT, is responsible for[2].
  • User requirements (URS): the functions a system must have, based on risk and traceable to tests[2, 4].
  • Change control: every change, including configuration, made under a defined procedure[2].
  • Audit trail: who changed what, when and why, with old and new values; normal users cannot switch it off[4].
  • ALCOA++: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available, traceable[4].
  • Part 11 record: a record an FDA rule requires, kept electronically instead of on paper, or submitted to the FDA[5].
  • GAMP 5: ISPE’s risk-based guide to compliant GxP computer systems, second edition 2022[6].
  • Context of use: in FDA draft guidance, the specific role and scope of an AI model[7].

Five questions to ask

  1. Which rules apply to this system and its data: Annex 11, Part 11, the EMA trial-systems guideline, GLP?
  2. Who are the Process Owner, the System Owner, and the person who approves validation and release?
  3. Is there a signed supplier agreement with responsibilities, and has anyone assessed the vendor’s validation documents?
  4. Where is the risk assessment, and does every user requirement trace to a test?
  5. Who owns audit trail review, access reviews, change control and periodic review after go-live?

Cases and one signal

  • Merck & Co. drafts clinical study reports with a generative AI platform designed to operate with oversight by qualified medical writers[8].
  • Novo Nordisk drafts clinical documents with NovoScribe from expert-approved text. Documentation that took more than 10 weeks now takes 10 minutes, a figure published by its AI vendor[9].
  • Signal, not yet shown in production: Bayer. One Bayer France drug-safety team of 3 people drafted case documentation letters with AI[10]. A peer-reviewed study ran about four months, 9 weeks without and then 9 weeks with the tool, and measured a 23.3% average time saving per case[10]. All users kept using it[10].
