    Level 1 of 4: the path, the systems and what good data means

    A medicine passes five regulated steps, and each step leaves records

    • Discovery, preclinical, clinical, review, post-market monitoring
    • Preclinical toxicity work follows GLP in 21 CFR Part 58
    • US trials need an IND; EU trials apply through CTIS

    Source: US FDA, 2018; US FDA, 2018; US FDA, 2018; European Medicines Agency

    Speaker notes

    The FDA describes the five steps on its patient pages. An IND (investigational new drug application) must be in place before clinical research; in the EU, CTIS implements Regulation (EU) No 536/2014. Ask the room: which of these steps produces records your current project touches?

    Trials grow from tens of people to thousands across three phases

    • Phase 1: 20 to 100 people, several months
    • Phase 2: up to several hundred, up to 2 years
    • Phase 3: 300 to 3,000 people, 1 to 4 years

    Source: US FDA, 2018

    Speaker notes

    The FDA gives these as typical sizes and durations, with about 70%, 33% and 25 to 30% moving on. A trial system built for Phase 1 must scale to Phase 3 volumes. Ask: what in your design would break at 30 times the subjects?

    About 8 in 100 drugs entering Phase I reach approval, 10.5 years later

    • Phase I 52.0%, Phase II 28.9%, Phase III 57.8%
    • Filings approved: 90.6%; overall 7.9%
    • Name the source: FDA and BIO rates differ

    Source: BIO, Informa Pharma Intelligence and QLS Advisors, 2021; US FDA, 2018

    Speaker notes

    BIO tracked 9,704 programmes from 2011 to 2020. The 10.5 years splits into 2.3, 3.6, 3.3 and 1.3 years by stage. Ask: how many releases will a validated EDC go through in 10.5 years?

    The modality class changes what a record must carry

    • Small molecule: identity is the structure, as an InChIKey
    • Peptide semaglutide is made by recombinant DNA in yeast
    • CAR-T: each lot is made from one patient's cells

    Source: InChI Trust (IUPAC); European Medicines Agency; European Medicines Agency

    The module table lists nine classes, each with one approved example from an EPAR or FDA page. Kymriah uses the patient's own T cells, so the lot record and the patient record must stay linked. Ask: which modality does your next project serve, and what is its unit of identity?

    Each of twelve departments writes into its own system of record

    • Lab: ELN and LIMS; trials: EDC, CTMS, eTMF
    • Plant: MES at ISA-95 Level 3, ERP at Level 4
    • Safety database reports to EudraVigilance in E2B(R3)

    Source: Raviteja and Gupta, JSS College of Pharmacy, 2013; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; ISA; European Medicines Agency, 2020

    Speaker notes

    RIM holds product data that follows ISO IDMP in the EU, and the QMS follows the ICH Q10 model. Fix a record in the system where it is born, not downstream. Ask: for the entity your project handles, which system holds the original?

    Good data has five properties: identity, unit, version, provenance, context

    • Stable ids, such as 6- or 10-character UniProt accessions
    • Units on every number; method version on every result
    • Who, when, which instrument: ALCOA++ and audit trails

    Source: UniProt Consortium, 2026; EMBL-EBI ChEMBL; European Medicines Agency (ICH), 2000; European Medicines Agency (GCP IWG), 2023; US Office of the Federal Register (eCFR)

    Speaker notes

    Context means the conditions a number holds under, such as assay conditions or species; FAIR asks for rich metadata. Part 11 requires secure, time-stamped audit trails. EMA's ALCOA++ lists ten attributes: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available and traceable. Ask: which of the five is weakest in the data you inherit?

    A unit slip makes a compound look 1,000 times more potent

    • 0.48 µM typed into an nM column reads 0.48 nM
    • pIC50 6.32 next to it shows the true 480 nM
    • Recompute pIC50 on load and reject mismatches

    Source: EMBL-EBI ChEMBL; US National Institute of Standards and Technology

    Speaker notes

    pChEMBL is minus log10 of the molar value, so 1 nM is 9. Micro is 10^-6 and nano 10^-9, a factor of 1,000. The table is illustrative, with internal ids. Row 1 hides a smaller slip: a pIC50 of 7.9 typed as an IC50 looks about 1.6 times more potent than the real 12.5 nM [D:sm-potency]. Ask: which column in your data could cross-check another?

    An out-of-specification result stays on record after a passing retest

    • Water 0.62% against not more than 0.5%: out of specification
    • The flag said N; recompute it from result and limit
    • A retest cannot replace the original without a lab error

    Source: US FDA, 2022

    Speaker notes

    In the messy table, a failing 93.8% assay run sits hidden behind a passing retest. The good table keeps run 1 with its investigation, and the retest on its own linked row [D:cmc-batch]. FDA guidance says the original result stands unless a laboratory error is documented, and all original data are kept. Ask: does your migration copy flags, or recompute them?

    Public sources describe the world; only the company holds its own records

    • Internal: registry, ELN, batch records, EDC, safety cases

    Learn

    How a medicine reaches a patient

    The FDA describes five steps: discovery and development, preclinical research, clinical research, FDA review, and post-market safety monitoring[1]. Discovery starts with thousands of candidate compounds, and only a few warrant further study[2]. Preclinical research tests toxicity before any human is dosed, under the Good Laboratory Practice rules in 21 CFR Part 58[3].

    In the US, an IND (investigational new drug application, the filing that lets a company start human trials) must be in place before clinical research begins[4]. In the EU, the sponsor (the company that runs the trial) submits a clinical trial application through CTIS, the system that implements the Clinical Trials Regulation (EU) No 536/2014[5]. The FDA gives typical sizes: Phase 1 enrols 20 to 100 people for several months, Phase 2 up to several hundred for several months to 2 years, and Phase 3 300 to 3,000 for 1 to 4 years[4]. About 70% of drugs move from Phase 1 to Phase 2, about 33% from Phase 2 on, and 25 to 30% from Phase 3 on[4].

    A second dataset gives sharper numbers. BIO tracked 9,704 programmes from 2011 to 2020: 52.0% passed Phase I, 28.9% Phase II, 57.8% Phase III and 90.6% of filings were approved[6]. Multiplied together, 7.9% of drugs entering Phase I reached approval, on average 10.5 years later[6]. Always name which of the two sources a plan uses, because their rates differ.

    The company then files an NDA or BLA (the marketing application for a drug or a biologic). FDA review takes 6 to 10 months once the file is accepted[7]. In the EU, the CHMP evaluates for up to 210 active days and the European Commission decides within 67 days of its opinion[8]. Both dossiers follow the five-module Common Technical Document, sent as eCTD[9, 10]. Commercial product is made under CGMP, set out in 21 CFR Parts 210, 211, 212 and 600[11]. After launch, pharmacovigilance (detecting and preventing side effects) continues; in the EU, suspected reactions go to EudraVigilance[12]. Real-world data from health records, claims and registries can then become real-world evidence[13].

    The class changes what a record must carry. A small molecule is defined by its structure, which an InChIKey (a 27-character hashed structure code) captures[23]. Semaglutide is a peptide, yet it is made by recombinant DNA technology in yeast followed by chemical modification, so its records look partly like a biologic’s[24]. Kymriah is made from each patient’s own T cells, so every lot belongs to one named patient and must stay linked to that patient[18].

    Twelve departments and where their data is born

    This course uses twelve departments: discovery, preclinical, clinical development, regulatory affairs, CMC (chemistry, manufacturing and controls), quality, supply chain, medical affairs, commercial, pharmacovigilance, real-world evidence, and IT and data. Each record is born in one system of record, and that system is the place to fix it.

    Laboratories write in an ELN (electronic lab notebook) and a LIMS (laboratory information management system), which tracks samples, workflows and assay data and links to the ELN[25]. Trials run on EDC (electronic data capture), a CTMS (clinical trial management system) and an eTMF (electronic trial master file)[26]. Plants run an MES (manufacturing execution system) at ISA-95 Level 3, under the ERP (enterprise resource planning) system at Level 4[27]. Regulatory affairs keeps product data in RIM (regulatory information management); in the EU that data follows the ISO IDMP standards[28]. Quality runs the QMS (quality management system) described by ICH Q10[29]. The safety database sends case reports to EudraVigilance in the ICH E2B(R3) format[30]. A CRM (customer relationship management) system logs contacts by commercial and medical-affairs staff; medical science liaisons do scientific exchange, not promotion[31], so the CRM design must be able to tell the two kinds of contact apart. The data platform holds real-world data, often in the OMOP common data model[32].

    What good data means, in one sentence each

    Identity: every compound, lot, patient and document has one stable identifier that is never reused; UniProt accessions, for example, are 6 or 10 characters and are kept stable so entries can be cited[33]. Unit: every number carries its unit; ChEMBL converts activities to nM before it computes its potency score[34]. Version: every result names the method version that produced it, because specifications are tied to analytical procedures[35]. Provenance: the record says who did what, when, with which instrument and protocol. EMA’s ALCOA++ lists ten attributes: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available and traceable[26]. Provenance rests mostly on attributable, contemporaneous and traceable; the MHRA’s ALCOA+ has the first nine[36]. Context: the record keeps the conditions under which the number holds, such as assay conditions, species or matrix; the FAIR principles ask for rich metadata so others can reuse data correctly[37].

    A Part 11 system must keep secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails that record who changed what and when[38]. That audit trail is provenance enforced by the system rather than by goodwill.

    How a right-looking number turns out wrong

    Potency is often reported as IC50, the concentration that halves a measured activity, such as an enzyme’s, in one assay, in nM. ChEMBL defines pChEMBL as minus log10 of the molar value, so 1 nM is 9[34]. Take 480 nM: 480 nM is 4.8 x 10^-7 M, and minus log10 of that is 6.32. Nano is 10^-9 and micro is 10^-6, so 0.48 µM is the same 480 nM[39]. Type 0.48 into the nM column and the compound looks 1,000 times more potent, with no error message. The pIC50 column next to it still reads 6.32, which is how a careful check finds the error[data].

    A quality example works the same way. A water result of 0.62% against a limit of not more than 0.5% is out of specification, even if a tick box says otherwise[data]. FDA guidance says the original out-of-specification result is kept, and a retest cannot replace it without a documented laboratory error[40].

    Internal versus external data

    Public sources describe the world. ChEMBL holds bioactivity records addressed by ChEMBL ids such as CHEMBL25, aspirin[41]. PubChem returns compounds by CID or InChIKey; aspirin is CID 2244[42]. UniProt holds annotated protein sequences[43]. The PDB uses 4-character structure ids[44]. The AlphaFold database serves predicted models by UniProt accession, with a per-residue confidence from 0 to 100[45, 46]. Open Targets scores target-disease links from 20 evidence sources (in its 2021 description)[47]. ClinicalTrials.gov numbers each study “NCT” plus 8 digits[48].

    Regulators publish too. EMA publishes an EPAR (European public assessment report) for every centrally authorised medicine[49]. DailyMed holds the FDA labeling that companies submit[50]. FAERS holds adverse event reports coded in MedDRA[51]. FAERS can point to a signal but cannot show causation or incidence[52]. EudraVigilance is the EU equivalent[12], and OMOP with OHDSI tools standardises observational data[32].

    Only the company holds its compound registry, ELN entries, executed batch records, EDC data and safety cases. The useful work is the join. An internal compound id maps to a structure, the structure to an InChIKey, and the InChIKey to PubChem and ChEMBL[42]. The first 14 characters of an InChIKey encode only the skeleton, so a match on that block alone merges stereoisomers[23].

    The delivery lens on day one

    Your first artefact is the system inventory. EU GMP Annex 11 asks for an up-to-date list of systems and their GMP functions[53]. For each system, decide and document which records are Part 11 records[54]. The URS (user requirements specification) then describes the required functions, based on a documented risk assessment and GMP impact[53]. ICH Q9(R1) defines risk as probability of harm combined with its severity, and scales effort to the risk[55]. GAMP 5 applies the same risk-based idea to computerised systems[56]. FDA’s final CSA guidance does too, though it is written for device production and quality software[57].

    Annex 11 also covers what a delivery plan touches most. Migration must check that data are not altered in value or meaning[53]. Changes are made only under a defined procedure, and validated systems get periodic review[53]. A dictionary is versioned like software. This module’s MedDRA reference is the version 29.0 guide[58]; version 29.1 came out on 1 September 2026 and became the global version on 2 November 2026[59]. Treat a dictionary or model update as a change request with an impact assessment. Annex 11 names process owner, system owner, Qualified Person and IT as the people who work together[53]. The sponsor keeps responsibility when a vendor does the work, needs written agreements before the vendor starts, and may rely on vendor validation only after assessing it[26]. Annex 11 likewise asks for formal agreements that state the supplier’s duties[53]. For trial systems, ICH E6(R3), in effect in the EU since 23 July 2025, keeps responsibility with the sponsor when work moves to a service provider, and bases the validation of computerised systems on a risk assessment of intended use and the importance of the data[60]. Annex 11 itself is being revised: the European Commission consulted on a revised draft, together with a new Annex 22 on AI, from 7 July to 7 October 2025[61]. This module cites the 2011 text, in operation since 30 June 2011[53].

    A careful team never overwrites an original result, never migrates without a count-and-sample reconciliation, never upgrades a dictionary silently, and never accepts a vendor pack unread[40, 53, 26].

    Example data

    Start with the potency table. In the good version, every row carries compound, batch, assay id, assay version and ATP concentration, and the pIC50 column agrees with the IC50 column. In the messy version, look for a pIC50 typed into the IC50 column, a unit typed in µM into an nM column, a missing assay version, a single-point % inhibition stored as an IC50, and a second replicate with no replicate id[data].

    Next, the batch release table, where the same five properties decide whether a lot can be released. Look at the unit and method-version columns, then compare each result with its specification limit yourself rather than trusting the flag[data]. In the good version the failing first run stays on record, and the retest is linked to its investigation[data].

    Last, the system inventory, the first table a delivery lead builds. Each row says where a record is born, whether it is GxP, whether it holds Part 11 records, who owns the process and the system, and which release was validated. In the messy version, look for a blank scope, a missing owner, a “Yes” with no release, a stale review, a vendor named as owner and a GMP table labelled analytics[data].

    Try it

    1. Your team validates an EDC build for a Phase I study. On BIO averages, how many years pass before an approval decision, and what share of that time is Phase II? What does that mean for the EDC?[6]
      Answer2.3 + 3.6 + 3.3 + 1.3 = 10.5 years from Phase I to approval, the report’s average[6]. Phase II is 3.6 / 10.5 = 0.34, about a third of the time. The EDC will see many releases over that decade, and each one goes through change control. Records from the Phase I build must stay readable: Annex 11 asks that archived data be checked for accessibility and readability, and that retrieval be tested after relevant system changes[53].
    2. In the messy potency table, CPD-0002 shows ic50_nm 0.48 and pic50 6.32. Use the pIC50 to find the true value, and write the acceptance check that catches the error automatically.[data]
      AnswerpIC50 = minus log10 of molar IC50, so a pIC50 of 6.32 means 10^-6.32 M, about 4.8 x 10^-7 M, which is 480 nM[34][data]. A real 0.48 nM would give pIC50 9.32, three units higher. The value was typed in µM: 0.48 µM x 1,000 = 480 nM[39]. Check: recompute pIC50 from ic50_nm on load and reject any row where the two differ by more than 0.05. Row 1 fails a related check: its pIC50 is blank and its IC50 of 7.9 matches the good table’s pIC50 for CPD-0001 (12.5 nM gives 7.90), so a pIC50 was typed into the IC50 column. Read as 7.9 nM it looks about 1.6 times more potent than the real 12.5 nM (12.5 / 7.9 = 1.58)[data]. The pIC50-to-IC50 check cannot catch this row because its pIC50 is blank, so a second check rejects any IC50 row with a blank pIC50.
    3. CPD-0005 appears twice in the messy potency table, at 55 and 61 nM, and the second row has no replicate id. What single value should be reported, and what happens if the table is joined one-to-one to a DMPK table on compound_id?[data]
      AnswerThe dataset’s fix note asks for the geometric mean of IC50, which equals averaging pIC50[data]. Square root of 55 x 61 = square root of 3,355 = 57.9 nM. Averaging the listed pIC50s gives (7.26 + 7.21) / 2 = 7.235, which is 10^-7.235 M = 58.2 nM; the small gap comes from rounding the pIC50s to two decimals. The messy table has 6 rows for 5 compounds. In a one-to-one join on compound_id, the single DMPK row for CPD-0005 matches both potency rows, so its DMPK values appear twice and count double in any average. The good table labels the two runs r1 and r2, so a reader can tell replicates from an accidental duplicate[data]. The acceptance check: one row per compound, batch, assay and replicate id, no blank replicate id, and a summary row with n before any one-to-one join.
    4. In the messy batch table, which rows are out of specification, by how much, and what must the record keep?[data]
      AnswerRow 4: water 0.62% w/w against “not more than 0.5” is 0.62 minus 0.5 = 0.12 percentage points over, or 0.12 / 0.5 = 24% above the limit, but oos_flag says N[data]. Row 5: the first assay run was 93.8% against 95.0 to 105.0, which is 95.0 minus 93.8 = 1.2 points below, hidden behind a passing retest of 101.0[data]. Both are out of specification. The original result stays on record, and a retest cannot replace it without a documented laboratory error[40]. The good table shows the target state: run 1 stays at 93.8 with flag Y and investigation INV-2024-0012, and the 101.0 retest sits on its own row linked to the same investigation, 6 rows in all[data]. A migration or ingest check recomputes the flag from result and limit, rather than copying it.
    5. Go through the messy system inventory row by row. Name each defect, the role that fixes it, and count how many of the six systems hold Part 11 records in the good version.[data]
      AnswerRow 1: blank GxP and Part 11 scope; the process owner and QA decide and document it[54]. Row 2: no process owner; the business names one[53]. Row 3: “Yes” instead of a validated release; the system owner records R2025.2 and its report. Row 4: last review 2021-02 against 2025 or 2026 for the other GxP systems; QA schedules a periodic review[53]. Row 5: the vendor is named as system owner. Name an internal system owner; the sponsor keeps responsibility and puts the vendor’s duties in a written agreement[26, 53]. Row 6: a table relied on for batch-release trending is labelled analytics; it is in scope and needs a risk assessment and validation[54, 55]. In the good table, 5 of 6 systems hold Part 11 records; only the research ELN does not[data].

    Check yourself

    1. Which US filing must be in place before a company starts clinical research? a) NDA b) IND c) EPAR d) BLA
    2. An nM column shows 0.48 next to a pIC50 of 6.32. What is the correct IC50? a) 0.48 nM b) 4.8 nM c) 48 nM d) 480 nM
    3. Which of these exists only inside the company? a) the executed batch record b) ChEMBL c) DailyMed d) EudraVigilance
    4. Under EU GMP Annex 11, what should user requirements be based on? a) the vendor’s feature list b) the project budget c) a documented risk assessment and GMP impact d) the test scripts
