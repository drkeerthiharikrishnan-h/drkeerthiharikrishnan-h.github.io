Biopharma: how the industry works

How a medicine goes from an idea in a lab to a patient, and the systems, rules and data behind each step.

The pipeline at a glance

Explore by department

The value chain runs left to right, then the functions that support it. Pick a department.

A new medicine passes through five steps: discovery, preclinical research, clinical research, regulatory review, and safety monitoring after approval[1]. This page walks through the departments you will meet in a biopharma company. It then covers the systems, standards and ontologies that connect them. Each fact links to a public source, listed at the end.

Discovery

What it does

Researchers look for a compound that could treat a disease. Ideas come from new insight into a disease, large-scale testing of compounds, unexpected effects of existing treatments, and new technologies[2]. Thousands of compounds may be candidates early on. Only a few look promising enough to study further[2].

For a promising compound, teams study how the body absorbs, distributes, breaks down and excretes it[2]. They also study how it works, the best dose and route, its side effects, and how it interacts with other drugs[2].

Main systems

Lab data sits in a laboratory information management system (LIMS). A LIMS tracks samples, workflows and assay data, and often links to an electronic lab notebook (ELN)[3]. Scientists record methods, results and summaries in the ELN[4]. A new compound or biomolecule is drawn, validated and given a unique identifier in a registration system before it goes for testing[5].

Standards and ontologies

Scientists rely on shared public vocabularies. UniProt covers proteins[6] and ChEBI covers chemical entities[7]. The Gene Ontology describes what genes do[8]. EFO is used to annotate data in resources such as the GWAS Catalog[9], and MeSH is used to search the literature[10].

Preclinical

What it does

Before anyone takes a new drug, researchers check whether it could cause serious harm (toxicity). They test in the lab (in vitro) and in animals (in vivo)[11]. The studies are usually small, but they must give detailed information on dose and toxicity. The team then decides whether the drug should be tested in people[11].

Key documents

Written protocols, standard operating procedures and study reports, all required by Good Laboratory Practice (GLP)[11].

Main systems

A LIMS holds samples, assay results and study data[3].

Standards and ontologies

GLP is set out in US law at 21 CFR Part 58[11]. Nonclinical data for submissions use the CDISC SEND standard[12].

Who you would meet

Study staff, and a quality assurance function that oversees each study, as GLP requires[11].

Clinical development

What it does

Trials test the drug in people, in phases[13]. Before the first trial, the sponsor files an Investigational New Drug (IND) application with the FDA[13].

The shares in the table are the FDA’s rounded figures[13]. An industry analysis of 9,704 programmes from 2011 to 2020 found lower pass rates for the first two phases: 52.0% from Phase I, 28.9% from Phase II, 57.8% from Phase III, and 90.6% from filing to approval, with 7.9% of drugs entering Phase I reaching approval[14]. The funnel on this page uses the 2011-2020 figures; the two sources differ in period, sample and method, so quote the source with the number.

Trials follow Good Clinical Practice (ICH E6), the international standard for designing, running, recording and reporting trials so participants are protected and the data can be trusted[15]. ICH E9 sets the statistical principles[16].

Key documents

  • The clinical study report (CSR) for each trial, in the structure set by ICH E3[17].
  • The trial master file (TMF). It must support document identification, version history, search and retrieval[18]. It must also be directly available to inspectors[18].

Main systems

EMA’s guideline on computerised systems in trials names the main ones[19]. They are electronic data collection (EDC) with electronic case report forms, electronic clinical outcome assessment (eCOA), and interactive response technology (IRT). It also names clinical trial management systems (CTMS) and the electronic TMF (eTMF)[19].

Standards and ontologies

  • CDISC CDASH for collecting data, SDTM for organising it, ADaM for analysis datasets[20, 21, 22].
  • MedDRA to code adverse events[23] and WHODrug to code medicines[24].
  • ALCOA++: data must be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, available and traceable[19].

Who you would meet

The sponsor, meaning the company that runs the trial and files the IND[13].

Regulatory affairs

What it does

Regulatory affairs gets a medicine approved and keeps its approval current. In the US, the company files a New Drug Application (NDA). It holds all preclinical and clinical data, proposed labeling, safety updates, patent information and more[25]. Once the FDA accepts the file as complete, its review team has 6 to 10 months to decide[25].

The FDA has four programmes that speed things up for serious conditions: Fast Track, Breakthrough Therapy, Accelerated Approval and Priority Review[26]. Accelerated Approval can rest on a surrogate endpoint, a measure expected to predict real benefit. Priority Review sets a goal of action within 6 months[26].

After approval, significant changes such as a new formulation or dosage strength generally need FDA approval first[27].

Key documents

  • The Common Technical Document (CTD), in five modules. Module 1 is specific to each region; Modules 2 to 5 (summaries, quality, nonclinical and clinical reports) are the same everywhere[28].
  • The label (prescribing information), which describes the basis for approval and how best to use the drug[25].

Main systems

Submissions go to the FDA in electronic CTD (eCTD) format. It is required for NDAs, for Biologics License Applications (BLAs) and for INDs for products meant for sale[29]. Product data follow the ISO IDMP (Identification of Medicinal Products) standards[30]. EMA runs them through SPOR, its master-data services for substances, products, organisations and reference lists[30].

Standards and ontologies

ICH M4 (CTD)[28], eCTD[29], IDMP[30], and CDISC SDTM, which the FDA and Japan’s PMDA require for study data[21].

Who you would meet

On the FDA side, a review team: medical officers and statisticians review clinical data, pharmacologists review animal data, and a project manager compiles the reviews[25].

CMC and manufacturing

What it does

CMC (chemistry, manufacturing and controls) makes sure the same medicine is made, at the same quality, every time. Current Good Manufacturing Practice (CGMP) sets the minimum requirements for methods, facilities and controls, so each product is safe and has the strength it claims[31]. ICH Q7 sets GMP for the active ingredient[32]. The FDA inspects manufacturing sites, including overseas, and can shut down a site that fails minimum standards[27].

Key documents

Records that support batch release. EU rules require that a printout can show whether any of that data changed after it was first entered[33].

Main systems

In the ISA-95 model, manufacturing execution systems (MES) run plant operations at Level 3, and enterprise resource planning (ERP) handles business planning and logistics at Level 4[34]. ISA-88 gives the terms and models for batch control[35].

Standards and ontologies

US CGMP rules in 21 CFR Parts 210, 211, 212 and 600[31]; ICH Q7[32] and Q9 (quality risk management)[36]; ISA-88 and ISA-95[35, 34].

Quality and GxP

What it does

Quality keeps the whole system under control. ICH Q10 describes a pharmaceutical quality system that runs from development to the end of a product’s life[37]. ICH Q9 sets out how to find, judge and control risks to quality[36]. Data integrity is part of CGMP[38].

GxP is shorthand for the good-practice rules on this page: GLP, GCP and GMP, plus good pharmacovigilance practice (GVP) for safety work[39]. In the EU, any computer system used in GMP work must be validated, and its IT infrastructure qualified[33].

Key documents

Validation records for each computerised system. GAMP 5 gives a risk-based method to show a system is fit for its intended use[40]. Audit trails record every relevant change or deletion with a reason, and must be reviewed regularly[33].

Standards and ontologies

  • 21 CFR Part 11 for electronic records and signatures in the US[41]; EU GMP Annex 11 for computerised systems[33].
  • ALCOA+: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available[42].
  • Computer software assurance, the FDA’s risk-based approach to software in production and quality systems, written for medical devices[43].

Supply chain

What it does

In the US, the Drug Supply Chain Security Act sets out an electronic, interoperable system to identify and trace certain prescription drugs at package level[44]. In the EU, outer packs must carry a unique identifier and an anti-tampering device. Directive 2011/62/EU applies since 2 January 2013[45]. The detailed safety-feature rules apply since 9 February 2019[45].

Main systems

ERP covers business planning and logistics (ISA-95 Level 4)[34].

Standards and ontologies

DSCSA[44], the EU Falsified Medicines Directive[45], ISA-95[34].

Medical affairs

What it does

Medical affairs gives doctors and other healthcare professionals accurate, unbiased scientific and medical information[46].

Who you would meet

Medical science liaisons (MSLs): field scientists who exchange scientific data with healthcare professionals. They must not take part in promotional messaging[46].

Commercial and market access

What it does

In the US, the FDA regulates prescription drug advertising: unapproved uses cannot be advertised, and ads must be truthful[27]. Promotional and medical materials go through medical, legal and regulatory (MLR) review, which Veeva describes as checking them against approved labels[47]. In Europe, the EFPIA Code, a self-regulatory industry code, requires a medical doctor or, where appropriate, a pharmacist to approve promotional material and certify that it is consistent with the summary of product characteristics[48].

Market access deals with health technology assessment. Since 12 January 2025, new cancer medicines and advanced therapy medicinal products in the EU are subject to a joint clinical assessment[49]. It analyses the clinical evidence on how a medicine’s effects compare with other options[49].

Generic copies follow when protection ends. They must match the brand in key characteristics[27]. They usually rely on bioequivalence studies (tests that the copy behaves the same way in the body) and an Abbreviated New Drug Application, not new clinical trials[27].

Standards and ontologies

The WHO ATC classification groups drugs by the organ or system they act on and by their therapeutic, pharmacological and chemical properties[50].

Pharmacovigilance

What it does

Pharmacovigilance detects, assesses and helps prevent side effects and other problems with medicines[51]. A drug’s safety picture keeps building for months and years after approval[27]. In the EU, reports of suspected side effects go to EudraVigilance[51]. In the US, the FDA reviews problem reports and collects them through MedWatch[27]. It describes Sentinel as a national system that uses large electronic health databases to spot safety issues faster[27].

Key documents

The individual case safety report (ICSR): one report per patient case of suspected side effects. In the EU, ICSRs must use the ISO format based on ICH E2B(R3) since 30 June 2022[52].

Main systems

On the regulator side, EudraVigilance is the EU’s single repository of suspected adverse reactions[51].

Standards and ontologies

ICH E2B(R3)[52], MedDRA[23], WHODrug[24].

Real-world evidence

What it does

Real-world data (RWD) is health data collected routinely from many sources: electronic health records, insurance claims, registries and digital health devices. Real-world evidence (RWE) is clinical evidence about a medicine’s use, benefits or risks that comes from analysing RWD[53].

Standards and ontologies

  • HL7 FHIR for exchanging health records data[54].
  • SNOMED CT for clinical terms[55], LOINC for lab tests[56], RxNorm for drug names[57], ATC for drug classes[50].

IT and data

What it does

Part 11 applies to electronic records created, changed, kept, archived, retrieved or sent under FDA rules[41]. In the EU, Annex 11 applies to every computerised system used in GMP work[33]. GAMP 5 is the industry’s risk-based guide to compliant computerised systems[40].

Standards and ontologies

Industry groups work on shared data standards. The Pistoia Alliance brings together more than 200 organisations across biopharma[58]. The Allotrope Foundation builds community standards to link and standardise lab data[59].
