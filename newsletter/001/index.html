Keerthi's Notes

Issue 01 · 25 October – 1 November 2026 · 2 min read
Keerthi Harikrishnan · developmental and cancer biologist, building AI for science

The thread through this week’s reading: the useful work checked its outputs before trusting them. Four papers, three news pieces, the week’s deals, and one event worth applying for.

Papers

bioRxivPreprint
bioRxiv · Preprint

An agent-built, verified atlas of public spatial transcriptomics

Language-model agents indexed all 298,179 series in the Gene Expression Omnibus, found 2,411 with spatial data and assembled them into one atlas. Agents did the work; checks decided what was kept. Models trained across many studies transferred better than models trained on one.

bioRxivPreprint
bioRxiv · Preprint

Antibody design is limited by its labels, not its models

Tools that steer antibodies away from immune reactions learn from one number per molecule: the share of trial patients who made anti-drug antibodies. The authors corrected and extended the standard dataset from 217 to 231 entries and show how thin that signal is.

bioRxivPreprint
bioRxiv · Preprint

EGFR signalling routes traced through proteins nobody measured

Phosphoproteomics misses proteins that do not change or are not detected. This method uses the order of events and known interaction networks to draw routes through those gaps, in EGF-stimulated MDA-MB-468, HEK293T and HeLa cells. EGFR was the receptor at the centre of my fibulin-1 work at IISER Pune.

Nature MethodsPaper
Nature Methods · Paper · image: Nature Methods

RNA editing without touching the genome

A new system recruits the cell’s own ADAR1 p150 enzyme at multiple sites, through its Zα domain, to correct disease-linked mutations in RNA: up to 100% editing in vitro and 84% in vivo. A change made to RNA leaves the genome as it was.

News and blogs

NatureNews
Nature · News · image: Nature

A publisher retracts about 1,400 conference papers in one day

A leading professional society for scientists and engineers pulled the papers in a single batch. Volume is not a quality check.

TechCrunchNews
TechCrunch · News · image: TechCrunch

AI has not yet shown a profit gain outside tech

An Apollo economist found no clear bottom-line benefit from AI for large companies outside the tech industry so far. Agree what counts as success before a pilot starts.

Simon WillisonBlog
Simon Willison · Blog

Trust in software, after LLMs

Kyle Kingsbury, quoted here: “the use of LLMs erodes trust in documentation, in software…”. Tests, provenance and honest documentation are how a project earns that trust back.
