Issue 02 · 2 – 8 November 2026 · 3 min read
Keerthi Harikrishnan · developmental and cancer biologist, building AI for science

The thread through this week’s reading: large datasets, and the risks that show only in them. Four papers, three news pieces, the week’s deals, and one health hackathon (4–11 November).

Papers

bioRxivPreprint
bioRxiv · Preprint

Lung fibrosis, mapped in three dimensions

The authors combined 3D X-ray histology with spatial omics to build a volumetric atlas of human lung fibrosis. Collapse of the alveolar-capillary unit is associated with early fibrosis: type 1 alveolar epithelial cells and capillary endothelial cells are lost, and the basement membrane breaks down. Stromal activation converges on fibrogenic cores, where collagen maturation is highest. Signatures of these tissue states were associated with lung function and mortality in independent patient cohorts. The extracellular matrix was the subject of my fibulin-1 work at MUSC and IISER Pune.

arXivPreprint
arXiv · Preprint

Routine pathology slides carry signals of sex and age group

The study used 1.3 million H&E whole-slide images from 257,322 patients across 25 organs. With pathology foundation-model features, sex, age group and self-reported race could be recovered from the slides across organs: mean AUROC 0.828 for sex and 0.733 for age group. Any model built on these slides could pick up the same signals; worth checking for.

bioRxivPreprint
bioRxiv · Preprint

Protein heat stability, predicted with a range instead of a single number

StableProt predicts a protein’s melting temperature and its source organism’s optimal growth temperature, and gives calibrated 95% intervals. Public databases are more than 87% mesophiles, so the authors subsampled those to give heat-loving organisms more weight. It recovered 92.0% of hyperthermophiles (80 °C and above).

Nature Methods
Nature Methods · image: Nature Methods

DIRAC: one framework for spatial multi-omic data

DIRAC does two linked jobs: it joins the omics layers within one tissue sample, and it carries annotations from a reference dataset to new ones. The aim is interpretable tissue maps across multi-omic assays, platforms and biological systems.

News and blogs

GENNews
GEN · News · image: GEN

Novartis confirms nine tumour cases after Zolgensma

Nine children treated with Zolgensma, the AAV9 gene therapy for spinal muscular atrophy, developed tumours; seven of the cases are newly disclosed. In two, there was evidence that the vector had integrated into the patient’s DNA. More than 6,000 patients have received the therapy. Some risks show only after thousands of patients have been treated.

NatureNews
Nature · News · image: Nature

A preprint platform pays its reviewers

XSci, launched in September, pays a reviewer US$200 for a report within five days and $100 within ten. Authors pay $399 per round of review, or $249 if the report is made public. Speed is paid for; whether quality follows is still open.
